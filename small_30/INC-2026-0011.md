## Symptom
The incident began with reports of pods stuck in CrashLoopBackOff after a node reboot. Specifically, a deployment would enter CrashLoopBackOff within 30 seconds of pod start, with pod logs showing a single line followed by the container exiting with code 137. This pattern suggested an issue with the container's startup process or its interaction with the Kubernetes environment.

Further investigation revealed that the affected pods were part of a critical service, and their failure to start was impacting overall system availability. The symptom was consistent across multiple attempts to restart the pods, indicating a persistent issue rather than a transient error.

## Escalation timeline
* 13:08 — L1 Platform support: Sara - She immediately began reviewing the pod logs and Kubernetes event history to identify any patterns or clues.
* 13:31 — L2 Platform engineer: Nikolai - He joined the investigation, focusing on potential configuration issues or recent changes that might have triggered the problem.

## Diagnosis steps and hypotheses
Initial hypotheses centered around issues with the deployment configuration or problems with the node's Kubernetes setup. To investigate etcd cluster health, the command ```etcdctl check datadir --cluster``` was used, which helped rule out certain hypotheses related to etcd integrity. However, the root cause remained elusive until a deeper review of the pod's liveness probe settings was conducted.

## Resolution steps
1. Set initialDelaySeconds to 60s on liveness probe
2. Add startupProbe with failureThreshold 30
3. Update deployment config with new probe settings
4. Apply updated config using helm upgrade

## Post-mortem note
Liveness probe killed container prematurely due to short interval. This incident highlights the importance of carefully configuring liveness and startup probes to ensure they do not interfere with the normal operation of containers, especially in critical services.
