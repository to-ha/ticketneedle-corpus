## Symptom

The incident began with reports of service discovery returning stale endpoints after a deploy, which led to failed connections and timeouts for dependent services. Initially, the issue seemed intermittent, but as time progressed, it became more consistent, indicating a growing problem. The monitoring system caught the increase in error rates and alerted the on-call team. However, the specific cause of the stale endpoints was not immediately clear, requiring further investigation into the CI runner pool and its interaction with service discovery.

As the issue evolved, it became apparent that the problem was not just about the endpoints being stale but also about the inability of the system to update them correctly after a deploy. This hinted at a deeper issue within the CI runner pool's configuration or its garbage collection process. The user impact was significant, with several critical services being unavailable due to the failure in service discovery. The business impact included delays in deployment and potential revenue loss due to the unavailability of key services.

The monitoring system played a crucial role in identifying the issue, as it provided real-time data on the error rates and service availability. However, it did not directly point to the root cause, which required manual investigation and diagnosis. The initial hypothesis was that there might be an issue with the deploy process itself or with the service discovery mechanism. But as the investigation progressed, focus shifted towards the CI runner pool due to its critical role in the deployment pipeline.

Further analysis of the logs and metrics revealed patterns suggesting that the CI runner pool was under significant stress, possibly due to inefficient resource utilization or lack of proper cleanup after previous runs. This led to a deeper dive into the runner's configuration and its garbage collection settings. It was observed that the runners were not being restarted frequently enough, and there was no regular cleanup of unused resources, which could lead to exhaustion of available capacity over time.

The symptom complex revolved around the service discovery issue, but it was clear that this was a manifestation of a more underlying problem related to resource management within the CI runner pool. Understanding this connection was key to resolving the incident and preventing future occurrences.

## Diagnosis steps and hypotheses

Initially, the team suspected a problem with the deploy process, thinking that perhaps the new version of the service was not correctly registered with the service discovery mechanism. To diagnose this, they reviewed the deploy logs and checked the service registration process. However, this did not yield any conclusive evidence pointing to the deploy process as the culprit.

Next, they considered the possibility of a network issue preventing the services from correctly registering their endpoints. This led to a series of network tests, including `tcpdump` and `dig`, to verify DNS resolution and network connectivity. These tests showed no issues with network connectivity or DNS resolution, ruling out the network as the cause.

Another hypothesis was that the service discovery itself was faulty, perhaps due to a software bug or incorrect configuration. The team reviewed the service discovery logs and configuration files but found no evidence supporting this hypothesis.

To understand the CI runner pool's behavior better, the team executed the following command to review the flag history:
```
flag-history --runner=17 --limit=100
```
This provided insights into how the runners were being utilized and configured over time, helping to identify any patterns or changes that could be contributing to the issue.

Further investigation led to considering the hypothesis that the CI runner pool was exhausting its capacity due to inefficient garbage collection. This would mean that over time, the pool was accumulating unused resources (like stale endpoints) without properly cleaning them up, leading to the observed symptoms. To explore this, the team looked into the garbage collection settings and the history of runner restarts.

After ruling out several hypotheses and gathering more evidence, it became clear that the root cause was related to the CI runner pool's capacity management and garbage collection. The actual root cause emerged as the team dug deeper into the runner's configuration and its interaction with service discovery, revealing a lack of proper cleanup and resource management.

## Resolution steps

1. Restart runner 17 with flag --max-retries=5
2. Set registry_gc_interval to 30m in /etc/runner/config.toml
3. Run registry-gc --dry-run to identify unused images
4. Remove unused images older than 14d with registry-gc --delete
5. Update CI config with new runner-image version 1.2.3

## Post-mortem note

The CI runner pool exhausted its capacity due to a lack of garbage collection. This root cause was initially missed because the focus was on the symptoms related to service discovery rather than the underlying resource management issue within the CI runner pool. In hindsight, earlier investigation into the runner's configuration and garbage collection settings could have led to a quicker resolution. Follow-up actions include implementing more frequent restarts of the runners, adjusting the garbage collection interval, and monitoring the CI runner pool's capacity more closely to prevent similar incidents in the future. Additionally, there will be a review of the monitoring system to improve its ability to detect such issues earlier, potentially through custom alerts based on CI runner pool utilization and garbage collection metrics.
