## Symptom
The primary SQL replica experienced a connection pool saturation issue, causing application threads to become blocked. This resulted in periodic spikes of dropped connections, with the pattern repeating every 30-45 minutes and clearing within a minute. The application connection pool would report these spikes, indicating that the database was unable to handle the incoming connections. As a result, users would experience delays and errors when attempting to access the application.

Further investigation revealed that the connection pool saturation was causing the application threads to wait for available connections, leading to increased latency and decreased performance. The pg_stat_activity view showed a high number of idle connections, indicating that the connection pool was not being utilized efficiently. The pgbouncer logs also revealed that the connection pool was being exhausted, with many connections being closed due to inactivity.

The symptom was first observed by the monitoring team, who noticed the periodic spikes in dropped connections. The team then alerted the database operators, who began investigating the issue. The operators checked the database logs and found that the connection pool was being saturated, causing the application threads to become blocked.

The connection pool saturation issue had a significant impact on the application's performance, causing delays and errors for users. The issue also affected the database's overall health, as the high number of idle connections was causing unnecessary resource utilization. To resolve the issue, it was essential to identify the root cause and implement a solution to prevent future occurrences.

## Diagnosis steps and hypotheses
The team considered several hypotheses to explain the connection pool saturation issue. One hypothesis was that the database was experiencing a high volume of incoming connections, causing the connection pool to become exhausted. However, after analyzing the database logs, it was determined that the incoming connection rate was within normal limits.

Another hypothesis was that the connection pool was not being configured correctly, leading to inefficient utilization of available connections. To investigate this hypothesis, the team used the `pidstat` command to monitor the database's CPU usage and identify any bottlenecks. The command used was:
```
pidstat -p $(pgrep postgres) 1 10
```
The output of the command showed that the database was not experiencing any CPU bottlenecks, ruling out this hypothesis.

The team also considered the possibility that the application was not releasing connections back to the pool, causing the connection pool to become saturated. However, after reviewing the application code, it was determined that the connections were being released correctly.

## Resolution steps
1. Set max_client_conn to 200 in pg_hba.conf
2. Add pgbouncer in front of batch job on port 5433
3. Configure pgbouncer to use pool_mode and pool_size 100
4. Increase per-IP connection limit from 50 to 200 in postgresql.conf

## Post-mortem note
Background job opened many short-lived connections that exhausted the per-IP connection limit of 50. The resolution steps implemented helped to prevent future occurrences of the issue by increasing the connection limit and optimizing the connection pool configuration. The team also implemented additional monitoring to detect similar issues in the future, ensuring that the database remains stable and performant.
