# Major Incident — INC-2026-0013
* **Ticket ID:** INC-2026-0013
* **Reported:** 2026-05-05 12:14 UTC
* **Priority:** P2
* **Domain:** Database
* **Status:** Resolved

## Symptom
The incident began with a spike in deadlocks during a release window, which was observed by the monitoring team. This symptom was characterized by a sudden increase in deadlock errors in the database logs, indicating that multiple transactions were competing for the same resources. As a result, the database performance deteriorated, leading to slower query execution times and increased latency. The monitoring team noticed that the deadlocks were occurring primarily on the read-replica pair, which suggested that the issue was related to the replication process.

Further investigation revealed that the deadlocks were causing a significant impact on the application's performance, resulting in errors and timeouts for users. The development team reported that the application was experiencing a high rate of failed transactions, which were being retried multiple times. This led to an increase in the overall load on the database, exacerbating the problem.

The symptom complex was quite specific, with the deadlocks occurring only during the release window and primarily affecting the read-replica pair. This suggested that the issue was related to the increased load on the database during this time period, rather than a more general problem with the database configuration.

The database team worked closely with the development team to understand the root cause of the issue and to identify potential solutions. They analyzed the database logs and metrics, looking for any clues that could help explain the sudden increase in deadlocks.

## Diagnosis steps and hypotheses
The diagnosis process involved several hypotheses, each of which was tested and ruled out based on the evidence. One hypothesis was that the issue was related to disk I/O bottlenecks, which could be causing the deadlocks. To test this hypothesis, the team used the following command:
```
iostat -x 10 /dev/sda
```
However, the results showed that the disk I/O was within normal limits, which ruled out this hypothesis.

Another hypothesis was that the issue was related to the database configuration, specifically the settings for the read-replica pair. The team analyzed the database configuration files and logs but found no evidence to support this hypothesis.

The team also considered the possibility that the issue was related to the application's behavior during the release window. They worked with the development team to understand the application's workflow and identified some potential areas for optimization.

## Resolution steps
1. Restart read-replica with max_connections set to 500
2. Set replica lag threshold to 30 seconds in pgbouncer.ini
3. Adjust iostat interval to 10 seconds for closer monitoring
4. Run explain analyze on slow queries to identify bottlenecks

## Post-mortem note
The read-replica pair fell behind due to excessive connection overload. This issue was resolved by adjusting the database configuration and optimizing the application's behavior during the release window. The team learned the importance of closely monitoring the database performance during peak periods and taking proactive measures to prevent similar issues in the future.
