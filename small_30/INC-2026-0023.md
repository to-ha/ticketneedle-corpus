As the incident evolved, the frequency and duration of these alerts increased, causing concern among the team. The user impact was significant, as the tracing collector is a critical component of the monitoring system, and its instability affected the team's ability to troubleshoot issues. The monitoring system caught the initial alerts, but the flapping pattern made it difficult to determine the root cause of the issue. Further investigation revealed that the tracing collector was experiencing issues with its database connection, leading to intermittent failures.

The symptoms were first observed at 06:15 UTC, and by 06:20 UTC, the alerts had already started to flap. The team tried to use tsdb-cli to investigate the issue, but the results were inconclusive.

The tracing collector's instability had a significant impact on the team's ability to troubleshoot issues. ## Diagnosis steps and hypotheses
The team initially suspected that the issue might be related to the tracing collector's configuration, so they checked the configuration files for any recent changes. However, this hypothesis was ruled out after reviewing the configuration history. The team then suspected that the issue might be related to the database connection, so they used tsdb-cli to investigate the database indexes.
```
tsdb-cli --check-indexes /var/lib/tsdb
```
The results showed that the database indexes were corrupted, which led the team to suspect that the issue might be related to the database itself. However, further investigation revealed that the database was functioning correctly, and the issue was specific to the tracing collector.

The team then suspected that the issue might be related to the tracing collector's performance metrics, such as latency and error rates. They used alert-history and trace-query tools to investigate these metrics, but the results were inconclusive. The team also suspected that the issue might be related to the tracing collector's ability to process incoming traces, so they checked the tracing collector's logs for any errors. However, this hypothesis was also ruled out after reviewing the logs.

As the team continued to investigate, they discovered that the tracing collector service had failed to start due to a corrupted database index. This discovery led the team to focus on resolving the database index issue and restarting the tracing collector service.

## Resolution steps
1. Restart tracing collector service on node 10.0.2.15
2. Set tsdb.max_idle_connections to 50 in /etc/tsdb.conf
3. Increase trace-buffer-size to 1048576 in /etc/trace-config.yaml
4. Run tsdb-cli with --repair option to fix corrupted indexes
