## Symptom
The database team noticed a growing replication lag after the midnight cron job. This lag was observed in the replication metrics and was causing delays in data consistency across the database cluster. The team initially thought it might be related to a recent change in the database configuration, but upon further investigation, they realized that the issue was more complex.

The symptom was first identified by monitoring alerts, which notified the team of an increasing lag in replication. Further analysis using `pg_stat_activity` views and `pidstat` revealed that the replication process was not keeping up with the workload. The team tried to investigate the cause of the lag but needed more expertise to resolve the issue.

## Diagnosis steps and hypotheses
The team considered several hypotheses, including issues with the database configuration, network connectivity problems, and hardware failures. However, after running diagnostic commands such as 
```
psql -c "SELECT * FROM pg_stat_replication;"
```
they were able to rule out some of these hypotheses and focus on the replication slot as the likely cause of the issue.

## Resolution steps
1. Stop logical replication
2. Set wal_sender_timeout to 60s
3. Restart slot with slot_name = my_slot
4. Clear backlog with pg_rewind -s my_slot

## Post-mortem note
Logical replication slot my_slot was not properly restarted after a timeout. This issue highlights the importance of monitoring replication slots and having procedures in place to handle such timeouts to prevent data inconsistencies and delays.
