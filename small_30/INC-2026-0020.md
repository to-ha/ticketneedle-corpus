## Symptom
The incident began with reports of ingress returning 502 errors for a single backend service. This symptom was consistent across multiple attempts to access the service, suggesting a problem with the service's availability or configuration. Upon further investigation, it became clear that the issue was not with the service itself but rather with the scaling of the cluster.

As the day progressed, the error rate increased, indicating that the problem was worsening over time. Monitoring tools showed that the number of available pods for the backend service was lower than expected, which aligns with the observed 502 errors due to insufficient capacity to handle incoming requests.

Initial observations pointed towards a potential issue with the cluster's ability to scale up or down in response to demand, specifically focusing on the autoscaling component.

## Diagnosis steps and hypotheses
Initial hypotheses focused on potential misconfigurations of the cluster autoscaler or recent changes in workload patterns. The team attempted to verify the current state of the cluster and its components using tools like `k9s` for cluster overview and `stern` for log insights. A key step in diagnosis involved checking the logs of the cluster autoscaler directly with 
```
kubectl logs -f cluster-autoscaler -n kube-system
```
to identify any errors or warnings that could indicate why the scaling was not occurring as expected. This step provided crucial insight into the autoscaler's decision-making process and helped narrow down the potential causes.

## Resolution steps
1. Set cluster-autoscaler.log-level to 4
2. Restart cluster-autoscaler with --v=4 flag
3. Edit /etc/cluster-autoscaler/config.yaml scale-down-unneeded-time to 10m

## Post-mortem note
The cluster autoscaler was not scaling down due to incorrect log level and config. This issue highlights the importance of regularly reviewing configuration settings, especially after updates or changes, to ensure they align with the current operational needs of the cluster. Regular audits and monitoring can help catch such misconfigurations before they lead to significant incidents.
