## Symptom
The incident began with reports of ingress returning 502 errors for a single backend service. This symptom was first observed by monitoring tools, which alerted the on-call team to a potential issue. As the team investigated, they found that the error was consistent across multiple ingress controllers, suggesting a problem with the underlying Kubernetes infrastructure. Further investigation revealed that the affected pods were running on node 3, which had been experiencing high resource utilization in the hours leading up to the incident.

The 502 errors were not accompanied by any other obvious symptoms, such as increased latency or packet loss. However, the team did notice that the node's CPU and memory usage had been trending upward over the past few days, potentially indicating a resource constraint. To better understand the issue, the team used stern to inspect the node's logs and helm to review the deployment configuration. They also used crictl to verify the pod counts and check for any obvious issues with the container runtime.

As the incident persisted, the team began to suspect that the problem might be related to the node's configuration or resource allocation. They used various tools, including kubectl and tcpdump, to gather more information about the node's behavior and the traffic flowing through it. Despite these efforts, the root cause of the issue remained unclear, and the team was forced to escalate the incident to higher-level support.

The escalation was necessary because the team had tried several potential fixes, including adjusting the node's resource allocation and restarting the affected pods. However, none of these measures had resolved the issue, and the 502 errors continued to occur. The team was concerned that the problem might be more complex than initially thought and that a more detailed analysis would be required to identify the root cause.

## Diagnosis steps and hypotheses
The team considered several potential causes for the issue, including a misconfigured node, a resource constraint, or a problem with the container runtime. They used various tools, including crictl and stern, to gather more information about the node's behavior and the traffic flowing through it. One potential hypothesis was that the node's maxPods value was set too low, causing the kubelet to reject new pod creations and resulting in the 502 errors.

To investigate this hypothesis, the team ran the following command:
```
crictl info --output=json
```
This command provided detailed information about the node's configuration and the container runtime, which helped the team to rule out some potential causes and focus on the most likely explanation. However, it was not until they examined the node's kubelet configuration that they found the root cause of the issue.

## Resolution steps
1. Restart kubelet on node 3
2. Set maxPods to 110 in /etc/kubernetes/kubelet.conf
3. Run crictl pods to verify pod counts
4. Adjust kubelet flags with --max-pods=110
5. Verify node capacity with stern --tail 100

## Post-mortem note
The kubelet on node 3 was misconfigured with too low a maxPods value. This caused the node to reject new pod creations, resulting in the 502 errors observed by users. The team has updated the node's configuration to prevent similar issues in the future and is working to implement additional monitoring and automation to detect and respond to potential problems more quickly.
