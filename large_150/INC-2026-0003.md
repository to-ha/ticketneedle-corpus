# Major Incident — INC-2026-0003
* **Ticket ID:** INC-2026-0003
* **Reported:** 2026-02-27 09:01 UTC
* **Priority:** P1
* **Domain:** Network
* **Status:** Resolved

## Symptom
The incident began with reports of an MTU black hole between two sites, causing packet loss and disrupting network connectivity. Users observed that large packets were being dropped, resulting in failed transfers and timeouts. Initial observations suggested a potential issue with the MPLS L3VPN configuration.

Further investigation revealed that the problem was not limited to a specific application or service, but rather affected all traffic passing through the affected links. The symptom was consistent across multiple users and locations, indicating a network-wide issue.

The MTU black hole symptom was identified through MTR tests, which showed packet loss at specific hop sizes. This pattern suggested a mismatch in MTU settings between the two sites.

## Diagnosis steps and hypotheses
Initial hypotheses focused on potential issues with the network topology or routing tables. However, these were ruled out after verifying the configuration and performing tests with `mtr` and `nslookup`. 
```
mtr -r -c 10 10.1.1.1
```
The results of these tests pointed towards an issue with the MPLS L3VPN configuration, specifically related to MTU settings and routing tables.

## Resolution steps
1. Restart MPLS service on router 10.1.1.1
2. Set MTU to 1500 on interface eth0
3. Update routing table with new VPN route 192.168.2.0/24

## Post-mortem note
MPLS L3VPN configuration mismatch caused packet loss. The root cause was identified and addressed through the resolution steps, which focused on correcting the MTU settings and updating the routing table to ensure proper packet forwarding.
