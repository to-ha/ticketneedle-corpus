# Major Incident — INC-2026-0097
* **Ticket ID:** INC-2026-0097
* **Reported:** 2026-03-11 07:11 UTC
* **Priority:** P2
* **Domain:** Platform infrastructure
* **Status:** Resolved

## Symptom
The incident began with a noticeable increase in the message broker queue depth, which grew unbounded over time. This symptom was first observed by monitoring tools at 07:05 UTC, triggering an alert that was escalated to the on-call team. As the queue depth continued to rise, users started reporting delays in message processing, affecting the overall performance of the platform. The growth rate of the queue depth was alarming, with thousands of messages being added every minute. By 07:10 UTC, the queue had reached a critical level, prompting immediate action from the on-call team.

Further investigation revealed that the symptoms were not limited to the message broker. The secrets manager, responsible for handling sensitive information, was also experiencing unusual behavior. The manager's logs indicated a high number of connection attempts, which suggested a potential issue with the database connections. However, at this point, the exact cause of the problem was still unclear.

The user impact was significant, as the delays in message processing affected multiple services relying on the platform. The business impact was also substantial, with potential losses estimated in the thousands of dollars per hour. The on-call team recognized the urgency of the situation and quickly escalated the issue to the L1 Platform support.

As the incident progressed, the team observed that the secrets manager's behavior became increasingly erratic. The manager's connection attempts continued to rise, and the message broker queue depth showed no signs of decreasing. It became clear that the root cause of the problem was related to the secrets manager, but the exact nature of the issue remained unknown.

The monitoring tools played a crucial role in detecting the incident, as they provided real-time insights into the platform's performance. However, the tools did not catch the issue immediately, and it took several minutes for the alerts to trigger. This delay highlights the importance of fine-tuning monitoring tools to detect potential issues earlier.

In the initial stages of the incident, the team focused on understanding the symptoms and identifying potential causes. They analyzed logs, metrics, and system performance data to gather more information about the issue. The team also reached out to subject matter experts, including the L2 Platform engineer, to get their input on the potential causes of the problem.

## Diagnosis steps and hypotheses
The team initially suspected that the issue might be related to a misconfiguration of the message broker. They tried adjusting the broker's settings, but this did not resolve the problem. Next, they considered the possibility of a network issue, causing the secrets manager to experience connectivity problems. However, after running network diagnostics, no issues were found.

Another hypothesis was that the secrets manager was experiencing a high volume of requests, causing it to become overloaded. The team tried to reduce the load on the manager by temporarily disabling non-essential services, but this did not improve the situation.

The team also suspected that there might be an issue with the database connections used by the secrets manager. To investigate this further, they ran the following command:
```
kubectl logs secrets-manager -c manager | grep 'max connections'
```
This command revealed that the secrets manager was indeed experiencing issues with its database connections, but the exact nature of the problem was still unclear.

As the team continued to investigate, they considered the possibility of a resource constraint, such as insufficient memory or CPU, causing the secrets manager to malfunction. However, after analyzing system resources, no constraints were found.

Finally, the team suspected that the issue might be related to expired secrets, causing the secrets manager to become stuck in an infinite loop. They tried removing expired secrets using the broker-admin tool, but this did not resolve the problem.

After trying various hypotheses and diagnostic steps, the team eventually discovered that the root cause of the issue was related to the secrets manager's connection limit. The manager had exceeded its connection limit due to unreclaimed database connections, causing it to malfunction and leading to the unbounded growth of the message broker queue.

## Resolution steps
1. Restart secrets manager with --force-reload flag
2. Set max_connections to 100 in /etc/secrets-manager/config.yaml
3. Run registry-gc with --dry-run flag to identify unused secrets
4. Remove expired secrets using broker-admin --purge-expired
5. Verify runner-logs for successful secret rotation

## Post-mortem note
The secrets manager exceeded its connection limit due to unreclaimed database connections. This issue was missed initially because the monitoring tools did not provide clear insights into the secrets manager's performance, and the team did not suspect a connection limit issue until later in the investigation. To prevent similar incidents in the future, the team will implement additional monitoring and alerting for the secrets manager, focusing on connection limits and database performance. The team will also review the secrets manager's configuration and ensure that it is properly optimized for the platform's workload. Furthermore, the team will develop a playbook for handling secrets manager issues, including procedures for restarting the manager, adjusting connection limits, and removing expired secrets.
