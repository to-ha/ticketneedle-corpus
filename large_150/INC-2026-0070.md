## Symptom
The incident began with reports of pods failing to become Ready after being scaled up by the Horizontal Pod Autoscaler (HPA). This issue was first observed by the monitoring team, who noticed that the HPA was scaling up pods as expected, but the pods were not transitioning to the Ready state. As a result, the application was unable to handle incoming traffic, leading to a significant impact on user experience. The symptoms were initially thought to be related to a resource constraint, but further investigation revealed that the pods were experiencing network connectivity issues.

As the incident progressed, the team observed that the pods were able to connect to the cluster's internal network, but were unable to communicate with external services. This suggested that the issue was related to the pod's network configuration, rather than a cluster-wide problem. The team also noticed that the pods were experiencing high levels of packet loss, which further supported the theory that the issue was related to network connectivity.

The monitoring team used tools such as `stern` and `kubectl` to investigate the issue, but were unable to identify the root cause. The team also reviewed the cluster's logs and metrics, but did not find any obvious indicators of the problem. As a result, the incident was escalated to the L1 Platform support team for further investigation.

The L1 Platform support team attempted to reproduce the issue, but were unable to do so. They suspected that the issue might be related to a transient network problem, but were unable to find any evidence to support this theory. The team also reviewed the cluster's configuration and did not find any obvious issues.

As the incident continued to affect users, the team realized that they needed to escalate the issue to the L2 Platform engineer for further assistance. The L2 Platform engineer, Kenji, was notified of the issue and began to investigate.

## Diagnosis steps and hypotheses
The team considered several hypotheses to explain the issue, including a problem with the cluster's network configuration, a issue with the pod's network interface, and a problem with the CNI plugin. The team used tools such as `etcdctl` and `kustomize` to investigate the issue.

One hypothesis was that the issue was related to a misconfiguration of the CNI plugin. To investigate this, the team ran the following command:
```
etcdctl get /cni/config --endpoints=https://127.0.0.1:2379
```
This command revealed that the CNI plugin was configured to use a custom network configuration, which might be causing the issue.

Another hypothesis was that the issue was related to a problem with the pod's network interface. To investigate this, the team used `kubectl` to inspect the pod's network interface and did not find any obvious issues.

The team also considered the possibility that the issue was related to a transient network problem, but were unable to find any evidence to support this theory.

After ruling out several hypotheses, the team began to suspect that the issue might be related to the CNI plugin configuration. They used `etcdctl` to inspect the CNI plugin configuration and found that it was incorrectly set.

## Resolution steps
1. Restart CNI plugin with etcdctl --endpoints=https://127.0.0.1:2379
2. Set bridgeMTU to 1500 in /etc/cni/net.d/10-mynet.conf
3. Run kustomize build with --load-restrictor=none flag
4. Verify pod connectivity using stern --namespace=default
5. Update etcd with etcdctl member add --cluster-id=1234

## Post-mortem note
The CNI plugin configuration was incorrectly set to use an MTU of 9000. This incorrect configuration caused the pods to experience network connectivity issues, leading to the incident. The team should have caught this issue earlier by reviewing the CNI plugin configuration more closely. To prevent similar incidents in the future, the team will review the CNI plugin configuration as part of their regular maintenance checks. Additionally, the team will consider implementing automated checks to detect incorrect CNI plugin configurations.
