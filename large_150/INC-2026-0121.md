* **Ticket ID:** INC-2026-0121

* **Domain:** Containers / Kubernetes
* **Status:** Resolved

## Symptom
The ingress controller began returning 502 errors for a single backend service, indicating a failure to properly route traffic. This issue was first observed by monitoring tools and reported by users attempting to access the affected service. The error persisted across multiple attempts, suggesting a configuration or connectivity problem rather than a transient issue.

Further investigation revealed that the 502 errors were consistent and not intermittent, pointing towards a specific misconfiguration or failure within the ingress controller or its associated components. The impacted backend service was otherwise healthy, responding correctly to direct requests that bypassed the ingress controller.

## Escalation timeline
* 19:29 — L1 Platform support: Pavel immediately started reviewing the ingress controller logs for any signs of errors or misconfigurations.
* 20:05 — L2 Platform engineer: Bashir began examining the network connectivity and firewall rules to ensure they were not blocking traffic to the backend service.
* 20:38 — L3 Platform lead: Mei took over, focusing on the etcd configuration and its potential impact on the ingress controller's operation.

## Diagnosis steps and hypotheses
Initial hypotheses centered around misconfiguration of the ingress controller or issues with etcd, given the nature of the error. To diagnose, the team used `k9s` to inspect the Kubernetes resources and `stern` to review pod logs for any errors. A critical step involved checking the etcd store for any stale entries that might be affecting the ingress controller's configuration, using the command:
```
etcdctl get /ingress/controllers/active
```
This step was crucial in identifying the root cause of the issue.

## Resolution steps
1. Restart ingress controller pod 7
2. Set max-worker-connections to 10 in /etc/ingress/config.yaml
3. Update etcd key /ingress/controllers/active to true

## Post-mortem note
The ingress controller failed to sync its configuration due to a stale etcd entry. This incident highlights the importance of regularly reviewing etcd entries for staleness and ensuring that the ingress controller configuration is correctly synced across all necessary components.
