## Symptom
The incident began with a noticeable increase in query latency, where the p99 latency jumped from 20ms to 4s. This significant degradation in performance was first observed by the monitoring system, which triggered an alert to the on-call team. As users started to experience delays in their queries, the help desk received multiple reports of slow database performance. The issue seemed to be affecting all types of queries, regardless of their complexity or the data they accessed. Further investigation revealed that the increased latency was not limited to a specific set of users or applications but was rather a widespread issue affecting the entire database cluster.

Upon closer inspection of the monitoring metrics, it became apparent that the connection pooler was under heavy stress, with a large number of connections being held in a waiting state. This observation suggested that the pooler might be exhausting its available connections, leading to the increased latency as new queries were forced to wait for an available connection. The database's error logs also showed a significant increase in connection timeout errors, further supporting this hypothesis.

As the incident evolved, the team noticed that the query latency continued to deteriorate, with some queries timing out entirely. This escalation in symptoms prompted a more urgent response from the engineering team, as the database's performance was now severely impacted. The team quickly realized that resolving the issue would require a deep dive into the connection pooler's configuration and its interaction with the database.

The user impact of this incident was substantial, with many critical applications relying on the database for their operations. The prolonged latency and eventual timeouts resulted in failed transactions, lost productivity, and significant business disruption. The incident highlighted the need for more robust monitoring and alerting mechanisms to detect such issues earlier and prevent them from escalating into major incidents.

The initial response to the incident involved reviewing the database and connection pooler logs to understand the root cause of the increased latency. The team used tools like `explain analyze` to examine the query plans and identify any potential bottlenecks. They also utilized `iostat` to monitor disk I/O activity, suspecting that disk bottlenecks might be contributing to the delays. However, these initial investigations did not yield a clear cause, prompting the team to escalate the issue further.

## Diagnosis steps and hypotheses
The team considered several hypotheses to explain the increased latency and connection timeouts. One initial suspicion was that the database might be experiencing disk I/O bottlenecks, given the high volume of queries. To investigate this, the team used `iostat` to monitor disk activity, but the results showed that disk utilization was within normal limits, ruling out this hypothesis.

Another hypothesis was that the queries themselves might be inefficient, causing them to hold onto connections for too long. The team used `explain analyze` to review query plans, but most queries were using appropriate indexes and had reasonable execution plans, making this an unlikely cause.

The team also suspected that the connection pooler might be misconfigured, leading to an insufficient number of connections being available. To diagnose this, they executed the following command to inspect the current connections:
```
psql -c 'SELECT * FROM pg_stat_activity;'
```
This revealed a large number of idle connections, suggesting that the pooler was indeed not managing connections efficiently.

Further investigation into the connection pooler's configuration led to the discovery of several misconfigured parameters, which were likely contributing to the exhaustion of available connections. This finding aligned with the observation of a high number of waiting connections and supported the hypothesis that the connection pooler was the root cause of the incident.

After ruling out other potential causes, such as network issues or database locking contention, the team focused on adjusting the connection pooler's parameters to improve its efficiency and increase the availability of connections. This involved a series of trial adjustments, monitored closely with the metrics platform to assess their impact.

## Resolution steps
1. Restart connection pooler with max_connections=500
2. Set pool_size to 200 in /etc/dbconfig
3. Adjust idle_timeout to 60 seconds
4. Increase shared_buffers to 2GB
5. Run ANALYZE on pg_stat_activity view

## Post-mortem note
The connection pooler exhausted its available connections due to misconfigured parameters. This root cause was initially missed due to the complexity of the database environment and the multitude of potential factors that could contribute to such symptoms. In hindsight, a more thorough review of the connection pooler's configuration at the outset might have led to a quicker resolution. Following this incident, the team has implemented additional monitoring and alerting for connection pooler metrics to prevent similar issues from arising in the future. Moreover, a review of the database's configuration and performance tuning parameters is scheduled to ensure that all components are optimized for the current workload.
