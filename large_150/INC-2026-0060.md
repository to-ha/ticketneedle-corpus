# Major Incident — INC-2026-0060
* **Ticket ID:** INC-2026-0060
* **Reported:** 2026-01-13 21:32 UTC
* **Priority:** P2
* **Domain:** Storage
* **Status:** Resolved

## Symptom
The incident began with reports of object replication lag exceeding the SLA threshold in our cold-tier archive storage. This was first observed by monitoring tools at 21:30 UTC, triggering an alert that was immediately escalated to the on-call team. As the lag continued to grow, it became clear that this was not a transient issue but rather a sustained problem affecting data availability.

Further investigation revealed that the replication lag was consistently increasing over time, with no signs of self-correction or mitigation. This pattern suggested a systemic issue rather than a localized problem, prompting concerns about the overall health and configuration of the storage system. The impact on users was significant, as the increased lag times meant delays in accessing archived data, affecting both operational efficiency and compliance with data retrieval SLAs.

Given the nature of the symptom—focused on object replication lag—it was reasonable to suspect issues related to storage performance, network connectivity, or configuration mismatches between nodes. However, without immediate evidence pointing to a specific cause, it was necessary to embark on a diagnostic process to identify and address the root cause.

The observation of growing replication lag also led to considerations about recent changes in the storage environment, such as updates, additions of new nodes, or alterations in data handling policies. These factors could potentially introduce inconsistencies or bottlenecks in the system, contributing to the observed lag.

## Escalation timeline
* 21:43 — L1 Platform support: Dmitri began reviewing the initial alert and associated metrics to determine the scope of the issue.
* 22:09 — L2 Platform engineer: Greta took over the incident, focusing on in-depth analysis of storage node performance and network connectivity.

## Diagnosis steps and hypotheses
Initial hypotheses included potential misconfigurations in the storage nodes, issues with the networking infrastructure affecting data transfer, or problems with the disk health of the storage devices. To explore these hypotheses, several diagnostic steps were undertaken. 
``` 
multipath -ll /dev/sdb
```
was executed to verify the path status of the storage device, checking for any signs of failure or misconfiguration that could impede data replication.

Another line of investigation involved analyzing the system's metrics using `iostat` to assess disk utilization and performance, looking for any bottlenecks or anomalies that could explain the replication lag. Additionally, `smartctl` was used to check the health of the disks, as faulty hardware could also be a contributing factor.

Despite these efforts, initial hypotheses about network issues or disk failures were not supported by the evidence. The focus then shifted towards configuration and policy settings, considering whether recent changes might have inadvertently affected the replication process.

## Resolution steps
1. Restart storage service on node 3
2. Set archive policy retention period to 365 days
3. Run multipath -ll on /dev/sdb to verify path status
4. Adjust iostat sampling interval to 10 seconds
5. Execute smartctl -H /dev/sda to check disk health

## Post-mortem note
The cold-tier archive lifecycle policy was not updated after adding new storage nodes. This oversight led to a mismatch in how data was being managed across the storage environment, ultimately resulting in the observed replication lag. Understanding the importance of maintaining consistent policies across all nodes will be key to preventing similar incidents in the future.
