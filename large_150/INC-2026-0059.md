# Major Incident — INC-2026-0059
- **Ticket ID:** INC-2026-0059
- **Reported:** 2026-03-31 13:32 UTC
- **Priority:** P2
- **Domain:** Database
- **Status:** Resolved

## Symptom
The incident began with a noticeable spike in deadlocks during a release window, which was initially observed by the monitoring system. The deadlocks were causing significant delays in query execution, leading to a substantial increase in latency. As the situation evolved, the database team started receiving reports from users experiencing timeouts and errors when attempting to connect to the database. The user impact was severe, with many critical applications relying on the database for their operations. The monitoring system caught the initial spike in deadlocks but missed the underlying cause, which was related to the read-replica pair.

Further investigation revealed that the deadlocks were not just a simple spike but a persistent issue that was affecting the overall performance of the database. The database team started digging deeper into the logs and metrics to understand the root cause of the problem. They found that the number of client connections to the read-replica pair had increased significantly, causing contention for resources and leading to deadlocks. The team also noticed that the replication lag between the primary and replica databases was increasing, which was a clear indication of a problem with the read-replica pair.

The symptoms were not limited to just deadlocks; the team also observed a significant increase in CPU usage on the replica host, which was causing further delays in query execution. The metrics platform showed a clear correlation between the increase in client connections and the rise in CPU usage, indicating that the read-replica pair was struggling to handle the workload. The database team realized that they needed to act quickly to resolve the issue and prevent further impact on the users.

As the incident progressed, the team continued to monitor the situation closely, gathering more data and insights into the problem. They used tools like `pgbouncer` logs, `pg_stat_activity` views, and `pidstat` to understand the behavior of the read-replica pair and identify potential bottlenecks. The team's focus was on resolving the issue as quickly as possible while minimizing the impact on the users.

The initial response to the incident was to increase the number of client connections allowed on the read-replica pair, but this only provided temporary relief. The team soon realized that they needed to address the underlying cause of the problem, which was related to the configuration of the read-replica pair and the behavior of the clients connecting to it.

## Diagnosis steps and hypotheses
The team initially suspected that the issue was related to a problem with the primary database, possibly a configuration error or a hardware failure. They started by checking the primary database logs and metrics, looking for any signs of trouble. However, after analyzing the data, they found no evidence of any issues with the primary database.

Next, the team suspected that the problem might be related to the network connectivity between the primary and replica databases. They used tools like `dig` and `tcpdump` to check the network connectivity, but found no issues.

Another hypothesis was that the issue was related to a problem with the read-replica pair itself, possibly a configuration error or a hardware failure. The team started investigating the read-replica pair by checking the logs and metrics, looking for any signs of trouble. They used the following command to check the `pgbouncer` logs:
```
pgbouncer -v /var/log/pgbouncer.log
```
This command provided valuable insights into the behavior of the read-replica pair and helped the team understand the cause of the deadlocks.

The team also suspected that the issue might be related to a problem with the clients connecting to the read-replica pair, possibly a configuration error or a bug in the client software. They started analyzing the client connections and found that there were many idle connections that were holding onto resources, causing contention and leading to deadlocks.

After ruling out these hypotheses, the team finally discovered the root cause of the issue, which was related to the configuration of the read-replica pair and the behavior of the clients connecting to it. The team found that the number of client connections allowed on the read-replica pair was too low, causing contention for resources and leading to deadlocks.

## Resolution steps
1. Restart pgbouncer on read-replica at 10.0.1.100
2. Set max_client_conn to 500 in pgbouncer.ini
3. Run pidstat -p 1234 to monitor cpu usage of replica process
4. Adjust kernel.shmmax to 1073741824 on replica host
5. Verify replication lag with psql -c 'SELECT pg_last_xlog_receive_location()' on replica

## Post-mortem note
The read-replica pair experienced connection overload due to excessive client connections. This issue was missed initially because the monitoring system was not configured to alert on the number of client connections, and the team did not have a clear understanding of the behavior of the clients connecting to the read-replica pair. In hindsight, the team should have been more proactive in monitoring the read-replica pair and analyzing the client connections. The incident highlighted the importance of having a robust monitoring system and a deep understanding of the database configuration and client behavior. The team has since implemented additional monitoring and alerting to prevent similar incidents in the future.
