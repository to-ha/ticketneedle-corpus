## Symptom
The incident began with reports of growing replication lag in the database, which started to increase unbounded after the midnight cron job. This symptom was first observed by the monitoring system, which alerted the L1 Database operator, Rafael, at 20:29 UTC. Initially, the lag was manageable, but as the night progressed, it continued to grow, causing concerns about data consistency and availability. The replication lag was observed using pidstat, which showed a significant increase in the time it took for the replica to catch up with the primary node. Further investigation revealed that the autovacuum daemon was not able to keep up with the transaction volume, leading to an accumulation of dead tuples and causing the replication lag to grow.

As the incident evolved, the user impact became more pronounced, with reports of slow query performance and timeouts. The business impact was significant, as the database is a critical component of the company's operations, and any downtime or performance issues can have far-reaching consequences. The monitoring system caught the initial symptom, but it did not alert on the underlying cause, which was the autovacuum daemon's inability to keep up with the transaction volume. This highlights the importance of having a comprehensive monitoring system that can detect not only symptoms but also underlying causes.

The symptom complex was characterized by a growing replication lag, slow query performance, and timeouts. The pgbouncer logs showed an increase in the number of connections being refused due to the growing replication lag, which further exacerbated the issue. The pidstat output revealed that the autovacuum daemon was not able to keep up with the transaction volume, leading to an accumulation of dead tuples and causing the replication lag to grow.

The incident highlighted the need for more granular monitoring of the autovacuum daemon's performance and its impact on the database. Currently, the monitoring system only alerts on replication lag, but it does not provide insights into the underlying causes. To improve the monitoring system, additional metrics should be collected, such as the autovacuum daemon's throughput and the number of dead tuples.

In addition to the monitoring system, the incident also highlighted the need for more comprehensive logging and auditing. The pgbouncer logs provided valuable insights into the issue, but they did not provide enough information to diagnose the root cause. To improve the logging and auditing, additional log messages should be added to capture key events, such as autovacuum daemon restarts and changes to the database configuration.

## Diagnosis steps and hypotheses
The team suspected that the issue might be related to the autovacuum daemon's configuration, specifically the `autovacuum_vacuum_scale_factor` setting. To investigate this hypothesis, the team ran the command:
``` 
psql -c 'SHOW autovacuum_vacuum_scale_factor;'
```
The output showed that the setting was not optimal for the current workload, which led the team to consider adjusting it. However, this hypothesis was later ruled out as the root cause.

Another hypothesis considered was that the replication lag was caused by a network issue between the primary and replica nodes. The team used `dig` and `tcpdump` to investigate the network connectivity and latency but found no issues.

The team also suspected that the issue might be related to the database's disk space usage, specifically the amount of free space available for the autovacuum daemon to operate. However, after checking the disk usage, it was found that there was sufficient free space, ruling out this hypothesis.

Additionally, the team considered the possibility that the issue was caused by a recent change to the database configuration. However, after reviewing the configuration history, no recent changes were found that could have caused the issue.

The team also explored the hypothesis that the autovacuum daemon was not running or was running with insufficient resources. However, after checking the daemon's status and resource utilization, it was found that it was running and had sufficient resources.

As the investigation progressed, the team began to suspect that the root cause might be related to the autovacuum daemon's ability to keep up with the transaction volume. This hypothesis was supported by the growing replication lag and the accumulation of dead tuples.

## Resolution steps
1. Set autovacuum_vacuum_scale_factor to 0.1
2. Increase autovacuum_vacuum_threshold to 1000
3. Restart autovacuum daemon with -d option
4. Adjust pidstat sampling interval to 10 seconds
5. Set log_autovacuum_min_duration to 100ms in psql
