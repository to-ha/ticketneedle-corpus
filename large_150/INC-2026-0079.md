## Symptom
The metrics ingestion lag was causing dashboard staleness, which was first observed by the monitoring team. This issue resulted in outdated metrics being displayed on the dashboards, making it difficult for the team to monitor the system's performance in real-time. The lag was consistent across all metrics, indicating a systemic issue rather than a problem with a specific metric or data source.

Further investigation revealed that the ingestion lag had been increasing over the past few hours, suggesting a gradual degradation of the system's ability to process metrics. This increase in lag time correlated with a rise in disk usage on the metrics storage server, hinting at a possible connection between the two.

The monitoring team tried to identify the root cause of the issue by checking the system logs and metrics ingestion rates. However, the sheer volume of data and the complexity of the system made it challenging to pinpoint the exact cause without further assistance.

## Diagnosis steps and hypotheses
Initial hypotheses included issues with the metrics ingestion pipeline, problems with the storage backend, or configuration errors. To diagnose the issue, the team tried running `log-tail -f /var/log/metrics/error.log` to check for any error messages that could indicate the root cause of the problem.
```
log-tail -f /var/log/metrics/error.log
```
This command helped identify a pattern of disk space warnings, which suggested that the storage was indeed a critical factor in the metrics ingestion lag.

## Resolution steps
1. Restart TSDB with --storage.path=/var/metrics
2. Set retention policy to 30d in /etc/metrics/config.yml
3. Run promtool to compact storage at /var/metrics/series
4. Verify disk space usage on /var/metrics is below 80%

## Post-mortem note
The long-term metrics storage exceeded its retention period and filled the disk. This incident highlights the importance of regularly monitoring disk usage and adjusting retention policies accordingly to prevent similar issues in the future.
