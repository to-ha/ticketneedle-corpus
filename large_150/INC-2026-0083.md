## Symptom

The incident presented itself as a MTU black hole between two sites, causing intermittent delays in outbound API calls. Users reported that these calls would occasionally take 5-10 seconds to complete because DNS resolution would stall. This slowdown would disappear for a few minutes after a resolver cache flush, only to return later. The pattern suggested a network-related issue that was affecting the efficiency of data transfer between sites.

Further investigation revealed that the problem was not consistent and seemed to be triggered by specific conditions. The delays were not limited to API calls but also affected other services relying on DNS resolution. This indicated that the root cause might be related to how traffic was being handled or routed through the network, particularly in relation to stateful firewall configurations.

Observations from monitoring tools showed periods of increased latency and packet loss, which correlated with the times when users experienced delays. This suggested that there was a capacity or configuration issue within the network that was causing the symptoms. Tools like `tcpdump`, `MTR`, and `nslookup` were utilized to gather more detailed information about the network traffic and potential bottlenecks.

Given the nature of the symptom, it was crucial to focus on the stateful firewall pair as the primary point of investigation. This was because the firewall's role in managing and filtering traffic could directly impact the performance and reliability of network communications, especially under conditions of high load or specific packet characteristics.

## Diagnosis steps and hypotheses

Initial hypotheses centered around potential misconfigurations of the stateful firewall or issues with the DNS resolution process itself. The team considered the possibility of the firewall dropping packets due to incorrect rules or insufficient resources to handle the volume of traffic. 

To gather more evidence, the team used `tcpdump` to capture network traffic, specifically focusing on DNS-related communications:
```
tcpdump -i any -n -vv -s 0 -c 100 port 53
```
This helped in understanding the flow of DNS packets and identifying if there were any patterns or anomalies that could point to the root cause.

Another hypothesis explored was related to the MTU size and potential fragmentation issues, given the nature of the black hole observed between the two sites. However, upon further investigation, it became clear that the issue was more closely related to the stateful firewall's capacity to handle connections rather than a straightforward MTU or fragmentation problem.

## Resolution steps

1. Set net.netfilter.nf_conntrack_max to 262144
2. Shorten UDP timeout to 10s in conntrack config
3. Reload conntrack module with new settings
4. Verify conntrack table size with sysctl -p

## Post-mortem note

The root cause of the incident was identified as the stateful firewall's conntrack table becoming exhausted under burst load, leading to dropped new UDP/53 flows. Conntrack table on stateful firewall was exhausted under burst load, dropping new UDP/53 flows.
