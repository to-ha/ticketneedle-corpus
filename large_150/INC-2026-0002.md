## Symptom
The incident began with a noticeable spike in DNS resolution latency, with response times increasing to 5-10 seconds. This symptom was first observed by users attempting to access internal resources, who reported delays in loading web pages and accessing network shares. Monitoring tools also picked up on the increase in latency, triggering alerts to the L1 Network operations team.

Further investigation revealed that the latency spike was not limited to a specific subset of users or resources, but rather was a widespread issue affecting all network traffic that relied on DNS resolution. This suggested a problem with the underlying network infrastructure, rather than a specific application or service.

The symptom was consistent across multiple locations and networks, indicating a centralized issue rather than a localized problem. This led the team to focus on the core network components, including routers and firewalls, as potential sources of the problem.

- 14:43 — L1 Network operations: Greta began reviewing network logs and monitoring data to identify the source of the latency spike.
- 14:58 — L2 Network engineer: Leila took over the investigation, focusing on the configuration of the routers and firewalls to ensure that they were not contributing to the problem.
- 15:21 — L3 Network architect: Sade was brought in to review the overall network design and architecture, looking for any potential bottlenecks or misconfigurations that could be causing the issue.

## Diagnosis steps and hypotheses
Initial hypotheses focused on potential issues with the DNS servers themselves, such as misconfiguration or overload. However, investigation revealed that the DNS servers were functioning correctly and responding to queries in a timely manner. The team then turned their attention to the network infrastructure, using tools like `traceroute` to investigate potential bottlenecks or misconfigurations.
```
traceroute -n 10.1.1.1
```
This led to a focus on the BGP peering session, which was identified as a potential source of the problem.

## Resolution steps
1. Restart BGP service on router 10.1.1.1
2. Set hold time to 30 seconds on router 10.1.1.1
3. Update BGP config with new ASN 12345 on router 10.1.1.1

## Post-mortem note
The BGP peering session failed due to a mismatched hold time. This highlighted the importance of ensuring consistent configuration across all network devices, particularly when it comes to critical protocols like BGP. In the future, the team will prioritize regular configuration reviews and audits to prevent similar issues from arising.
