- **Reported:** 2026-02-04 01:01 UTC
- **Priority:** P1
- **Domain:** Containers / Kubernetes
- **Status:** Resolved

## Symptom
The incident began with reports of pods stuck in CrashLoopBackOff after a node reboot. This pattern was consistently observed across multiple deployments, indicating a widespread issue rather than an isolated problem with a specific pod or application. The monitoring system alerted the team to an unusual spike in container restarts, which further pointed towards a node-level issue rather than a problem with the deployed applications themselves.

Upon initial investigation, it became clear that the affected pods were all scheduled on node-1, suggesting that the issue was localized to this specific node. The node had been recently rebooted as part of routine maintenance, which might have triggered the problem. However, without further diagnosis, it was unclear if the reboot was the cause or merely a coincidence.

Further observation showed that new pods could not be scheduled on node-1, and existing pods that were restarted due to the CrashLoopBackOff condition could not recover. This indicated a severe disruption to the normal functioning of the Kubernetes cluster, necessitating immediate attention to prevent further degradation of service.

The impact of this symptom was significant, as it affected the availability of several critical services hosted on the Kubernetes cluster. Users began reporting errors and timeouts when attempting to access these services, highlighting the need for a swift resolution to the issue.

## Diagnosis steps and hypotheses
Initial hypotheses included the possibility of a configuration error introduced during the node reboot, a resource exhaustion issue preventing pods from running, or a problem with the node's kubelet service. To investigate these hypotheses, several diagnostic steps were taken. 

One key command used in the diagnosis was:
```
crictl logs -n 1234567890ab
```
This command helped in understanding the container logs and identifying any patterns or errors that could point towards the root cause of the CrashLoopBackOff condition.

Despite these efforts, the initial hypotheses were not fully supported by the evidence. For instance, resource utilization on the node did not appear to be at a critical level, and there were no obvious configuration errors in the kubelet settings. It was necessary to continue investigating to find the root cause of the issue.

## Resolution steps
1. Restart kubelet on node-1
2. Set --max-pods to 100 in /etc/kubernetes/kubelet.conf
3. Run crictl stopp 1234567890ab to free up resources
4. Verify etcdctl member list shows node-1 as healthy

## Post-mortem note
The kubelet on node-1 failed to restart after a config change. This root cause was identified after detailed diagnosis and was addressed through the resolution steps outlined above. The successful recovery of node-1 and the resolution of the CrashLoopBackOff issue for the affected pods highlight the importance of thorough troubleshooting and the need for prompt action in response to critical incidents affecting Kubernetes cluster stability.
