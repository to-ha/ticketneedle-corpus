## Symptom
The incident began with reports of pods stuck in CrashLoopBackOff after a node reboot. This symptom was consistently observed across multiple deployments, suggesting a systemic issue rather than an application-specific problem. Further investigation revealed that the affected pods were dependent on a service that relied on etcd for configuration and state management.

Upon closer inspection, it became clear that the CrashLoopBackOff state was not resolving as expected, indicating a potential issue with the underlying infrastructure or the etcd cluster itself. The monitoring system showed an increase in error rates for requests to etcd, hinting at a performance or availability problem.

Given the critical role of etcd in managing the Kubernetes cluster's state, any disruption to its service could have far-reaching consequences, including the inability to schedule new pods or maintain the health of existing ones.

## Diagnosis steps and hypotheses
Initial hypotheses centered around network connectivity issues or resource constraints affecting etcd. However, after running diagnostic commands such as 
```
kubectl get etcdcluster -o yaml
```
it became apparent that the issue was more closely related to etcd's internal state and configuration rather than external factors.

## Resolution steps
1. Restart etcd with --listen-peer-urls=http://localhost:2380
2. Set etcd quorum learn timeout to 15s in /etc/etcd/etcd.conf
3. Increase etcd log level to debug with --log-level=debug flag

## Post-mortem note
The etcd cluster lost quorum due to misconfigured peer URLs. This incident highlights the importance of regularly reviewing and validating the configuration of critical components like etcd to prevent such disruptions.
