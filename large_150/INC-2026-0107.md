# Major Incident — INC-2026-0107
* **Ticket ID:** INC-2026-0107
* **Reported:** 2026-03-14 08:06 UTC
* **Priority:** P1
* **Domain:** Database
* **Status:** Resolved

## Symptom
The incident began with a noticeable increase in query latency, where the p99 latency jumped from 20ms to 4s. This significant degradation in performance was first observed by monitoring tools and promptly reported by the on-call team. As the situation unfolded, it became clear that this latency issue was not transient but rather a persistent problem affecting a wide range of database queries. The impact was substantial, with many applications relying on the database experiencing timeouts and errors, leading to a cascade of failures across multiple services.

Further investigation into the symptom revealed that the issue was closely related to the WAL (Write-Ahead Logging) archive process. The process, critical for ensuring database consistency and durability, seemed to be under stress, possibly due to an imbalance in resource allocation or configuration settings. This hypothesis was supported by observations of increased disk I/O wait times and higher than usual CPU utilization on the database servers.

The latency increase and its relation to the WAL archive process hinted at a deeper issue that required immediate attention. Given the critical nature of the database in supporting core business operations, resolving this incident quickly was paramount. The observable pattern of query latency increase served as the primary focus for the initial diagnostic efforts.

As the on-call engineer delved deeper into the issue, tools like `pidstat` and `pgbouncer` logs were utilized to gain insights into the system's current state and performance bottlenecks. The `explain analyze` command was also employed to understand the execution plans of affected queries, providing valuable information on where time was being spent during query execution.

## Diagnosis steps and hypotheses
Diagnosing the root cause of the issue involved considering several hypotheses, each of which was systematically evaluated and ruled out based on evidence. Initially, it was thought that the problem might stem from inadequate resource allocation to the database server, such as insufficient RAM or CPU cores. However, upon reviewing the system metrics, it became apparent that resources were not the limiting factor.

Another hypothesis considered was that the issue could be related to network connectivity problems between the database server and its clients. This was tested by running network diagnostics, but no issues were found that could account for the observed latency.

To gain more insight into the performance of the WAL archive process, the command ```pidstat -p $(pgrep archive) 1 10``` was used. This provided detailed statistics on the CPU usage of the archive process over time, helping to pinpoint if the process was indeed a bottleneck.

Given the focus on the WAL archive process and its potential impact on database performance, the team also examined the `pgbouncer` logs for any signs of connection pooling issues or other database client-related problems. While these logs provided useful context, they did not directly point to the root cause of the latency issue.

## Resolution steps
1. Restart WAL archive process with archive_timeout=60s
2. Set wal_sender_timeout to 30s in postgresql.conf
3. Increase max_wal_senders to 5 in postgresql.conf
4. Verify archive_command points to valid directory /var/lib/pgsql/archive

## Post-mortem note
The WAL archive process failed due to excessive timeouts and insufficient sender resources. This incident highlights the importance of regularly reviewing database configuration settings and monitoring performance metrics to prevent similar issues from arising in the future. By adjusting the timeouts and increasing the resources available to the WAL archive process, we were able to resolve the incident and restore normal database operation.
