## Symptom
The incident began with reports of growing replication lag in the database cluster, particularly noticeable after the midnight cron job ran. This symptom was first observed by the monitoring system, which alerted the on-call engineer to potential issues. As the morning progressed, the lag continued to increase, causing concerns about data consistency and availability. Further investigation revealed that the issue was tied to the WAL archive process, which seemed to be underperforming. The replication lag grew unbounded, indicating a problem with how the database was handling its write-ahead logging (WAL) archives.

The impact of this growing lag was significant, as it affected the ability of the database to serve read replicas efficiently, leading to increased latency for queries that relied on these replicas. This, in turn, started to affect application performance, with some users reporting slower response times. The monitoring system's alerts and the initial observations from the on-call engineer, Mateo, pointed towards a database-centric issue that needed immediate attention.

Given the focus on the WAL archive process and its potential impact on replication lag, the engineering team concentrated on understanding the current state of the database's logging and archiving mechanisms. This involved checking the disk usage, as excessive disk consumption could lead to performance issues with the WAL archive process. The team also started looking into the SQL queries being executed around the time the replication lag began to increase, using tools like `explain analyze` to understand query performance.

As the incident unfolded, it became clear that resolving the issue quickly was crucial to preventing further degradation of service. The team's initial steps included reviewing database metrics, such as those provided by `pg_stat_activity`, to identify any bottlenecks or unusual patterns that could be contributing to the replication lag.

## Diagnosis steps and hypotheses
Initial hypotheses included the possibility of a configuration issue with the WAL archive process, a disk space problem, or even a network connectivity issue affecting the replication. The team considered the hypothesis that the database might be experiencing contention due to high transaction volumes, but evidence from `pg_stat_activity` views did not support this theory. Another hypothesis was that the system might be running low on resources (CPU, memory), but checks on these metrics did not reveal any shortages.

To further diagnose the issue, the team executed a command to check disk usage and performance:
```
iostat -xd 2
```
This command provided insights into the disk I/O statistics, which helped in understanding if there were any bottlenecks at the storage level. However, the output did not immediately point to a disk I/O issue as the primary cause, leading the team to consider other factors affecting the WAL archive process.

## Resolution steps
1. Restart WAL archive process with -o flag
2. Set archive_timeout to 60 seconds
3. Increase max_wal_senders to 10
4. Reduce wal_level to minimal
5. Remove stale archives older than 7 days

## Post-mortem note
The WAL archive process failed due to excessive disk usage. This root cause was identified after a thorough analysis of the database's logging and archiving mechanisms, coupled with the diagnostic steps taken during the incident. The resolution steps effectively addressed the issue by ensuring the WAL archive process was properly managed, and preventive measures were put in place to avoid similar incidents in the future.
