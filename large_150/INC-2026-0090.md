## Symptom

The incident began with reports of service discovery returning stale endpoints after a deploy. This issue manifested as users being directed to outdated or non-existent service instances, resulting in failed requests and errors. Initially, the symptoms were intermittent and only affected a small subset of users, but as time progressed, the issue became more widespread and persistent. The monitoring system caught the increase in error rates and alerted the on-call team. However, the root cause of the issue was not immediately apparent, and it required further investigation to identify the problem.

As the incident evolved, it became clear that the stale endpoints were being returned due to a caching issue within the internal DNS system. The cache was not being updated correctly, leading to outdated information being served to users. This caching issue was not immediately obvious, and it took some time to isolate the problem. The user impact was significant, with many users experiencing errors and frustration due to the stale endpoints.

The monitoring system played a crucial role in detecting the issue, as it alerted the on-call team to the increase in error rates. However, the monitoring system did not provide enough information to immediately identify the root cause of the issue. Further investigation was required to determine the cause of the caching issue and to develop a plan to resolve it.

The incident highlighted the importance of having a robust monitoring system in place, as well as the need for effective troubleshooting and diagnostic tools. The fact that the issue was not immediately apparent and required further investigation underscores the complexity of the system and the need for skilled engineers to troubleshoot and resolve issues.

In addition to the technical impact, the incident also had a significant business impact. The errors and frustration experienced by users led to a loss of trust and confidence in the system, which could have long-term consequences if not addressed. Therefore, it was essential to resolve the issue quickly and effectively to minimize the business impact.

## Diagnosis steps and hypotheses

The diagnosis process involved considering several hypotheses and testing them to determine the root cause of the issue. One initial hypothesis was that the DNS server was not configured correctly, leading to caching issues. To test this hypothesis, the team checked the DNS server configuration and found that it was set up correctly. However, they did notice that the cache expiration time was set relatively low, which could potentially lead to frequent refresh failures.

Another hypothesis was that the issue was related to the deploy process itself, perhaps due to a misconfiguration or error during the deployment. To investigate this, the team reviewed the deploy logs and found no issues or errors that could be contributing to the problem.

The team also considered the possibility that the issue was related to the vault configuration, perhaps due to an incorrect or outdated DNS server IP address. To test this hypothesis, they checked the vault configuration and found that it was correct.

To further investigate the issue, the team used the `dig` command to test the DNS resolution:
```
dig +short example.service @10.0.1.5
```
This command helped to isolate the issue and determine that the problem was indeed related to the DNS caching.

After ruling out several hypotheses, the team finally discovered that the internal DNS cache expiration was set too low, causing frequent refresh failures. This led to the stale endpoints being returned to users.

## Resolution steps

1. Set DNS_CACHE_TTL to 300 in /etc/dns/config
2. Restart dns-server on node 10.0.1.5
3. Update vault with new DNS_SERVER_IP at path secret/dns
4. Run flag-history to verify --dns-cache-ttl flag
5. Verify dns-server logs at /var/log/dns/server.log

## Post-mortem note

The internal DNS cache expiration was set too low, causing frequent refresh failures. This issue was missed initially due to the complexity of the system and the fact that the monitoring system did not provide enough information to immediately identify the root cause. In hindsight, the team could have moved more quickly to investigate the DNS caching issue, rather than exploring other hypotheses first. To prevent similar issues in the future, the team will review the DNS configuration and caching settings to ensure they are optimal, and will also work to improve the monitoring system to provide more detailed information about DNS-related issues. Additionally, the team will conduct a thorough review of the incident to identify areas for improvement and implement changes to prevent similar incidents from occurring in the future.
