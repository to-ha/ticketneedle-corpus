## Symptom
The BGP session was flapping every few minutes, causing intermittent connectivity issues for users. This pattern was observed by the monitoring system and reported by several users who experienced dropped connections. The flapping BGP session resulted in brief outages, making it challenging for users to maintain a stable connection.

Upon further investigation, it became clear that the issue was not limited to a specific user or location, but rather a widespread problem affecting multiple users across the network. The monitoring system's alerts and user reports both pointed to the BGP session flapping as the primary symptom of the incident.

## Escalation timeline
* 15:39 — L1 Network operations: Kenji - Kenji began investigating the issue and gathering initial data on the BGP session flapping.
* 15:52 — L2 Network engineer: Liu - Liu took over the investigation, focusing on potential network configuration issues that could be causing the BGP session instability.

## Diagnosis steps and hypotheses
Initial hypotheses focused on potential misconfigurations or conflicts within the network. To troubleshoot the issue, the `iperf3` tool was used to test network performance, as seen in the command:
```
iperf3 -c router1 -p 5001
```
This test helped rule out certain hypotheses related to network congestion or performance issues. Further investigation led to a deeper examination of the OSPF backbone area configuration, seeking any inconsistencies that could explain the BGP session flapping.

## Resolution steps
1. Set OSPF cost to 10 on interface eth0
2. Restart OSPF service on router1
3. Increase hello interval to 30 seconds

## Post-mortem note
The OSPF backbone area failed to converge due to mismatched timers. This root cause, once identified and addressed through the resolution steps, resolved the BGP session flapping issue, restoring stable connectivity across the network.
