## Symptom
The application connection pool began reporting periodic spikes of dropped connections, with the pattern repeating every 30-45 minutes and clearing within a minute. This symptom was first observed during a release window, where deadlocks also spiked. The monitoring system alerted the team to the issue, and initial investigation revealed that the connection pool was experiencing a high rate of timeouts and failures. As the team dug deeper, it became clear that the issue was related to the database, specifically with the WAL archive process. The pattern of dropped connections seemed to be correlated with the timing of the release window, suggesting that some aspect of the database configuration or workload was contributing to the problem.

Further investigation revealed that the deadlocks were occurring due to a buildup of pending transactions, which in turn was causing the connection pool to timeout and drop connections. The team suspected that there might be an issue with the database's ability to process transactions efficiently, possibly related to the WAL archive process. The symptom was consistent across multiple instances of the application, suggesting a systemic issue rather than a localized problem.

The team began to suspect that the issue might be related to the replication slot, as the error messages and logs suggested a problem with the slot's size and the writer's ability to keep up with the workload. The periodic nature of the spikes also suggested that there might be a timing-related issue, possibly related to the way the database was configured to handle replication.

As the incident continued, the team worked to gather more data and understand the root cause of the issue. They collected logs and metrics from the database and the application, and began to analyze the data to identify patterns and correlations. The team also reached for tools like psql and pg_stat_activity to gain a better understanding of the database's behavior and identify potential bottlenecks.

## Diagnosis steps and hypotheses
The team considered several hypotheses to explain the symptom, including issues with the database configuration, problems with the replication slot, and timing-related issues. One hypothesis was that the replication slot had grown too large, causing the writer to pause and leading to a buildup of pending transactions. To investigate this hypothesis, the team used the following command to check the status of the replication slot:
```
psql -c "SELECT * FROM pg_replication_slots WHERE slot_name = 'slot_123';"
```
The results of this query suggested that the replication slot was indeed large, but did not provide a clear explanation for the periodic spikes in dropped connections. The team also considered the possibility that the issue was related to the WAL archive process, and investigated the database's configuration and logs to see if there were any issues with the process.

As the team continued to investigate, they ruled out several hypotheses, including issues with the database's memory allocation and problems with the network connectivity. However, the hypothesis that the replication slot had grown too large and was causing the writer to pause remained a strong candidate, and the team decided to investigate further.

## Resolution steps
1. Drop orphaned replication slot named 'slot_123' using psql
2. Set wal_sender_timeout to 60 seconds in postgresql.conf
3. Add monitoring alert for replication slot size exceeding 30 GB
4. Restart postgres service to apply configuration changes
5. Verify slot size using psql command: SELECT pg_size_pretty(pg_wal_lsn_diff(pg_current_wal_lsn(), 'slot_123'))
