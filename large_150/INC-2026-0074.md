## Symptom
The incident began with reports of object replication lag exceeding the SLA threshold in our distributed filesystem. Initially, the lag was observed to be around 10 minutes, but it quickly grew to over an hour, prompting immediate attention from the on-call team. The monitoring system alerted the team to this issue after detecting a significant increase in replication latency. As the incident evolved, users started experiencing delays in data availability across different nodes, affecting their ability to access and process data in a timely manner. The business impact was substantial, with several critical workflows being slowed down or halted due to the unavailability of fresh data.

Further investigation into the monitoring data revealed that the issue was not limited to a specific set of objects or nodes but was rather a widespread problem affecting the entire distributed filesystem. The metrics platform showed a sharp increase in error rates for replication operations, indicating a systemic issue rather than an isolated problem. Despite the comprehensive monitoring in place, the root cause of the replication lag remained elusive, requiring a deeper dive into the system's configuration and operational logs.

The symptom complex was characterized by a growing replication lag, increased error rates in replication operations, and a consequent impact on data availability and business workflows. The fact that the issue affected the entire distributed filesystem suggested a potential problem with the underlying infrastructure or configuration rather than a localized fault. As the on-call team delved deeper into the issue, they focused on understanding the current state of the metadata servers, the health of the storage devices, and the configuration of the replication factor, given their critical roles in maintaining data consistency and availability across the distributed filesystem.

The initial hypothesis was that the issue might be related to network connectivity or storage device health, given the nature of the symptoms. However, as the team began to analyze logs and system metrics, it became clear that the problem was more complex and likely related to the configuration or operational state of the metadata servers. The team decided to investigate further by checking the status of the metadata servers, running diagnostic commands to assess the health of the storage devices, and reviewing the replication factor configuration to ensure it was optimal for the current workload.

As the investigation progressed, it became apparent that the object replication lag was not only affecting data availability but also causing a backlog of pending replication operations. This backlog had the potential to exacerbate the issue if not addressed promptly, as it could lead to further delays and increased latency in the system. The team recognized the need to resolve the issue quickly to prevent long-term impacts on the business workflows that relied on the distributed filesystem.

## Diagnosis steps and hypotheses
The team initially suspected a network connectivity issue between the nodes, given the nature of the replication lag. To investigate this hypothesis, they ran `tcpdump` on key nodes to capture network traffic related to replication operations. However, the captured traffic did not reveal any significant issues with network connectivity, ruling out this hypothesis.

Another hypothesis considered was that the storage devices might be experiencing hardware failures or performance degradation, leading to slowed replication. The team used `multipath -ll` to check the health and status of the storage paths but found all paths to be healthy and functioning correctly, which ruled out storage device issues as the primary cause.

The possibility of a misconfigured replication factor was also explored. The team reviewed the configuration files but initially did not identify any obvious misconfigurations. However, this hypothesis was revisited later in the investigation with more scrutiny.

To further diagnose the issue, the team executed the following command to list objects in the affected directory with debug information:
```
s3-style-cli ls -l --debug /mnt/data/
```
This command provided valuable insights into the state of object replication but did not directly point to the root cause. The output suggested that there were issues with metadata consistency across nodes, hinting at a potential problem with the metadata servers.

Another hypothesis was that the metadata server itself might be experiencing issues, such as being overloaded or incorrectly configured. The team investigated the server's logs and found indications of replication errors but needed to take further steps to understand the root cause of these errors.

As the investigation deepened, the team considered the hypothesis that the issue might be related to the rsync configuration or its execution. They ran `rsync --dry-run -avz /mnt/data/ /mnt/backup/` to test the synchronization process without making changes, which helped to rule out issues with the rsync process itself.

Finally, after ruling out several hypotheses and gathering more evidence, the team focused on the metadata server's configuration and operation. A detailed review of the server logs and configuration files revealed a critical issue that had been overlooked initially: the replication factor was not set correctly for the current workload, leading to inefficient replication and the observed lag.

## Resolution steps
1. Restart metadata server on node 3 with --force-reload flag
2. Set replication_factor to 3 in /etc/distributed-fs.conf
3. Run rsync --dry-run -avz /mnt/data/ /mnt/backup/
4. Verify multipath -ll output for healthy paths to storage devices

## Post-mortem note
Metadata server node 3 failed to replicate data due to misconfigured replication factor. This issue was missed initially because the team focused on more obvious potential causes such as network connectivity and storage device health. The root cause was eventually identified through a thorough review of the metadata server's configuration and operational logs. To prevent similar incidents in the future, the team will implement additional checks on the replication factor configuration during setup and maintenance of the distributed filesystem, and enhance monitoring to include more specific alerts for metadata server issues. Furthermore, the incident highlighted the importance of systematically evaluating all potential causes of an issue, even those that seem less likely at the outset, to ensure timely and effective resolution.
