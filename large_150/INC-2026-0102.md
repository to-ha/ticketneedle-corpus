## Symptom
The incident began with a noticeable increase in query latency, where the p99 latency jumped from 20ms to 4s. This significant degradation in performance was first observed by the monitoring system, which triggered an alert due to the abnormal spike in latency. Further investigation revealed that this issue was not isolated but was instead part of a recurring pattern where application connection pools would report periodic spikes of dropped connections. These spikes would appear every 30-45 minutes and would clear within a minute, suggesting a transient issue that was nonetheless having a profound impact on database performance and reliability.

As the incident unfolded, it became clear that the impact was not limited to just the latency but also affected the overall throughput and stability of the database services. Users began to report errors and timeouts when attempting to perform queries, indicating that the issue was not just a matter of performance degradation but also of service availability. The recurring nature of the spikes in dropped connections suggested a potential issue with resource management or connection handling within the database.

The symptom complex presented a challenge for diagnosis due to its periodic nature and the lack of an obvious single point of failure. It was clear, however, that the read-replica pair was at the center of the issue, given the latency increases and connection drops were most pronounced in interactions with these database components.

Given the specific focus on the read-replica pair and the nature of the symptoms observed, the initial hypothesis was that there might be an issue related to how connections were being managed or a resource constraint affecting these replicas. This led to an exploration of potential bottlenecks or misconfigurations in the database setup that could explain the periodic spikes in latency and connection drops.

## Diagnosis steps and hypotheses
Initial diagnosis steps involved reviewing database logs for any error messages or signs of resource exhaustion. The team also utilized tools like `psql` to query database metrics and assess the current state of connections and queries being executed. One of the key commands used during this phase was:
```
psql -c 'SELECT * FROM pg_stat_activity WHERE state = "idle";'
```
This command helped in understanding the distribution and state of current database connections, which was crucial in formulating hypotheses about potential connection leaks or mismanagement.

Given the periodic nature of the issue, one hypothesis considered was that there might be a batch job or a recurring query that was consuming excessive resources or holding onto connections for too long. Another hypothesis explored was whether there was a configuration issue with the connection pooler that was leading to inefficient use of available connections.

However, as the team dug deeper, it became clear that the root cause was more directly related to how connections were being established and managed, rather than a specific resource bottleneck or periodic query. The evidence from `pg_stat_activity` and other database metrics views pointed towards an issue with connection limits and pooling, suggesting that the current configuration was not adequately supporting the workload.

## Resolution steps
1. Set max connections to 200 in pg_hba.conf
2. Restart database service on read-replica pair
3. Configure connection pooler with 50 idle connections
4. Update batch job to use connection pooler on localhost:5432

## Post-mortem note
Background job opened many short-lived connections that exhausted the per-IP connection limit of 50. This root cause explains the periodic spikes in latency and connection drops observed during the incident. The resolution steps effectively addressed this issue by adjusting connection limits, optimizing the connection pooler configuration, and ensuring that batch jobs utilized the connection pooler efficiently. These changes will help prevent similar incidents in the future by better managing database connections and resources.
