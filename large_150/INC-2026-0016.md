## Symptom
The incident began with reports of service discovery returning stale endpoints after a deploy, indicating a potential issue with the CI runner pool's ability to update and reflect changes in the environment. This symptom was first observed by monitoring tools, which alerted the team to a discrepancy between expected and actual endpoint configurations. Further investigation revealed that this issue was not isolated but rather a persistent problem affecting multiple deployments over a short period. The impact was significant, as it led to failed requests and errors for users attempting to access newly deployed services.

As the team dug deeper, they found that the stale endpoints were not being updated correctly due to a problem with the runner pool's resource allocation. The CI runner pool, responsible for managing and executing deployment tasks, seemed to be struggling with its workload, leading to incomplete or incorrect updates of service discovery records. This observation suggested that the root cause might be related to how resources were being managed or allocated within the CI runner pool.

The symptom complex revolved around the CI runner pool's performance and its direct impact on service discovery and deployment success. It became clear that resolving this incident would require a deep dive into the configuration and operational parameters of the CI runner pool. The team prepared to investigate potential misconfigurations, resource constraints, or other issues that could be affecting the pool's ability to manage deployments effectively.

Initial observations pointed towards an issue that was both critical and complex, requiring a structured approach to diagnose and resolve. Given the potential for widespread impact on deployments and service availability, it was essential to move swiftly while ensuring that any actions taken were well-considered and targeted at the root cause of the problem.

## Diagnosis steps and hypotheses
The diagnosis process involved several hypotheses, each of which was tested and eventually ruled out based on evidence gathered from logs, metrics, and direct observations of the system's behavior. One of the initial hypotheses was that the issue might be related to a recent change in the deployment pipeline, potentially introducing a bug or misconfiguration that affected service discovery updates.

To investigate this hypothesis, the team used various tools, including flag-history to review recent changes and runner-logs to examine the execution details of recent deployments. However, after reviewing these logs and histories, no direct evidence supporting this hypothesis was found, leading the team to consider other potential causes.

Another hypothesis considered was that the registry might be filled with unused images, causing the CI runner pool to spend excessive resources on managing these, thus failing to update service discovery records correctly. To explore this, the team ran the command:
```
kubectl logs -f runner-pool
```
This provided insights into the real-time activity of the runner pool but did not directly point to unused images as the primary cause. The team then considered the possibility of incorrect concurrency settings within the CI runner pool, which could lead to resource exhaustion and failed deployments.

## Resolution steps
1. Restart runner-pool with --max-retries 5
2. Set CI_CONCURRENCY to 10 in /etc/runner/config
3. Run registry-gc --dry-run to identify unused images
4. Remove unused images older than 30 days with registry-gc --delete
5. Update flag-history to reflect new concurrency settings

## Post-mortem note
The CI runner pool exhausted its resources due to incorrect concurrency settings. This incident highlighted the importance of regularly reviewing configuration parameters for critical components like the CI runner pool to prevent similar issues in the future. The swift resolution and the lessons learned from this incident will inform future operational practices and configuration management processes.
