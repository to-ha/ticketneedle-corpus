## Symptom
The incident began with reports of intermittent connectivity issues to the anycast VIP, characterized by BGP session flapping every few minutes. This resulted in brief outages and packet loss for users attempting to access services behind the VIP. Initially, the symptoms were sporadic and seemed to resolve on their own, but as time progressed, the frequency and duration of these outages increased, significantly impacting user experience and business operations. Monitoring systems caught glimpses of these issues through alerts on BGP state changes and increases in packet loss, but the transient nature made it challenging to pinpoint the root cause. The symptoms evolved over the first hour, with outages becoming longer and more frequent, prompting urgent action from the network operations team.

As the incident unfolded, the network operations team noticed that the BGP session flapping was not consistent across all peers, suggesting a potential issue with the anycast VIP's routing configuration or the underlying network infrastructure. The impact on users was significant, with many experiencing failed connections or extremely slow service, leading to a surge in support requests. Despite the monitoring systems' ability to detect some aspects of the issue, such as increased packet loss and BGP session state changes, the dynamic nature of the problem made it difficult to identify a clear pattern or cause.

Further analysis revealed that the anycast VIP's routing configuration might be at the heart of the issue, given the specific nature of the outages and the fact that not all peers were equally affected. This led to a focus on diagnosing potential problems with the anycast VIP setup, including its interaction with the BGP sessions and the impact of any recent changes to the network or routing policies. The user impact was substantial, with business operations severely hindered by the unreliable connectivity, underscoring the need for a swift resolution.

The monitoring systems, while helpful in identifying the existence of a problem, were not fully configured to capture the nuances of the anycast VIP's behavior under stress or to provide detailed insights into the routing decisions being made. This limitation meant that the team had to resort to manual troubleshooting and the use of specialized tools like MTR and iperf3 to gather more detailed information about the network's performance and the anycast VIP's operation.

Given the complexity of the issue and its impact on business operations, it was clear that a thorough and structured approach to diagnosis and resolution was necessary. The team recognized the need to methodically explore different hypotheses, ruling out potential causes through a combination of logical deduction and empirical evidence gathered from the network.

## Diagnosis steps and hypotheses
Initially, the team suspected that the issue might be related to a recent software update on the network devices, potentially introducing a bug that affected BGP stability. To investigate this hypothesis, they reviewed the update logs and compared the current configuration with previous versions. However, after analyzing the update process and verifying that all devices were properly updated and functioning as expected, this hypothesis was ruled out due to lack of evidence linking the update to the BGP flapping.

Another hypothesis considered was that the packet loss was due to congestion on the links, prompting an examination of the traffic volumes and utilization rates on the affected paths. Although some links were found to be operating near capacity, adjustments to traffic routing and prioritization did not alleviate the BGP session flapping, leading the team to conclude that congestion, while a potential contributing factor to packet loss, was not the primary cause of the issue.

The team also explored the possibility of a misconfiguration in the anycast VIP setup, specifically how it interacted with the BGP sessions. To diagnose this, they utilized tools like `mtr` to analyze packet loss and routing decisions:
```
mtr -r --report anycast-vip.example.com
```
This command provided valuable insights into the path packets were taking and helped identify potential issues in the routing configuration that could be contributing to the observed symptoms. However, initial findings did not point to a clear misconfiguration, and further investigation was needed.

A hypothesis that the issue was related to the nft rules and how they prioritized traffic on the primary uplink interface eth0 was also considered. The team reviewed the nft configuration and tested adjustments to these rules to see if they would stabilize the BGP sessions. While this line of inquiry showed promise, it ultimately did not yield the root cause, as the adjustments made did not significantly impact the frequency of the BGP session flapping.

As the diagnosis progressed, the team began to suspect that the root cause might be more deeply related to the anycast VIP's interaction with the network infrastructure, potentially involving aspects of its routing configuration that were not immediately apparent. It was through a combination of these diagnostic steps and hypotheses, along with a deeper dive into the anycast VIP's setup and its implications on BGP stability, that the team finally began to converge on the actual root cause of the issue.

The actual root cause emerged after a thorough review of the anycast VIP routing configuration and its optimization for high traffic volumes. It became clear that the current setup was not adequately prepared to handle the levels of traffic being directed at it, leading to instability in the BGP sessions. This realization guided the team towards the necessary adjustments to resolve the incident.

## Resolution steps
1. Set iperf3 buffer size to 128KB for accurate measurement
2. Run mtr with --report option to analyze packet loss on the anycast VIP
3. Update nft rule 10 to prioritize traffic on the primary uplink interface eth0
4. Configure iperf3 to use UDP protocol with --udp option for better throughput testing

## Post-mortem note
The anycast VIP routing configuration was not properly optimized for high traffic volumes. This oversight led to a complex and challenging incident that impacted business operations significantly. In retrospect, more thorough testing and validation of the anycast VIP setup under various traffic conditions could have prevented this issue. The resolution highlighted the importance of carefully considering the implications of network configurations on service reliability and performance. Future actions will include a review of all anycast VIP configurations to ensure they are optimized for expected traffic volumes and implementing additional monitoring to detect early signs of similar issues.
