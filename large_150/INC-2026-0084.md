## Symptom
The incident began with reports of JWT signature validation failures for a subset of tokens, which initially seemed sporadic but soon became a consistent pattern. Users attempting to access protected resources were met with authentication errors, despite having valid credentials. The issue was first observed by monitoring tools, which alerted on an unusual spike in authentication failures. As the incident evolved, it became clear that the problem was not limited to a specific group of users or resources, but rather seemed to affect a random subset of authentication attempts.

Further investigation revealed that the affected tokens all had one thing in common: they were issued by the SAML federation gateway. This suggested that the issue might be related to the gateway's configuration or operation. The user impact was significant, as many critical services relied on the SAML gateway for authentication. Business operations were severely impaired, with many users unable to access necessary resources.

Monitoring tools caught the initial spike in authentication failures, but the root cause of the issue was not immediately apparent. The metrics platform showed a clear increase in error rates, but the logs did not provide an obvious explanation. As the incident progressed, the team began to suspect that the issue might be related to the SAML gateway's certificate configuration.

The SAML gateway is a critical component of the authentication infrastructure, responsible for verifying user identities and issuing tokens. Any issue with the gateway can have far-reaching consequences, affecting multiple services and users. In this case, the failure of the JWT signature validation was a clear indicator that something was amiss with the gateway's operation.

As the team delved deeper into the issue, they began to explore various diagnostic avenues. They used tools like session-trace and ldapsearch to gather more information about the affected tokens and the SAML gateway's configuration. The goal was to identify the root cause of the issue and develop a plan to resolve it.

The incident highlighted the importance of robust monitoring and logging capabilities. If not for the metrics platform's alerts, the issue might have gone unnoticed for an extended period, causing even more disruption to business operations. The team's ability to quickly respond to the incident and gather diagnostic data was crucial in resolving the issue.

## Diagnosis steps and hypotheses
Initially, the team suspected that the issue might be related to a misconfiguration of the SAML gateway's certificate settings. They attempted to verify the certificate chain and ensure that all necessary certificates were properly installed. However, this hypothesis was ruled out after reviewing the certificate configuration and finding no obvious issues.

Another hypothesis was that the problem might be caused by a mismatch between the SAML gateway's clock and the identity provider's clock, leading to token validation failures. The team checked the clock settings on both sides but found no significant discrepancies, ruling out this hypothesis as well.

The team also considered the possibility that the issue was related to a recent software update, which might have introduced a bug or changed the behavior of the SAML gateway. However, after reviewing the release notes and testing the update in a staging environment, they found no evidence to support this hypothesis.

As part of their diagnostic efforts, the team used various tools to gather more information. For example, they used the following command to query the LDAP directory:
```
ldapsearch -x -D cn=admin,dc=example,dc=com -W -b dc=example,dc=com
```
This helped them verify that user attributes were correctly configured and matched the expected values.

Despite these efforts, the root cause of the issue remained elusive. It wasn't until the team took a closer look at the SAML gateway's configuration files that they began to suspect the true cause of the problem. The discovery of an outdated certificate in one of the configuration files ultimately led them to the solution.

## Resolution steps
1. Update idp-metadata.xml at /etc/saml/config to reflect new certificate
2. Set verify_cert to true in /etc/saml/settings.json
3. Restart saml-gateway service with --debug flag
4. Run pam-test with --saml2 option to verify authentication
5. Use ldapsearch to ensure user attributes match SAML claims

## Post-mortem note
The SAML federation gateway failed to verify the identity provider's certificate. This issue was initially missed due to a lack of clear monitoring alerts and logging data pointing directly to the certificate verification process. In hindsight, the team should have more closely examined the SAML gateway's configuration files and certificate settings earlier in the incident. To prevent similar incidents in the future, the team will implement additional monitoring and logging for the SAML gateway, focusing on certificate-related events and errors. This will enable them to detect and respond to certificate issues more quickly, reducing the impact of such incidents on business operations.
