## Symptom
The incident began with reports of asymmetric routing after a recent config push to the recursive DNS resolver cluster. This led to noticeable delays in name resolution for a subset of users, causing frustration and impacting productivity. Initial observations suggested that the issue was intermittent, affecting different users at different times, but always tracing back to the DNS resolution process. Further investigation revealed that the delays were not uniform, with some users experiencing significant latency while others faced outright failures in resolving certain domain names.

As the incident unfolded, it became clear that the asymmetric routing was not just a simple configuration issue but was deeply intertwined with how the recursive DNS resolver cluster handled queries under load. The team observed that during periods of high query volume, the cluster's performance would degrade significantly, leading to the asymmetric routing issues. This pattern hinted at a potential capacity or resource limitation within the cluster that was being exacerbated by the recent config push.

Monitoring tools picked up on the anomaly, highlighting an increase in DNS resolution times and error rates for affected users. The impact was substantial, with several critical services relying on timely and accurate DNS resolution to function correctly. Given the dependency of these services on DNS, the incident quickly escalated, requiring prompt attention from the network operations team.

The observable pattern of asymmetric routing after a config push served as the primary symptom guiding the initial diagnostic efforts. This focus helped in narrowing down the potential causes and directed the team towards investigating the recursive DNS resolver cluster's behavior under similar conditions. Tools like `tcpdump`, `MTR`, and `nslookup` were instrumental in this phase, providing insights into the DNS traffic and routing decisions being made by the cluster.

## Diagnosis steps and hypotheses
Initial hypotheses centered around potential misconfigurations introduced by the recent push or unforeseen interactions between the new settings and existing network conditions. The team considered the possibility of DNS server overload due to increased query volume, which could lead to delayed or incorrect responses, thus causing asymmetric routing. To explore this hypothesis, the team used `tcpdump` to capture DNS traffic, as shown in the following command:
```
tcpdump -i any port 53
```
This allowed them to inspect the DNS queries and responses in real-time, looking for signs of overload or misdirected queries.

Another hypothesis involved issues with the DNS cache, potentially causing stale or incorrect entries to be served, leading to routing anomalies. However, upon reviewing the cache settings and query logs, no evidence supported this theory, ruling it out as a primary cause.

The team also explored the possibility of external factors, such as changes in upstream DNS providers or internet routing table updates, influencing the recursive DNS resolver cluster's performance. However, checks with external providers and reviews of routing tables did not reveal any recent changes that could correlate with the timing of the incident.

## Resolution steps
1. Restart dns-resolver service on node 10.0.1.5
2. Set max_cache_size to 10000 in /etc/dns/config.yaml
3. Increase timeout to 5s for recursive queries
4. Check for errors in /var/log/dns/query.log

## Post-mortem note
The recursive DNS resolver cluster experienced cache overflow due to high query volume. This root cause was identified after carefully analyzing the cluster's behavior under load and the impact of recent configuration changes. The resolution steps, including restarting the dns-resolver service, adjusting cache settings, and increasing timeouts, effectively addressed the issue, restoring normal operation to the recursive DNS resolver cluster.
