## Symptom
The CI pipelines began to stall consistently on the same stage across all repositories, indicating a potential issue with the shared resources or dependencies. This symptom was first observed by the development team around 06:00 UTC and was reported immediately, as it affected multiple critical projects. The stalling of pipelines suggested that there might be a bottleneck or a limitation in one of the shared components, such as the message broker, which is crucial for the communication between different stages of the pipeline.

Further investigation revealed that the error messages in the pipeline logs hinted at a connection issue, possibly related to the message broker's capacity to handle concurrent connections. The development team attempted to rerun some of the failed pipelines to see if the issue was transient, but the stalling persisted, indicating a more systemic problem. Given the critical nature of the CI pipelines for the development workflow, this incident had a significant impact on the team's productivity and the overall project timelines.

The observability tools showed an increase in connection timeouts and errors related to the message broker around the time the pipelines started stalling. This correlation strengthened the hypothesis that the issue was indeed related to the message broker's performance or configuration. The team responsible for the platform infrastructure was notified, and they began to investigate the matter further, focusing on the health and configuration of the shared message broker.

## Diagnosis steps and hypotheses
Initial hypotheses included the possibility of a network issue affecting the communication between the pipeline stages and the message broker, or a recent configuration change in the broker that might have introduced the bottleneck. The team used tools like `flag-history` to check for any recent flag changes that could impact the message broker's behavior and `registry-gc` to ensure the registry was healthy and not causing issues due to outdated or unnecessary entries.

To further diagnose the issue, the team executed the following command to check the connections:
```
broker-admin --check-connections --broker-address=:9092
```
This command provided insight into the current connection state of the broker, helping to identify if the issue was indeed related to the number of connections or their management. However, after running this command and analyzing its output, it became clear that the connection limit was being exceeded due to an incorrect configuration setting.

## Resolution steps
1. Restart broker with --max-connections=500
2. Set registry_gc_interval to 30m in /etc/broker/config.yaml
3. Run registry-gc with --dry-run=false --broker-address=:9092
4. Verify flag-history for --max-connections changes in the last hour
5. Adjust broker-admin --connection-timeout to 10s

## Post-mortem note
The shared message broker exceeded its connection limit due to incorrect configuration. This incident highlighted the importance of regularly reviewing configuration settings for critical components like the message broker and ensuring that monitoring and alerts are in place to quickly detect when such limits are being approached or exceeded.
