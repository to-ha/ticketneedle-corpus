## Symptom
The network team noticed asymmetric routing after a recent config push, which caused packets to take a different path than expected. This resulted in increased latency and packet loss for some users. The issue was first identified by monitoring tools, which alerted on unusual traffic patterns.

Further investigation revealed that the asymmetric routing was causing issues with BGP peering sessions, leading to flapping routes and instability in the network. The team observed that some prefixes were being advertised multiple times, causing confusion among the routers.

The symptom complex was centered around the BGP peering session, which seemed to be the root cause of the issue. The team focused on troubleshooting this specific area to identify the problem.

## Escalation timeline
- 07:57 — L1 Network operations: Linnea began reviewing the monitoring alerts and notified the L2 team.
- 08:24 — L2 Network engineer: Nina started investigating the BGP peering sessions and checking for any configuration issues.

## Diagnosis steps and hypotheses
The team considered several hypotheses, including a possible misconfiguration of the BGP peers or an issue with the underlying network infrastructure. To troubleshoot the issue, they used tools like `tcpdump` and Wireshark to capture and analyze traffic.
```
tcpdump -i eth0 port 179
```
This command helped them identify the issue with the BGP peering session and narrow down the possible causes.

## Resolution steps
1. Restart BGP service on router 10.1.1.1
2. Set hold time to 30 seconds on peer group 65001
3. Increase keepalive interval to 10 seconds on all BGP sessions

## Post-mortem note
The BGP peering session timed out due to mismatched hold times. This issue highlights the importance of ensuring consistent configuration across all BGP peers to prevent such problems in the future.
