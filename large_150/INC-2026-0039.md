## Symptom
The incident began with nodes entering a NotReady state during a rolling kernel upgrade. Initially, this was observed in a single node, but as the upgrade progressed, more nodes started to exhibit the same behavior. The monitoring system caught this anomaly and alerted the team. Further investigation revealed that the issue was not directly related to the kernel upgrade itself but seemed to be triggered by it. As more nodes became NotReady, the cluster's overall health began to deteriorate, impacting the availability of services running on the cluster.

The first sign of trouble came when the monitoring system alerted on a spike in node restarts and an increase in the number of NotReady nodes. Upon closer inspection, it became clear that the etcd cluster was experiencing issues, which in turn were causing the nodes to fail health checks and enter the NotReady state. The team noticed that the problem seemed to coincide with periods of high network latency, suggesting a potential link between the two.

As the incident evolved, the team observed that the NotReady nodes would sometimes recover on their own, only to fall back into the NotReady state shortly after. This behavior suggested an intermittent issue, possibly related to communication within the cluster or external factors affecting the nodes. The user and business impact was significant, as services hosted on the affected nodes became unavailable, leading to a degradation in overall system performance and user experience.

The monitoring system played a crucial role in identifying the issue early on, but it missed capturing the root cause of the problem. The initial alerts focused on the symptoms (nodes entering NotReady state) rather than the underlying cause, which led to a delay in pinpointing the exact issue. Tools like k9s and helm were instrumental in diagnosing the state of the nodes and the cluster, but it was the deeper investigation into etcd and network performance that ultimately revealed the source of the trouble.

Given the complexity of the issue and its impact on the cluster, the team decided to escalate the incident to ensure timely resolution and minimize downtime. The escalation process involved several levels of support, each contributing to the diagnosis and eventual resolution of the incident.

## Diagnosis steps and hypotheses
Initially, the team suspected that the issue might be related to the rolling kernel upgrade itself, possibly due to incompatibilities or unforeseen side effects. However, after reviewing the upgrade logs and comparing them across affected and unaffected nodes, this hypothesis was ruled out due to lack of evidence directly linking the upgrade to the NotReady state.

Another hypothesis considered was that there might be a resource constraint (e.g., CPU, memory) causing nodes to fail health checks. The team used k9s to inspect node resources but found no indication of widespread resource shortages that could account for the observed behavior.

The possibility of a network issue affecting etcd communication was also explored. The team ran diagnostics on network connectivity and latency, which showed some periods of high latency but did not conclusively prove this as the root cause.

Further investigation involved checking the etcd cluster's health directly. The team used the following command to list etcd members with a specific timeout to assess cluster connectivity:
```
etcdctl member list --cluster=dial-timeout=5s
```
This step provided valuable insights into the etcd cluster's status but did not immediately reveal the root cause.

Another hypothesis was that the etcd data directory might be corrupted or improperly configured, leading to issues with node registration and health checks. However, upon inspection, the etcd data directory appeared to be correctly configured and intact.

It wasn't until the team delved deeper into etcd's behavior under network stress and its quorum management that they began to suspect the actual root cause. The etcd cluster's design and its dependency on stable, low-latency network conditions for maintaining quorum pointed towards a potential issue with how the cluster was handling recent network fluctuations.

The actual root cause emerged after a thorough review of etcd logs and network performance metrics, which indicated that the etcd cluster had indeed experienced a quorum loss due to transient network issues. This realization led the team to focus on stabilizing the etcd cluster and adjusting its configuration to better tolerate such events in the future.

## Resolution steps
1. Restart etcd cluster with 3 members
2. Set etcd election timeout to 3000ms
3. Verify etcd data directory at /var/lib/etcd
4. Run etcdctl member list to check cluster health

## Post-mortem note
The etcd cluster experienced a quorum loss due to network latency. This incident highlights the importance of closely monitoring network conditions and their potential impact on critical cluster components like etcd. The root cause was initially missed because the focus was on node-level issues rather than the broader cluster health, particularly etcd's sensitivity to network latency. Follow-up actions include reviewing and adjusting etcd settings for better resilience against transient network issues and enhancing monitoring to catch similar problems earlier. Additionally, the team will conduct a review of the escalation process to identify areas for improvement in communication and issue resolution timelines.
