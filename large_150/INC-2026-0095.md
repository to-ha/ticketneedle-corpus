## Symptom
The incident was characterized by an MTU black hole between two sites, resulting in intermittent packet loss and latency spikes. Users reported difficulty accessing resources hosted at the remote site, with some experiencing complete loss of connectivity. Initial observations suggested that the issue was related to the MPLS L3VPN configuration, as packets were being dropped due to excessive fragmentation.

Further investigation revealed that the black hole was caused by a mismatch in MTU sizes between the two sites, leading to packet fragmentation and subsequent drops. This was evident from the high rate of ICMP "fragmentation needed" errors observed in the network logs. The symptom complex was relatively straightforward, with a clear correlation between the MTU mismatch and the packet loss.

The MTU black hole symptom was first detected by the monitoring system, which alerted the network operations team to the issue. The team then used tools like tcpdump and Wireshark to capture and analyze network traffic, confirming the presence of excessive fragmentation and packet drops.

## Diagnosis steps and hypotheses
Initial hypotheses focused on potential issues with the BGP configuration or VPN route propagation. However, these were ruled out after verifying the BGP sessions and VPN routes using `dig` commands. The team then turned their attention to the MPLS L3VPN configuration, suspecting a possible issue with label allocation or MTU sizing. To investigate this further, the team used the following command to capture BGP traffic:
```
tcpdump -i any -n -vv -s 0 -c 100 port 179
```
This captured data helped to rule out BGP-related issues and narrowed the focus to the MPLS L3VPN configuration.

## Resolution steps
1. Set MPLS label range to 1000-2000
2. Restart L3VPN service on router 10.1.1.1
3. Configure BGP timer to 30 seconds on router 10.1.1.2
4. Verify VPN route propagation with dig +short 10.2.2.2

## Post-mortem note
MPLS label range was exhausted due to misconfigured L3VPN. This root cause was identified through a combination of network traffic analysis and configuration reviews, highlighting the importance of regular audits and monitoring of critical network components.
