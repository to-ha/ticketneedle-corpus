## Symptom
The incident began with reports of pods stuck in CrashLoopBackOff after a node reboot. This symptom was consistently observed across multiple deployments, suggesting a systemic issue rather than an application-specific problem. Further investigation revealed that the affected pods were dependent on a service that relied on etcd for configuration and coordination.

As the situation unfolded, it became clear that the etcd cluster was not functioning correctly, leading to cascading failures in dependent services. The symptom of pods in CrashLoopBackOff was a direct result of the etcd issue, as these pods were unable to properly initialize due to the lack of required configuration data.

Monitoring tools indicated a sharp increase in errors related to etcd connectivity around the time of the node reboot, reinforcing the hypothesis that the etcd cluster was at the root of the problem.

## Diagnosis steps and hypotheses
Initial hypotheses centered around potential misconfigurations or resource issues within the Kubernetes cluster. However, as more data was collected, attention turned to the etcd cluster itself. A key command used in diagnosing the issue was:
```
etcdctl member list --cluster
```
This helped in understanding the state of the etcd members and identifying any potential network partitions or member failures. The evidence pointed towards an etcd cluster issue, specifically related to quorum and network connectivity.

## Resolution steps
1. Restart etcd member on node 3
2. Set etcd quorum threshold to 2
3. Increase heartbeat interval to 500ms
4. Run etcdctl alarm list to verify

## Post-mortem note
The etcd cluster lost quorum due to a network partition. This incident highlights the importance of monitoring etcd cluster health and taking proactive measures to prevent such network issues, including regular checks on etcd member connectivity and adjusting cluster settings as necessary to maintain quorum.
