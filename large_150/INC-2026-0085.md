## Symptom
The incident began with reports of an MTU black hole between two sites, causing packet loss and latency issues for users. This symptom was first observed by the network monitoring team, who noticed a significant increase in packet loss and latency on the links between the two sites. As the issue evolved, users started reporting difficulties accessing resources and applications hosted on the other site, with some experiencing complete loss of connectivity. The monitoring system caught the initial spike in packet loss, but the root cause of the issue was not immediately apparent. Further investigation was needed to determine the cause of the MTU black hole and the resulting packet loss.

The network operations team was alerted to the issue and began investigating the cause. They started by reviewing the network configuration and checking for any recent changes that could have caused the issue. However, no recent changes were found, and the team had to dig deeper to identify the root cause. The team used tools like `nslookup` and `MTR` to troubleshoot the issue and gather more information about the packet loss and latency.

As the investigation continued, it became clear that the issue was related to the BGP peering session between the two sites. The team noticed that the BGP session was not establishing correctly, which was causing the packet loss and latency issues. However, the exact cause of the BGP session failure was still unknown, and further diagnosis was needed.

The user impact of the incident was significant, with many users experiencing difficulties accessing critical resources and applications. The business impact was also substantial, with the incident affecting multiple teams and departments. The incident highlighted the importance of having a robust and reliable network infrastructure in place to support business operations.

The monitoring system played a crucial role in detecting the issue, but it did not provide enough information to identify the root cause. The team had to rely on additional tools and troubleshooting techniques to gather more information and diagnose the issue. In hindsight, the monitoring system could have been configured to provide more detailed information about the packet loss and latency issues, which could have helped identify the root cause more quickly.

## Diagnosis steps and hypotheses
The team considered several hypotheses to explain the MTU black hole and packet loss issues. One initial hypothesis was that there was a misconfiguration in the network devices, which was causing the packet loss and latency issues. The team checked the device configurations, but no issues were found. Another hypothesis was that there was a problem with the physical links between the sites, which was causing the packet loss and latency issues. The team used `iperf3` to test the links, but no issues were found.

The team also considered the possibility of a routing loop or a BGP configuration issue. They used `nslookup` to check the DNS resolution and `MTR` to trace the packet path. However, these tools did not provide enough information to identify the root cause. The team then decided to investigate the BGP peering session in more detail.

One of the diagnostic steps taken was to use the following command to troubleshoot the issue:
```
nslookup -type=txt 10.2.2.2
```
This command provided some useful information about the DNS resolution, but it did not point to the root cause of the issue. The team had to continue investigating and gathering more information to identify the root cause.

As the team continued to investigate, they considered several other hypotheses, including a possible issue with the firewall configuration or a problem with the network protocols. However, none of these hypotheses were supported by the evidence, and the team had to continue searching for the root cause. It wasn't until they investigated the BGP peering session in more detail that they began to understand the root cause of the issue.

The actual root cause of the issue emerged after a thorough investigation of the BGP peering session. The team discovered that the BGP session was not establishing correctly due to a mismatched hold timer, which was causing the packet loss and latency issues. This discovery led to the identification of the root cause and the development of a plan to resolve the issue.

## Resolution steps
1. Restart BGP service on router 10.1.1.1
2. Set hold time to 30 seconds in bgp.conf
3. Adjust keepalive interval to 10 seconds
4. Verify peer IP 10.2.2.2 is reachable via MTR
