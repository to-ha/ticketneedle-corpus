## Symptom
The incident began with reports of directory sync lag causing stale group membership, which led to authentication issues for a subset of users. Initially, the symptoms were sporadic and only affected a small number of users, but as time progressed, the issue became more widespread and persistent. The monitoring system caught the anomaly in the authentication logs, showing an increasing number of failed login attempts due to outdated group membership information. Further investigation revealed that the SAML federation gateway was not updating user group memberships in a timely manner, resulting in users being denied access to resources they should have been able to access.

As the incident evolved, more users began to experience authentication issues, and the support team started receiving a higher volume of calls and tickets related to the problem. The users impacted were primarily those who relied on group membership to access specific resources and applications. The business impact was significant, as the inability to access necessary resources hindered productivity and disrupted critical business operations. The monitoring system's alerts and logs provided valuable insights into the issue, helping the team to focus their troubleshooting efforts on the SAML federation gateway and directory sync processes.

The symptom complex was characterized by a consistent pattern of failed authentication attempts due to stale group membership information. This pattern was observed across multiple users and applications, pointing to a systemic issue rather than an isolated problem. The team's initial hypothesis was that the directory sync process was malfunctioning or not running frequently enough, causing the group membership information to become outdated. However, as they dug deeper, they realized that the issue was more complex and involved the interaction between the directory sync process and the SAML federation gateway.

The monitoring system played a crucial role in detecting the anomaly and alerting the team to the issue. The logs and metrics provided by the monitoring system helped the team to understand the scope and impact of the problem, guiding their troubleshooting efforts and enabling them to focus on the most critical areas. The incident highlighted the importance of timely and accurate directory sync and the need for robust monitoring and alerting mechanisms to detect and respond to issues quickly.

The team's investigation also revealed that the issue was not limited to a specific application or resource but was rather a systemic problem affecting multiple areas of the business. This realization underscored the need for a comprehensive solution that addressed the root cause of the issue, rather than just treating the symptoms. As the team worked to resolve the incident, they remained focused on identifying and addressing the underlying cause, ensuring that the solution would be durable and effective in preventing similar issues in the future.

## Diagnosis steps and hypotheses
The team considered several hypotheses as they worked to diagnose the issue. One initial suspicion was that the directory sync process was not running correctly, causing the group membership information to become stale. To investigate this, they checked the directory sync logs and verified that the process was running as scheduled. However, the logs showed that the process was completing successfully, which ruled out this hypothesis.

Another hypothesis was that the SAML federation gateway was not properly configured, leading to authentication issues. The team reviewed the gateway's configuration and found that it was set up correctly, according to the documentation and previous troubleshooting guides. This led them to suspect that the issue might be related to the interaction between the directory sync process and the SAML federation gateway.

To further diagnose the issue, the team used tools like pam-test and session-trace to analyze the authentication flow and identify any potential bottlenecks or misconfigurations. They also utilized a JWT decoder equivalent to jwt.io to inspect the tokens being issued and verify their contents. One key command used in the diagnosis was:
```
jwt.io decode https://sp.example.org/assertion
```
This helped them understand the structure and content of the tokens, which in turn provided insights into the authentication process and potential issues with the SAML federation gateway.

As the team continued to investigate, they considered the hypothesis that the issue was due to a misconfiguration of the SAML federation gateway itself, rather than the directory sync process. They reviewed the gateway's settings and compared them against the expected configuration, looking for any discrepancies that could explain the authentication issues.

After ruling out several hypotheses and gathering more evidence, the team finally began to suspect that the root cause of the issue was related to the SAML federation gateway's configuration, specifically how it was handling group membership information. This realization came after a thorough analysis of the logs, metrics, and configuration data, which collectively pointed to a misconfiguration as the likely cause.

## Resolution steps
1. Restart SAML gateway service with debug log level 7
2. Set idp_ttl to 3600 in /etc/saml/idp.conf
3. Update entity_id in /etc/saml/sp.conf to match IdP metadata
4. Run pam-test with --saml2 option to verify authentication flow
5. Verify JWT issuer claim matches expected value https://idp.example.org

## Post-mortem note
The SAML federation gateway was misconfigured with an incorrect entity_id. This root cause was initially missed because the team's focus was on the directory sync process and its potential impact on group membership information. However, as they delved deeper into the issue, analyzing logs, metrics, and configuration settings, they eventually identified the misconfiguration of the SAML federation gateway as the primary cause of the authentication issues. Follow-up actions include reviewing and updating documentation to ensure that the correct entity_id is used in all configurations, as well as enhancing monitoring and alerting to quickly detect similar misconfigurations in the future. The incident highlighted the importance of thorough troubleshooting and the need for a systematic approach to identifying and addressing complex technical issues.
