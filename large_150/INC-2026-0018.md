## Symptom
Users were experiencing intermittent 401 errors after successfully logging in, indicating an issue with the authentication process. This symptom was observed across various user sessions, suggesting a problem with the SAML federation gateway. Further investigation revealed that outbound HTTPS calls to a partner endpoint were failing at the TLS handshake stage, resulting in a generic 'handshake failure' error. The behavior was bimodal, with half the requests succeeding and half failing, making it challenging to identify the root cause.

The failed TLS handshakes were not consistently reproducible, adding complexity to the diagnosis. However, the pattern of successful and failed requests suggested an issue related to the trust store or certificate validation.

## Diagnosis steps and hypotheses
Initial hypotheses focused on potential misconfigurations in the SAML gateway or issues with the partner's endpoint. Diagnostic efforts included using `pam-test` to validate authentication configurations and a JWT decoder equivalent to inspect token validity. A key diagnostic step involved running the command:
```
openssl s_client -connect partner.example.com:443 -showcerts
```
This command helped identify a discrepancy in the expected and actual certificates presented by the partner's endpoint, hinting at a certificate trust issue.

## Resolution steps
1. Download new intermediate CA bundle from partner portal https://partner.example.com/ca.bundle
2. Add CA bundle to system trust store at /etc/ssl/certs/truststore.pem
3. Reload connection pool with updated trust store using systemctl restart saml-gateway
4. Verify TLS handshake success with openssl s_client -connect partner.example.com:443

## Post-mortem note
Partner rotated their server certificate to use an intermediate CA not in our trust store. This change was not communicated to us, leading to the unexpected TLS handshake failures. Implementing a regular review of partner certificates and ensuring our trust store is up-to-date will help prevent similar incidents in the future.
