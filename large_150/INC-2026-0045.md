## Symptom
The incident began with reports of an MTU black hole between two sites, resulting in intermittent packet loss and latency issues. Users noticed that large file transfers would often fail or timeout, while smaller requests seemed to work fine. As the issue persisted, it became clear that the problem was not limited to file transfers, but also affected other services relying on stable network connectivity. The network monitoring system caught the anomaly, triggering alerts for unusual packet loss patterns. However, the initial symptoms were somewhat misleading, as they suggested a simple MTU mismatch rather than a deeper issue with the BGP peering session.

Further investigation using `iperf3` and `nslookup` revealed that the problem was indeed related to the MTU, but the root cause was more complex. The symptoms evolved over time, with some users experiencing complete loss of connectivity, while others saw sporadic errors. This variability made it challenging to pinpoint the exact cause, as different diagnostic tools yielded different results. For instance, `tcpdump` captures showed inconsistent packet sizes, which hinted at a possible issue with path MTU discovery, but this was later ruled out.

The user and business impact of the incident was significant, as it affected critical services used by multiple teams across the organization. The incident highlighted the importance of robust network monitoring and the need for swift incident response to minimize downtime. As the incident unfolded, the team relied heavily on `tcpdump` and `iperf3` to gather more information about the packet loss and latency issues.

The network operations team worked closely with the service owners to understand the scope of the impact and to identify potential causes. They used a combination of metrics from the monitoring platform and logs from the network devices to piece together the events leading up to the incident. Although the initial analysis suggested a straightforward MTU issue, the team recognized that the symptoms could be indicative of a more intricate problem, possibly related to the BGP peering session.

As the team dug deeper, they began to suspect that the issue might not be solely related to MTU sizing but could involve the BGP configuration itself. This realization marked a turning point in the investigation, as it shifted the focus from simple network configuration issues to more complex protocol-related problems. The team decided to escalate the incident to ensure that all necessary expertise was brought to bear on the problem.

## Diagnosis steps and hypotheses
The team considered several hypotheses during the diagnosis phase. Initially, they suspected a simple MTU mismatch between the two sites, which would have been straightforward to resolve. However, as they delved deeper into the issue, using tools like `iperf3` and `nslookup`, they realized that the problem was more complex. They then hypothesized that the issue could be related to path MTU discovery, but captures from `tcpdump` did not support this theory.

Another hypothesis involved potential issues with the BGP session establishment or maintenance, possibly due to incorrect configuration or external factors affecting the session. To investigate this, the team used the following command to capture BGP traffic:
```
tcpdump -i any port 179 -n -vv -s 0 -c 100
```
This command provided valuable insights into the BGP communication but did not immediately reveal the root cause.

The team also explored the possibility of a network device malfunction or a software bug affecting the BGP protocol implementation. They checked logs and metrics from the devices involved but found no evidence to support this hypothesis.

As the investigation progressed, the team considered the possibility of an incorrect holdtime configuration in the BGP peering session. This hypothesis seemed plausible given the intermittent nature of the packet loss and the fact that the issue seemed to be related to the BGP session. However, it wasn't until they reviewed the BGP configuration settings that they found the actual root cause.

The root cause emerged after a thorough review of the BGP configuration and the analysis of the `tcpdump` captures. It became clear that the issue was indeed related to the BGP peering session, specifically due to an incorrect configuration that led to session timeouts.

## Resolution steps
1. Restart BGP daemon with flag -r to reload config
2. Set bgp holdtime to 30 and keepalive interval to 10
3. Increase tcp buffer size to 1048576 for BGP session
4. Verify mtu size of 1500 on both peering interfaces

## Post-mortem note
The BGP peering session timed out due to incorrect holdtime configuration. This root cause was initially missed because the symptoms suggested a simpler MTU-related issue. The incident highlights the importance of thorough diagnostic work and the value of escalating incidents to ensure that the right expertise is applied to complex problems. Following this incident, the team will review the BGP configuration across all peering sessions to prevent similar issues from arising in the future. Additionally, the network monitoring system will be enhanced to provide more detailed alerts for BGP session issues, allowing for quicker identification and resolution of such problems.
