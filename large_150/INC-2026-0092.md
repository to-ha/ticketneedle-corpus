## Symptom
The filesystem metadata server experienced intermittent failures, resulting in a failover every few hours. This pattern was consistently observed over the course of several days, with each failure occurring without an immediately apparent cause. Monitoring systems detected these failures through increased error rates and latency spikes in metadata access requests.

Further investigation revealed that the failovers were often preceded by brief periods of elevated disk utilization and slower than usual response times from the storage subsystem. These symptoms suggested a potential issue with the underlying storage infrastructure, possibly related to the configuration or performance of the SAN multipath driver.

Given the specific nature of the failures and their impact on the filesystem metadata server, it was crucial to focus on the storage layer for diagnostic efforts. Tools like `smartctl` for disk health checks, `iostat` for monitoring disk performance, and `multipath -ll` for examining the multipath configuration were considered essential for troubleshooting.

## Diagnosis steps and hypotheses
Initial hypotheses centered around issues with disk health or network connectivity affecting the storage subsystem. However, upon further examination using `iostat` and `smartctl`, no significant problems were found with the disks themselves or the network links. The focus then shifted to the SAN multipath driver, considering its critical role in managing storage paths.

To better understand the multipath configuration and its behavior, the command ```multipath -ll``` was executed. This provided detailed information about the current multipath setup, including the paths' status and configuration parameters. The output helped in identifying a potential misconfiguration that could lead to the observed failovers.

## Resolution steps
1. Restart multipathd service with debug flag
2. Set multipath.conf max_polling_interval to 10
3. Update /etc/multipath.conf alias to match WWID 3600508e0000000000e13955cc3756d70
4. Reload multipath maps with multipath -r

## Post-mortem note
The SAN multipath driver failed to properly configure paths due to outdated configuration. This root cause, once addressed through the resolution steps, ensured the stability of the filesystem metadata server and prevented further failovers. The incident highlighted the importance of regularly reviewing and updating storage configurations to prevent such issues.
