## Symptom
The incident began with reports of BGP session flapping every few minutes, affecting the anycast VIP routing. This flapping caused intermittent connectivity issues for users relying on the anycast VIP for access to critical network services. The symptoms were first observed by monitoring tools, which alerted the network operations team to the issue. Further investigation revealed that the BGP sessions would establish and then quickly terminate, repeating this cycle.

As the incident progressed, it became clear that the impact was not limited to a single user group or geographic location, suggesting a more widespread issue with the anycast VIP configuration. Network performance metrics showed spikes in packet loss and latency during the periods of BGP session flapping, further indicating the severity of the problem. The operations team attempted to isolate the issue by checking for any recent changes to the network configuration but found none that would directly explain the BGP session behavior.

Given the nature of the symptom, the initial hypothesis was that there might be an issue with the anycast VIP routing table or the BGP peering configuration. However, without direct access to the routing tables and BGP session details, it was challenging to pinpoint the exact cause. The team decided to escalate the issue to gather more expertise and resources to diagnose and resolve the problem.

The flapping BGP sessions and resulting network instability made it crucial to find a swift resolution to prevent further disruption to services. The anycast VIP plays a critical role in maintaining high availability and redundancy for key network services, and its malfunction was having a significant impact on operational continuity.

## Diagnosis steps and hypotheses
Initial diagnosis steps involved checking the health of the network devices involved in the anycast VIP setup and verifying the BGP peering configurations. The team considered the hypothesis that the issue might be related to a recent software update on the network devices, but after reviewing the changelogs and release notes, no relevant changes were found that would directly impact BGP session stability.

Another hypothesis was that there could be an issue with the anycast VIP routing table itself, potentially due to incorrect or missing routes. To investigate this, the team used `tcpdump` to capture network traffic and analyze the BGP updates:
```
tcpdump -i any -n -vv -s 0 -c 100 port 80
```
However, the captures did not reveal any obvious issues with the routing updates or advertisements.

The team also explored the possibility of a hardware failure or a physical layer issue affecting the network connectivity, but checks on the device statuses and physical connections did not indicate any problems.

## Resolution steps
1. Set anycast route metric to 10
2. Restart nft service with --reload flag
3. Run iperf3 with -p 5201 option for throughput test
4. Increase tcpdump buffer size to 100000
5. Apply updated nft ruleset from /etc/nftables.conf

## Post-mortem note
Anycast VIP routing table was misconfigured with incorrect metric values. This misconfiguration led to the BGP session flapping observed during the incident, as the routing decisions were being made based on incorrect assumptions about the network topology and path costs. The correction of the anycast route metric and the application of the updated nft ruleset resolved the issue by ensuring that the routing tables accurately reflected the intended network configuration.
