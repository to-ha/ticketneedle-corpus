## Symptom
The tracing collector, a critical component of our monitoring infrastructure, began to exhibit unusual behavior. Users reported that critical spans were missing from the trace sampler output, indicating a potential misconfiguration of the trace sampler rate. This symptom was first observed by the on-call team during a routine check of the monitoring dashboards. Further investigation revealed that the issue was not limited to a specific service or endpoint, but rather seemed to be a widespread problem affecting multiple components of the system.

As the issue persisted, it became clear that the missing spans were not only affecting the accuracy of our monitoring data but also hindering the ability of the engineering team to debug and troubleshoot issues. The tracing collector's inability to capture complete trace data meant that engineers were unable to identify performance bottlenecks or diagnose problems effectively. This had a direct impact on the team's productivity and the overall quality of service.

The symptom complex was characterized by an inconsistent trace sampler rate, with some spans being captured correctly while others were missing. This inconsistency made it challenging to pinpoint the root cause of the issue, as it did not appear to be related to any specific component or service. The on-call team attempted to reproduce the issue using various test cases, but the problem seemed to be intermittent and difficult to replicate.

Initial attempts to address the issue focused on adjusting the trace sampler rate and verifying the configuration of the tracing collector. However, these efforts did not yield the expected results, and the problem persisted. It became clear that a more in-depth investigation was required to identify the underlying cause of the issue.

## Diagnosis steps and hypotheses
The diagnosis process involved considering several hypotheses and attempting to rule them out through a combination of log analysis, metric inspection, and targeted testing. One of the initial hypotheses was that the issue might be related to a resource constraint, such as CPU or memory usage, affecting the tracing collector's ability to capture and process spans.

To investigate this hypothesis, the team used the following command to check for any out-of-memory errors in the tracing collector's log:
```
log-tail -f /var/log/tracing-collector.log | grep OOM
```
This command did not reveal any immediate evidence of memory issues, but it did prompt further investigation into the tracing collector's configuration and resource utilization.

Another hypothesis considered was that the trace sampler rate might be misconfigured, leading to the inconsistent capture of spans. However, upon reviewing the configuration, it appeared to be correctly set up. The team then turned their attention to the tracing collector's underlying storage, wondering if disk space or series cardinality could be contributing factors.

## Resolution steps
1. Restart tracing collector on node 3 with --max-memory 4096
2. Set tsdb.max_series 5000 in /etc/tsdb.conf
3. Increase trace-query timeout to 30000ms with -t flag
4. Run tsdb-cli compact on /var/lib/tsdb to free disk space

## Post-mortem note
The tracing collector ran out of memory due to high series cardinality. This root cause was not immediately apparent and required a thorough investigation of the tracing collector's configuration, logs, and metrics. In retrospect, the intermittent nature of the issue made it challenging to diagnose, but the eventual resolution steps addressed the underlying problem effectively. To prevent similar issues in the future, the team will review the tracing collector's resource allocation and series cardinality limits to ensure they are adequately configured for the current workload.
