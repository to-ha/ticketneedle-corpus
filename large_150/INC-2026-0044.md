## Symptom
The incident began with reports of objects being deleted earlier than expected by the lifecycle policy, which led to a significant impact on business operations. Users relying on these objects for critical tasks found themselves without access, causing delays and disruptions across multiple teams. The initial observation was that the deletion seemed to occur randomly, without any discernible pattern related to object size, type, or creation time. However, as the incident evolved, it became clear that the issue was closely related to the block storage volume snapshot process. Monitoring tools caught glimpses of increased latency in snapshot creation, which hinted at a potential resource bottleneck. Despite this, the exact cause remained elusive, requiring a deeper dive into the system's performance metrics and configuration.

As the symptom complex unfolded, it became apparent that the problem was not solely with the lifecycle policy itself but rather with the underlying storage infrastructure's ability to manage snapshots efficiently. The user impact was substantial, with teams unable to access necessary data, leading to a halt in several critical projects. The business impact was also significant, as these projects were time-sensitive and had strict deadlines. Monitoring caught the increase in snapshot latency and the premature deletion of objects but missed the root cause of these issues, necessitating a manual investigation.

The investigation into the symptom revealed that the block storage volume snapshot process was indeed at the heart of the issue. The snapshots, which were supposed to be temporary and deletable after a certain period, were being deleted prematurely due to a misconfiguration in the lifecycle policy triggered by the snapshot process's inefficiency. This led to a chain reaction where objects were being removed from the system before they should have been, causing the observed symptom.

Further analysis showed that the increased latency in snapshot creation was due to insufficient resources allocated to the snapshot service. This insufficient allocation caused timeouts and failures in the snapshot process, which in turn triggered the premature deletion of objects by the lifecycle policy. The exact mechanism of this process was complex and involved a deep interaction between the storage system's configuration, the snapshot service, and the lifecycle policy.

The symptom's evolution over time provided valuable clues about its nature. Initially, it seemed like a simple issue with the lifecycle policy, but as more data was collected and analyzed, it became clear that the root cause was related to the block storage volume snapshot process and its interaction with system resources. This realization guided the investigation towards examining resource allocation, snapshot service configuration, and the overall performance of the storage system.

## Diagnosis steps and hypotheses
Initially, the team suspected that the issue might be related to a misconfiguration in the lifecycle policy itself. To diagnose this, they reviewed the policy settings and compared them against the expected behavior. However, upon closer inspection, it became clear that the policy was correctly configured, leading to the ruling out of this hypothesis.

Another hypothesis considered was that there might be an issue with the communication between the storage system and the service managing the lifecycle policy. Diagnostic steps involved checking the API logs for any errors or inconsistencies in the communication flow. However, the logs showed no evidence of such issues, which led to the dismissal of this hypothesis as well.

The team also suspected that perhaps the block storage volume itself was experiencing issues, such as corruption or hardware faults, leading to the premature deletion of objects. To investigate this, they ran a series of disk checks and monitored the system for any signs of hardware failure. The results showed no indication of corruption or hardware issues, thus ruling out this possibility.

Further investigation led to the consideration that the snapshot process might be failing due to insufficient resources, such as CPU or memory. To diagnose this, the team used the following command to monitor disk usage and performance:
```
iostat -x 10 /dev/sda1
```
This command provided insights into the disk's performance and helped identify if there were any bottlenecks in terms of resource allocation.

Another hypothesis explored was the possibility of a software bug in the snapshot service causing it to timeout prematurely. The team reviewed the service's logs and version history to see if any recent updates could have introduced such a bug. However, no evidence of a bug was found, leading to the dismissal of this hypothesis.

As the diagnosis progressed, it became clear that the root cause was more complex and related to the interaction between the snapshot service, resource allocation, and the lifecycle policy. The team's persistence in exploring different hypotheses eventually led to the identification of the insufficient resources as the primary cause of the issue.

## Resolution steps
1. Restart snapshot service with --debug flag
2. Set snapshot timeout to 300 seconds in /etc/snap.cfg
3. Run fio --size 1G --bwavg to test volume throughput
4. Adjust iostat interval to 10 seconds for detailed monitoring
5. Use s3-style CLI to verify snapshot integrity with --checksum option

## Post-mortem note
The block storage volume snapshot timed out due to insufficient resources. This root cause was initially missed due to the complexity of the system and the multifaceted nature of the symptom. The investigation highlighted the importance of thoroughly examining system performance and resource allocation when dealing with issues related to block storage volume snapshots. Follow-up actions include reviewing and adjusting resource allocations for the snapshot service to prevent similar incidents in the future and enhancing monitoring tools to better catch resource bottlenecks before they lead to significant issues. Additionally, the team will conduct a review of the lifecycle policy's configuration and its interaction with the snapshot process to ensure that such premature deletions do not occur again. This incident also underscores the value of persistence and thoroughness in diagnosing complex technical issues, as the root cause was only uncovered after exploring several hypotheses and gathering extensive evidence.
