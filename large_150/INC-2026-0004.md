# Major Incident — INC-2026-0004
- **Ticket ID:** INC-2026-0004
- **Reported:** 2026-03-19 10:01 UTC
- **Priority:** P1
- **Domain:** Authentication
- **Status:** Resolved

## Symptom
The incident began with reports of MFA push notifications being delivered but never accepted, leading to a significant increase in authentication failures. This symptom was first observed by users attempting to access critical applications, resulting in a surge of support requests. Further investigation revealed that the issue was not isolated to a specific group or location, indicating a widespread problem. As the incident progressed, it became clear that the authentication system was experiencing a high rate of re-authentication failures, causing disruptions to business operations.

The impact of this symptom was substantial, with many users unable to access necessary resources and applications. The support team received a large volume of requests, and the incident quickly gained attention from senior management due to its potential to severely impact business continuity. The operations team was tasked with identifying the root cause and resolving the issue as quickly as possible to minimize downtime and restore normal functionality.

Initial observations suggested that the issue might be related to the session token cache, given the nature of the authentication failures. However, without further diagnostic evidence, it was crucial to explore all potential hypotheses to ensure the correct root cause was identified and addressed. The focus, therefore, remained on understanding the behavior of the session token cache and its potential impact on the authentication process.

As the team delved deeper into the issue, they noticed that the frequency of re-authentication requests was unusually high, further supporting the suspicion that the session token cache might be at the heart of the problem. This observation guided the next steps in diagnosing the issue, aiming to confirm or rule out the session token cache hypothesis.

## Diagnosis steps and hypotheses
The diagnosis process involved several hypotheses, each of which was tested and eventually ruled out based on the evidence collected. One of the initial hypotheses was that the issue could be related to network connectivity or firewall rules blocking authentication requests. However, after conducting a series of network tests, including `tcpdump` and `dig`, no issues were found that would support this hypothesis.

Another hypothesis considered was that the authentication server itself might be experiencing technical difficulties or was improperly configured. To test this, the team used `pam-test` to verify the configuration and functionality of the authentication server. Specifically, they ran the following command:
```
pam-test -c /etc/auth/pam.conf -a debug
```
This test revealed no configuration issues or server-side problems that could account for the observed symptoms.

Given the focus on the session token cache, the team also explored the possibility of cache exhaustion or misconfiguration. Although initial checks did not reveal any obvious issues with cache settings, this avenue was considered a high priority for further investigation due to the nature of the symptoms observed.

## Resolution steps
1. Restart auth service on port 8080
2. Set session_token_cache_timeout to 3600 seconds
3. Update /etc/auth/config.yaml with new cache settings
4. Clear existing cache at /var/lib/auth/cache.db

## Post-mortem note
The session token cache timeout was set too low, causing frequent re-authentication failures. This root cause, once identified and addressed through the resolution steps outlined above, led to a complete resolution of the incident. The experience highlighted the importance of regularly reviewing configuration settings, especially those related to caching mechanisms, to prevent similar issues in the future.
