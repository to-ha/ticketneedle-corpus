# Major Incident — INC-2026-0130
* **Ticket ID:** INC-2026-0130
* **Reported:** 2026-04-29 21:11 UTC
* **Priority:** P2
* **Domain:** Authentication
* **Status:** Resolved

## Symptom
The incident presented with a peculiar pattern where MFA push notifications were successfully delivered to users but were never accepted, indicating a potential issue in the authentication flow. Further investigation revealed that outbound HTTPS calls to a partner endpoint were failing at the TLS handshake stage, resulting in a generic 'handshake failure' error. This behavior was bimodal, with approximately half of the requests succeeding and the other half failing. This inconsistency suggested a problem that might be related to the configuration or the interaction between our systems and the partner endpoint.

The observed symptom of failed TLS handshakes, coupled with the successful delivery but non-acceptance of MFA push notifications, pointed towards an issue that could be impacting directory replication and authentication processes. It was crucial to identify the root cause of this handshake failure to restore normal functionality.

## Diagnosis steps and hypotheses
Initial hypotheses centered around potential misconfigurations in the TLS settings or issues with the partner endpoint's TLS implementation. To diagnose the issue, the `ldapsearch` command was utilized to test LDAP connections and understand if there were any specific patterns or errors related to directory replication. Specifically, the following command was tried:
```
ldapsearch -x -H ldaps://partner_endpoint -d 1
```
This helped in isolating the problem to the TLS handshake phase but did not immediately reveal the root cause. Further investigation into the TLS configurations and the behavior of outbound proxies was necessary.

## Resolution steps
1. Set tls_cipher_list to TLS_ECDHE_RSA_WITH_AES_256_GCM_SHA384
2. Remove legacy_proxy from proxy_rotation_config.json
3. Update proxy_config to use pinned_cipher_list=true
4. Restart proxy_service on all nodes
5. Verify partner endpoint TLS handshake with openssl s_client

## Post-mortem note
The root cause of the incident was determined to be related to the behavior of outbound proxies. Two outbound proxies presented different SNI behavior, causing handshake failures. This specific issue highlighted the importance of consistent configuration across all components involved in directory replication and authentication processes. Further review of proxy configurations and TLS settings is planned to prevent similar incidents in the future.
