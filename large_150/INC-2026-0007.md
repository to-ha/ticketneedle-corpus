## Symptom
The incident began with reports of BGP session flapping every few minutes on the WAN aggregation router. This symptom was first observed by monitoring tools, which alerted the team to the repeated establishment and teardown of BGP sessions. As the issue persisted, it became clear that this flapping was causing network instability and intermittent connectivity issues for users. The monitoring system's graphs showed a clear pattern of session establishment, followed by a brief period of stability, and then sudden termination, repeating over the course of several minutes. This pattern suggested a configuration or protocol issue rather than a hardware fault.

Further investigation revealed that the BGP session flapping was not limited to a single peer or neighbor but was instead a widespread issue affecting multiple BGP sessions. The network operations team attempted to correlate the BGP flapping with other network events or issues, such as interface errors or routing table changes, but initially found no clear correlation. The user impact was significant, with reports of slow network performance, dropped connections, and an inability to access certain resources due to the instability of the BGP sessions.

The symptom complex was characterized by its periodic nature, with BGP sessions dropping and re-establishing at irregular intervals. This periodicity made it challenging to pinpoint the exact cause, as the issue would temporarily resolve itself, only to recur a short time later. The network team focused on understanding the BGP session establishment and teardown process, looking for any clues that might explain why the sessions were flapping.

As the incident continued, the team began to suspect that the issue might be related to the configuration of the WAN aggregation router or the BGP protocol settings. They started to review configuration files, looking for any recent changes or anomalies that could be contributing to the problem. The fact that the issue was not isolated to a single BGP peer suggested a more systemic problem, possibly related to the router's configuration or its interaction with the broader network.

The monitoring system played a crucial role in detecting and characterizing the symptom. Alerts were triggered based on thresholds for BGP session state changes, allowing the team to quickly identify the issue and begin investigating. However, the initial monitoring setup did not provide a clear indication of the root cause, necessitating further diagnostic work to understand the underlying problem.

## Diagnosis steps and hypotheses
The team initially suspected that the issue might be related to a recent software update on the WAN aggregation router. They attempted to roll back the update to see if it would resolve the BGP session flapping. However, after reverting to the previous software version, the issue persisted, ruling out the update as the cause.

Another hypothesis was that the problem could be due to a misconfiguration of the BGP protocol settings. The team reviewed the BGP configuration files and compared them against known good configurations, but found no discrepancies that would explain the session flapping.

The team also considered the possibility of a hardware issue with the WAN aggregation router, such as a faulty interface or a problem with the routing engine. However, diagnostics run on the hardware did not reveal any issues, and the problem seemed to be more related to the protocol or configuration rather than a physical fault.

In an attempt to isolate the issue, the team used diagnostic tools like `dig` and `nslookup` to test DNS resolution and verify that the router was correctly resolving hostnames and IP addresses. One specific command used was:
```
dig +short mx example.com @192.168.1.1
```
This helped to confirm that the DNS resolution process was not contributing to the BGP session flapping.

Further investigation involved analyzing network captures to understand the BGP session establishment and teardown process in more detail. This analysis revealed clues about the potential cause of the flapping but did not immediately point to a specific root cause.

As the diagnosis progressed, the team began to suspect that the issue might be related to the BGP hold time setting. They reviewed the relevant configuration files and found that the hold time was set lower than recommended, which could lead to frequent session timeouts and flapping.

## Resolution steps
1. Restart router with config file /etc/network/config.txt
2. Set BGP hold time to 30 seconds in /etc/network/bgp.conf
3. Update nft table filter input to allow ICMP type 8 code 0 on port 179
4. Reload nft rules with nft -f /etc/nftables.conf

## Post-mortem note
The BGP hold time was set too low, causing frequent session timeouts. This root cause was initially missed due to the focus on other potential issues, such as software updates or hardware faults. In retrospect, a more thorough review of the BGP configuration settings earlier in the investigation might have led to a quicker resolution. The incident highlights the importance of carefully configuring protocol settings and regularly reviewing them against best practices to prevent similar issues in the future. Follow-up actions include a thorough audit of all BGP configurations across the network to ensure that hold times are appropriately set, and additional training for the network team on BGP configuration and troubleshooting.
