## Symptom
The monitoring system alerted the team to an object replication lag that had grown past the SLA threshold. This issue was observed in the cold-tier archive, where data is stored for long-term retention. The replication lag was causing delays in data availability, which could potentially impact business operations.

Further investigation revealed that the lag was consistent across all archive nodes, suggesting a systemic issue rather than a node-specific problem. The team noted that the archive lifecycle policy had recently been updated, but the changes might not have been fully propagated to all nodes.

## Diagnosis steps and hypotheses
Initial hypotheses focused on issues with the archive nodes themselves, such as hardware failures or configuration errors. However, as the team dug deeper, they considered the possibility that the replication lag was related to the cold-tier archive lifecycle policy. To investigate disk health, the team used the command:
```
smartctl -H /dev/sdX
```
This helped rule out hardware issues as the primary cause. The team then turned their attention to the policy settings and how they might be affecting data replication.

## Resolution steps
1. Set archive_lifecycle_policy.max_retention_days to 365
2. Run smartctl -H /dev/sdX on all archive disks
3. Verify multipath -ll shows all archive paths as active
4. Execute rsync --dry-run -avz /archive/ /new-archive/

## Post-mortem note
The cold-tier archive lifecycle policy was not updated to reflect the new retention period. This oversight led to the replication lag, as the system was attempting to manage data based on outdated policies. Thankfully, the issue was identified and resolved quickly, minimizing the impact on business operations.
