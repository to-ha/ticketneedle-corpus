## Symptom
The incident began with reports of an MTU black hole between two sites, resulting in intermittent packet loss and significant latency spikes. Users first noticed the issue when attempting to access resources located at the remote site, with many experiencing timeouts or failed connections. As the incident evolved, it became clear that the problem was not limited to a specific application or service, but rather affected all traffic traversing the WAN aggregation router. Monitoring tools initially caught the issue through increased error rates and latency alerts, but the full extent of the problem only became apparent as user reports flooded in. The network operations team was quickly overwhelmed with requests to investigate and resolve the issue, which had a significant impact on business operations.

Further analysis revealed that the MTU black hole was causing packets to be fragmented, leading to reassembly issues at the receiving end. This resulted in a high volume of packet retransmissions, further exacerbating the latency problem. The team attempted to use MTR and tcpdump to gather more information about the issue, but initial results were inconclusive. It wasn't until they used traceroute that they began to suspect a problem with the WAN aggregation router's configuration.

As the incident continued to affect users, the network operations team worked to gather more data and identify the root cause. They collected logs from the router and analyzed traffic patterns, looking for any clues that might explain the MTU black hole. Despite their best efforts, however, the team was unable to pinpoint the exact cause of the issue, and it wasn't until they escalated the incident to more senior engineers that progress began to be made.

The user impact of the incident was significant, with many employees unable to access critical resources or complete their work. The business impact was also substantial, with delayed projects and lost productivity resulting in financial losses. As the incident dragged on, it became clear that a swift resolution was essential to minimize further disruption.

In addition to the technical challenges, the incident also highlighted communication breakdowns within the team. It became apparent that there were gaps in knowledge and expertise, particularly when it came to troubleshooting complex network issues. The team recognized the need for additional training and resources to ensure that they were better equipped to handle similar incidents in the future.

## Diagnosis steps and hypotheses
The team initially suspected a problem with the router's MTU settings, and attempted to adjust them to resolve the issue. However, after making the changes and verifying that they had taken effect, the team found that the problem persisted. They then turned their attention to the router's fragmentation settings, wondering if perhaps packets were being fragmented incorrectly. Again, however, after adjusting the settings and re-testing, the issue remained.

Next, the team considered the possibility of a hardware fault, and attempted to isolate the problem to a specific component. They used `tcpdump` to capture traffic on both sides of the router, but were unable to find any evidence of a hardware issue. The team then decided to use `mtr` to test the connectivity between the two sites, and it was during this testing that they stumbled upon a crucial clue.

``` 
mtr -r -c 10 8.8.8.8
```
The output from this command revealed a pattern of packet loss that seemed to be related to the BGP keepalive timer. The team had not previously considered this as a potential cause, but now they began to suspect that it might be contributing to the issue.

The team also explored the possibility of a routing loop, using `traceroute` to map the path that packets were taking between the two sites. However, after analyzing the output, they found no evidence of a loop, and were forced to rule out this hypothesis as well.

Another potential cause that the team considered was a misconfiguration of the router's routing table. They used `psql` to inspect the table and verify that it was correctly populated, but again found no issues. It wasn't until they decided to update the routing table using `psql -U router -d routing_db -c 'UPDATE routes SET metric = 5'` that they began to see some improvement in the packet loss rates.

Despite these efforts, however, the team was still unable to fully resolve the issue, and it wasn't until they took a closer look at the BGP keepalive timer that they finally identified the root cause. The timer was set too low, causing the router to become unstable and resulting in the MTU black hole.

## Resolution steps
1. Restart router with config file /etc/router/config.txt
2. Set BGP keepalive timer to 30 seconds
3. Update routing table with psql -U router -d routing_db -c 'UPDATE routes SET metric = 5'
4. Run MTR to verify WAN aggregation

## Post-mortem note
The BGP keepalive timer was set too low, causing router instability. This issue was initially missed due to a lack of understanding of the router's configuration and the potential impact of the BGP keepalive timer on stability. In hindsight, it is clear that the team should have investigated this possibility earlier, and that additional training on router configuration and troubleshooting would have been beneficial. To prevent similar incidents in the future, the team will prioritize education and training on complex network issues, and will also implement more robust monitoring and alerting to quickly identify potential problems. Additionally, the team will review and update the router's configuration to ensure that the BGP keepalive timer is set to a stable value, and will conduct regular audits to verify that the configuration remains correct.
