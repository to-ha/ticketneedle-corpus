- **Ticket ID:** INC-2026-0072

- **Status:** Resolved

## Symptom
The incident began with reports of a DNS resolution latency spike to 5-10 seconds, affecting multiple users across the network. This spike was first observed by monitoring tools and later confirmed by user reports. The increase in latency was consistent and persistent, indicating a potential issue with the underlying network infrastructure.

Further investigation revealed that the latency spike was specifically related to DNS queries that relied on BGP peering sessions for routing. This suggested that the issue might be related to the configuration or stability of these sessions.

## Escalation timeline
- 07:30 — L1 Network operations: Nora
Nora began by reviewing the network monitoring logs to identify any patterns or errors that could be contributing to the latency spike.
- 08:00 — L2 Network engineer: Theo
Theo took over the investigation, focusing on the BGP peering sessions and their potential impact on DNS resolution latency.

## Diagnosis steps and hypotheses
Initial hypotheses focused on potential misconfigurations or instability in the BGP peering sessions. To investigate this, the team used `tcpdump` to capture and analyze BGP traffic:
```
tcpdump -i eth0 port 179
```
This analysis revealed discrepancies in the hold times configured for the BGP sessions, which could lead to session timeouts and subsequently affect DNS resolution latency.

## Resolution steps
1. Restart BGP service on router 10.1.1.1
2. Set hold time to 30 seconds in /etc/bgp.conf
3. Adjust keepalive interval to 10 seconds

## Post-mortem note
The BGP peering session timed out due to mismatched hold times. This incident highlights the importance of ensuring consistent configuration across all network devices, especially for critical protocols like BGP that underpin DNS resolution and other network services.
