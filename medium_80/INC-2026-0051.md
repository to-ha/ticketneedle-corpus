## Symptom
The incident began with reports from real users encountering errors while interacting with the system, despite synthetic probes indicating that everything was functioning correctly. This discrepancy suggested a problem that wasn't immediately apparent through automated monitoring. As the issue persisted, it became clear that the errors were related to the tracing collector, which is responsible for gathering and processing trace data. This data is crucial for understanding system performance and identifying bottlenecks. The fact that synthetic probes were reporting green (i.e., no issues) while real users were experiencing problems pointed towards a nuanced issue that required careful diagnosis.

Further investigation revealed that the errors were not constant but rather sporadic, making it challenging to pinpoint the exact cause. The tracing collector's role in monitoring system health meant that its malfunction could have significant implications for the overall performance and reliability of the system. Given the nature of the symptom, where synthetic probes and real user experiences diverged, it was essential to delve deeper into the tracing collector's operation and its potential points of failure.

The impact of this incident was primarily on the system's observability and the team's ability to respond effectively to issues. Without reliable trace data, diagnosing and resolving problems became significantly more difficult, potentially leading to prolonged downtime or performance degradation. Thus, it was critical to address the issue promptly and ensure that the tracing collector was functioning as expected.

## Diagnosis steps and hypotheses
Initial hypotheses focused on potential configuration issues with the tracing collector or problems with the data storage backend. The team considered the possibility of network connectivity problems affecting the tracing collector's ability to gather data but found no evidence supporting this hypothesis. Another line of inquiry involved checking for any recent changes to the system that could have inadvertently caused the issue, but none were found.

To further diagnose the problem, the team employed various tools, including `tsdb-cli` for interacting with the time-series database and `trace-query` for examining the trace data directly. An attempt was made to check the consistency of the tracing data using the following command:
```
tsdb-cli --check-consistency
```
This step was crucial in understanding whether the issue lay with the data itself or with how it was being collected or processed. However, the results from this command did not immediately reveal the root cause, necessitating further investigation.

## Resolution steps
1. Set tsdb.max_open_files to 1024
2. Restart tracing collector on node3
3. Update trace-query timeout to 30s
4. Run tsdb-cli with --repair flag

## Post-mortem note
The tracing collector failed due to excessive file descriptors. This realization highlighted the importance of monitoring system resources closely, especially in components as critical as the tracing collector. Following the resolution, the team discussed implementing additional monitoring and alerts for file descriptor usage to prevent similar incidents in the future.
