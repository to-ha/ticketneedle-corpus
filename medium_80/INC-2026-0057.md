## Symptom
The application connection pool experienced periodic spikes of dropped connections, with the pattern recurring every 30-45 minutes and resolving within a minute. This resulted in intermittent errors for users attempting to access the database, leading to frustration and decreased productivity. The database monitoring system alerted the team to these connection drops, prompting an investigation into the underlying cause.

Further analysis revealed that autovacuum was canceling under sustained write load, potentially contributing to the connection pool issues. However, this symptom alone did not provide enough information to pinpoint the root cause, requiring additional diagnosis and exploration of other potential factors.

The observable pattern of dropped connections was consistent, with each spike followed by a brief recovery period before the next occurrence. This suggested a cyclical or threshold-based issue rather than a continuous problem, which helped guide the initial troubleshooting efforts.

As the incident progressed, the team collected more data on the connection pool behavior, including metrics on active connections, wait times, and error rates. These metrics provided valuable insights into the database performance and helped identify potential bottlenecks or misconfigurations that could be contributing to the dropped connections.

The symptom complex presented a challenging puzzle for the team to solve, requiring a combination of database expertise, analytical skills, and methodical troubleshooting to uncover the underlying root cause.

## Diagnosis steps and hypotheses
The team explored several hypotheses to explain the periodic connection drops, including issues with the database configuration, network connectivity problems, and resource constraints. One potential cause considered was a misconfiguration of the connection pooler, which could lead to inefficient use of available connections and result in dropped connections under load.

To investigate this hypothesis, the team executed the following command to examine the current connection limit:
```
psql -c "SHOW max_connections;"
```
However, the results did not immediately point to a simple configuration issue, prompting further exploration of other potential causes.

Another hypothesis involved issues with the autovacuum process, given its cancellation under sustained write load. While this could contribute to performance issues and potentially affect connection availability, it did not directly explain the periodic spikes in dropped connections observed by the application.

Through a process of elimination and continued data collection, the team narrowed down the potential causes, eventually focusing on the connection pooler configuration as the most likely culprit.

## Resolution steps
1. Set max_client_conn to 200 in pgbouncer.ini
2. Restart pgbouncer service on port 5432
3. Configure batch job to use new pooler host at localhost:6432
4. Increase per-IP limit from 50 to 200 in pg_hba.conf
5. Reload pgbouncer configuration with pgbouncer -R

## Post-mortem note
Background job opened many short-lived connections that exhausted the per-IP connection limit of 50. This root cause, once identified and addressed through the resolution steps, resolved the incident and prevented future occurrences of the periodic connection drops. The team's methodical approach to diagnosing the issue ensured that the correct solution was implemented, highlighting the importance of thorough troubleshooting in complex database environments.
