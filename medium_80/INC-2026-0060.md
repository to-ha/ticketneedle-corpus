## Symptom
The incident began with reports of metrics ingestion lag, causing dashboard staleness. This symptom first appeared as a slight delay in updates on the main monitoring dashboard, which gradually increased over the course of several hours. As the lag worsened, it became clear that the issue was affecting not just the dashboard but also the underlying metrics storage, leading to concerns about data integrity and the ability to accurately monitor system performance. The user impact was significant, as teams relying on real-time metrics for decision-making and troubleshooting were unable to do so effectively. Monitoring tools initially caught the ingestion lag through alerts set up for dashboard freshness, but as the issue deepened, it became apparent that the root cause was more complex and related to the long-term metrics storage capacity.

Further investigation into the symptom revealed that the metrics ingestion pipeline was experiencing significant backpressure due to the storage issues. This backpressure resulted in a backlog of unprocessed metrics, further exacerbating the dashboard staleness. The monitoring team observed that the usual remedies for ingestion lag, such as adjusting the pipeline's throughput or restarting components, did not alleviate the issue, suggesting a more fundamental problem with the storage system.

As the incident progressed, the focus shifted from merely addressing the symptoms to understanding the underlying cause of the storage issues. The team began to suspect that the long-term metrics storage might be reaching its capacity limits, given the recent increase in monitored systems and the retention period of the metrics. This hypothesis was supported by observations of disk space usage on the storage nodes, which showed a steady increase over time, correlating with the onset of the ingestion lag.

The evolution of the symptom from a minor delay to a full-blown outage highlighted the importance of proactive monitoring and capacity planning for critical infrastructure components like long-term metrics storage. It also underscored the need for more sophisticated alerting mechanisms that can detect not just the immediate symptoms of an issue but also its underlying causes.

Throughout the incident, the team relied heavily on tools like `tsdb-cli` for inspecting the metrics storage, `trace-query` for understanding the flow of metrics through the system, and `log-tail` for monitoring the logs of the components involved in metrics ingestion and storage. These tools provided invaluable insights into the state of the system and helped guide the diagnosis and resolution efforts.

## Diagnosis steps and hypotheses
The team initially suspected that the issue might be related to network connectivity problems between the metrics ingestion pipeline and the long-term storage system. To investigate this hypothesis, they attempted to `ping` the storage nodes from the ingestion pipeline hosts and checked for any recent changes in network configurations. However, finding no issues with connectivity, they ruled out this hypothesis.

Next, the team considered the possibility of a software bug in the metrics ingestion pipeline, potentially causing it to malfunction and lead to the observed lag. They reviewed recent code changes and deployment histories but found no evidence supporting this theory. Thus, they moved on to other potential explanations.

Another hypothesis was that the long-term metrics storage system was experiencing performance issues due to high query loads. The team used `tsdb-cli` to inspect query patterns and load on the storage system but did not find any unusual activity that would explain the ingestion lag.

The team also explored the idea that the storage system might be nearing its capacity limits, given the recent growth in monitored systems and metrics volume. To assess this, they executed the following command to check the health and status of the storage:
```
tsdb-cli check /var/lib/tsdb/data
```
This command provided insights into the storage's current state, including any potential issues with data consistency or available space.

Further diagnosis involved reviewing disk usage patterns on the storage nodes, which revealed a steady increase in used space over time. This evidence supported the hypothesis that the storage system was indeed running low on space, potentially causing the ingestion pipeline to stall.

After considering and ruling out several hypotheses, the team converged on the theory that the long-term metrics storage had exceeded its allocated disk space, leading to the observed symptoms. This conclusion was based on a combination of evidence from disk usage patterns, the behavior of the metrics ingestion pipeline, and the results of diagnostic commands like `tsdb-cli check`.

## Resolution steps
1. Restart tsdb with --retention 30d flag
2. Set storage.max_size to 100GB in /etc/tsdb/config.yaml
3. Run tsdb-cli compact on /var/lib/tsdb/data directory
4. Adjust cache.size to 2GB in /etc/tsdb/config.yaml for better performance

## Post-mortem note
The long-term metrics storage exceeded its allocated disk space. This root cause was initially missed due to the focus on symptoms rather than underlying capacity issues. In retrospect, more proactive monitoring of storage capacity and regular reviews of retention policies could have prevented the incident. Following this incident, the team has implemented additional checks for storage space and adjusted the retention period to ensure that similar issues do not arise in the future. The experience highlighted the importance of comprehensive monitoring that includes not just system performance but also resource utilization and capacity planning.
