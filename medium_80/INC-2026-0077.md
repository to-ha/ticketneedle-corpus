This issue was first observed by the monitoring team, who noticed that certain dashboards were not reflecting real-time data. As the issue persisted, it became apparent that the problem was not limited to a single dashboard, but rather a systemic issue affecting multiple monitoring tools. The metrics ingestion lag was causing a ripple effect, leading to inaccurate reporting and delayed decision-making. Further investigation revealed that the synthetic-probe runner, responsible for collecting and forwarding metrics data, was experiencing issues.

The symptoms of the incident evolved over time, with the metrics ingestion lag increasing in severity. The monitoring team observed that the issue was not consistent across all metrics, with some data points being updated in real-time while others were delayed by several minutes. This inconsistency made it challenging to identify the root cause of the issue, as it appeared to be related to specific metrics or data sources. However, as the incident progressed, it became clear that the problem was more fundamental, affecting the underlying infrastructure responsible for collecting and processing metrics data.

The user and business impact of the incident was significant, as the delayed metrics ingestion was causing inaccurate reporting and decision-making. The monitoring team relies heavily on real-time data to identify issues and respond promptly, and the dashboard staleness was hindering their ability to perform their duties effectively. Furthermore, the incident was affecting multiple teams and stakeholders, including development, operations, and management, who rely on accurate metrics data to inform their decisions.

The monitoring tools caught the issue relatively quickly, with alerts being triggered for metrics ingestion lag and dashboard staleness. However, the initial alerts did not provide sufficient information to identify the root cause of the issue, and further investigation was required to determine the underlying problem. The monitoring team used a combination of tools, including trace-query and promtool-equivalent, to analyze the metrics data and identify patterns or anomalies that could indicate the source of the issue.

As the incident progressed, the monitoring team continued to investigate and gather more information about the issue. ## Diagnosis steps and hypotheses
The team initially suspected that the issue was related to network connectivity or firewall rules, given the inconsistent metrics ingestion lag. To investigate this hypothesis, they used tcpdump to capture network traffic and analyze packet loss or corruption. However, the results did not indicate any issues with network connectivity, ruling out this hypothesis.

Next, the team considered the possibility that the issue was related to the SQL database engine, which is responsible for storing and processing metrics data. They used psql to query the database and check for any errors or performance issues, but the results did not indicate any problems with the database.

The team then suspected that the issue might be related to the synthetic-probe runner itself, given its responsibility for collecting and forwarding metrics data. To investigate this hypothesis, they used the following command to check the retention period configuration:
```
tsdb-cli --get-config retention
```
The results indicated that the retention period was set incorrectly, which could be contributing to the metrics ingestion lag.

Another hypothesis considered by the team was that the issue was related to the metrics platform, which is responsible for processing and visualizing metrics data. They used dig to query the metrics platform and check for any errors or performance issues, but the results did not indicate any problems with the platform.

The team also suspected that the issue might be related to the SIEM, which is responsible for monitoring and analyzing security-related data. They used kubectl to query the SIEM and check for any errors or performance issues, but the results did not indicate any problems with the SIEM.

As the team continued to investigate, they began to suspect that the issue was more fundamental, related to the underlying infrastructure responsible for collecting and processing metrics data. They used a combination of tools, including trace-query and promtool-equivalent, to analyze the metrics data and identify patterns or anomalies that could indicate the source of the issue.

The actual root cause of the issue emerged after careful analysis of the synthetic-probe runner configuration and metrics data. The team discovered that the retention period was set incorrectly, causing the metrics ingestion lag and dashboard staleness.

## Resolution steps
1. Restart synthetic-probe runner with --debug flag
2. Set tsdb-cli retention period to 30d for probe data
3. Run promtool-equivalent with --query-interval 15s
4. Adjust trace-query timeout to 10s for slow probe queries

## Post-mortem note
The synthetic-probe runner was incorrectly configured with a low retention period. This issue was missed initially due to the complexity of the metrics ingestion pipeline and the lack of clear documentation on the synthetic-probe runner configuration. In retrospect, the team should have investigated the synthetic-probe runner configuration more thoroughly earlier in the incident. To prevent similar issues in the future, the team will review and update the documentation on the synthetic-probe runner configuration and ensure that all team members are aware of the correct retention period settings. Additionally, the team will implement more robust monitoring and alerting for the synthetic-probe runner to detect any issues with the retention period configuration promptly.
