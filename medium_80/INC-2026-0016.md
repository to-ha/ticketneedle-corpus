## Symptom
The incident began with reports of DNS resolution latency spikes to 5-10 seconds, which were first observed by users attempting to access internal web applications. Initially, the issue seemed intermittent, but as time progressed, the latency became more consistent and widespread. Monitoring tools caught the anomaly, triggering alerts for the network operations team. The spike in latency was not limited to a specific geographic region or user group, suggesting a centralized cause. Further investigation revealed that the increased latency was correlated with a surge in BGP update messages, hinting at a potential issue with the BGP peering session.

As the incident evolved, users began experiencing timeouts and failures when attempting to access resources that relied on DNS resolution. The impact was significant, affecting multiple business-critical applications and causing disruptions to daily operations. The network operations team sprang into action, working to identify the root cause of the issue. They started by analyzing network traffic patterns, looking for any signs of congestion or misconfiguration that could be contributing to the latency spike.

The team used tools like `tcpdump` and `MTR` to gather more information about the network traffic and potential bottlenecks. They also examined the configuration of the BGP peering session, searching for any misconfigurations or inconsistencies that could be causing the issue. Despite these efforts, the root cause of the problem remained elusive, and the team decided to escalate the incident to ensure timely resolution.

The escalation was necessary due to the significant impact on business operations and the potential for further disruptions if the issue was not resolved quickly. The team recognized that the BGP peering session was a critical component of their network infrastructure and that any issues with it could have far-reaching consequences. With this in mind, they worked diligently to gather as much information as possible to aid in the diagnosis and resolution of the incident.

As the investigation continued, the team discovered that the DNS resolution latency spike was not an isolated issue but rather a symptom of a larger problem with the BGP peering session. They found that the session was experiencing timeouts and failures, which were causing the latency spike and disrupting network traffic. This discovery helped to focus the team's efforts on resolving the underlying issue with the BGP peering session, rather than just addressing the symptoms.

## Diagnosis steps and hypotheses
The team considered several hypotheses as they worked to diagnose the issue. One initial suspicion was that the problem might be related to network congestion, potentially caused by an increase in traffic volume. To investigate this hypothesis, they used `nft` to analyze network traffic patterns and identify any potential bottlenecks. However, after reviewing the data, they found no evidence of congestion that could account for the latency spike, ruling out this hypothesis.

Another hypothesis was that the issue might be related to a misconfiguration of the DNS servers themselves. The team used `dig` to test DNS resolution and verify that the servers were responding correctly. However, these tests showed no issues with the DNS servers, leading the team to rule out this hypothesis as well.

The team also suspected that the problem could be related to the BGP peering session, potentially due to a misconfiguration or issue with the session itself. To investigate this hypothesis, they used the following command to capture BGP update messages:
```
tcpdump -i eth0 port 179 -vvv -c 100
```
This command helped them gather more information about the BGP traffic and identify any potential issues with the peering session. After analyzing the output, they found evidence suggesting that the BGP peering session was indeed experiencing timeouts and failures, which were causing the latency spike.

As the team continued to investigate, they considered additional hypotheses, including the possibility of a problem with the network infrastructure or a issue with the routing configuration. However, after gathering and analyzing more data, they were able to rule out these hypotheses and focus on the BGP peering session as the likely cause of the incident.

The actual root cause of the issue emerged after a thorough analysis of the BGP peering session configuration and the capture of BGP update messages. The team discovered that the timers for the BGP keepalive and holdtime were misconfigured, leading to frequent timeouts and failures of the peering session. This, in turn, caused the DNS resolution latency spike and disruptions to network traffic.

## Resolution steps
1. Set BGP keepalive timer to 30 seconds
2. Increase hold time to 120 seconds on peer 192.168.1.1
3. Restart BGP service with debug flag -d 5
4. Verify peering session with tcpdump -i eth0 port 179

## Post-mortem note
The BGP peering session timed out due to misconfigured timers. This issue was missed initially because the team was focused on other potential causes of the latency spike, such as network congestion or DNS server misconfiguration. In hindsight, a more thorough review of the BGP peering session configuration earlier in the investigation could have led to a faster resolution of the incident. To prevent similar issues in the future, the team will conduct regular reviews of the BGP peering session configuration and ensure that the timers are properly set. Additionally, the team will develop more detailed documentation of the BGP peering session configuration and troubleshooting procedures to aid in the diagnosis and resolution of future incidents.
