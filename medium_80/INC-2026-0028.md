As the team dug deeper, they found that this issue was not limited to a single pod or deployment, but rather was affecting multiple pods across different namespaces. The pods in question were all behind an ingress controller, which suggested that the issue might be related to the controller's configuration or performance.

Further investigation revealed that the CrashLoopBackOff issue was causing significant delays and errors for users attempting to access applications through the affected ingress controller. The monitoring team also reported seeing increased error rates and latency spikes in the metrics platform, which indicated that the issue was having a broad impact on the system's performance and availability. The team's initial hypothesis was that the node reboot might have caused some kind of transient issue with the pods or the ingress controller, but as they continued to investigate, it became clear that the root cause was more complex.

The symptom complex was characterized by a consistent pattern of pod crashes and restarts, along with associated errors and latency spikes. However, as they dug deeper, they found that the issue was not limited to a single version, but rather seemed to be related to the controller's configuration and performance under certain conditions.

As the incident continued to unfold, the team worked to gather more data and evidence to support their hypothesis. ## Resolution steps
1. Restart ingress controller with --v=5 flag
2. Set max-worker-connections to 10 in /etc/ingress/config.yaml
3. Update kustomize version to 4.5.4 using --force flag
4. Verify stern logs for error 503 at path /var/log/stern.log

## Post-mortem note
Ingress controller misconfiguration caused connection timeouts. The team's investigation and diagnosis efforts ultimately revealed that the root cause of the issue was a misconfiguration of the ingress controller, which had been causing connection timeouts and other errors.
