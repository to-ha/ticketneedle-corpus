## Symptom
The incident began with a node entering a NotReady state during a rolling kernel upgrade. This was first observed by monitoring tools, which alerted the team to potential issues with container runtime. Further investigation revealed that the affected node was experiencing difficulties in managing its containers, leading to a cascade of errors and ultimately resulting in the NotReady status. The error messages indicated problems with the container runtime's ability to manage logs and allocate resources efficiently.

As the team delved deeper into the issue, it became clear that the symptom was not isolated to a single node but was rather a systemic problem that could potentially affect other nodes undergoing similar upgrades. The impact of this incident was significant, as it affected the overall availability and reliability of the cluster. Users relying on services hosted on the affected node experienced disruptions, highlighting the need for swift resolution.

The team's initial focus was on understanding the nature of the container runtime issue and how it was related to the rolling kernel upgrade. They examined system logs, container logs, and resource utilization metrics to pinpoint the root cause of the problem. Tools like `crictl`, `k9s`, and `stern` were instrumental in this diagnostic process, providing insights into container states, node conditions, and log outputs.

Given the complexity of the issue and its potential to affect multiple nodes, the team recognized the importance of a thorough diagnosis. They considered various factors, including network connectivity, storage availability, and configuration changes, to ensure that all possible causes were explored. The symptom of a node entering a NotReady state during a rolling kernel upgrade served as a critical starting point for their investigation.

## Diagnosis steps and hypotheses
The diagnosis involved several steps and hypotheses. Initially, the team considered the possibility of a network issue causing the containers to lose connectivity and thus leading to the NotReady state. However, upon further investigation using tools like `dig` and `tcpdump`, no evidence of network problems was found. Another hypothesis revolved around storage constraints, but checks on disk space and volume availability did not reveal any issues.

A critical step in the diagnosis involved examining the container logs more closely. The team used the command ```
crictl logs -f 1234567890ab
``` to follow the logs of a specific container and gain insights into its behavior leading up to the error. This revealed patterns of excessive logging, which hinted at a potential resource constraint issue related to log management.

Given the evidence of excessive logging and its potential impact on resource availability, the team began to suspect that the container runtime's logging configuration might be a contributing factor. They explored the possibility of adjusting logging levels and sizes to mitigate the issue, considering it a plausible root cause.

## Resolution steps
1. Restart container runtime with --log-level=debug
2. Set /etc/containers/containers.conf max_log_size to 100MB
3. Run crictl stopp 1234567890ab to force-stop stuck container
4. Edit /etc/sysctl.conf to increase net.core.somaxconn to 1024

## Post-mortem note
The container runtime failed due to excessive logging and resource constraints. This incident highlighted the importance of monitoring logging configurations and resource allocations within container runtimes, especially during maintenance tasks like kernel upgrades. Implementing adjustments to logging settings and resource limits can prevent similar incidents in the future, ensuring greater stability and reliability of the cluster.
