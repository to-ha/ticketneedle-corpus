## Symptom
The CI runner pool experienced a significant disruption, causing secret rotation to break for one downstream consumer. This resulted in authentication failures and service interruptions. The issue was first noticed when automated tests began failing due to expired secrets.

Further investigation revealed that the runner logs contained errors related to job timeouts and queue overflows. It appeared that the runner pool was unable to handle the volume of concurrent jobs, leading to a cascade of failures. The monitoring system alerted the team to the issue, and initial troubleshooting focused on identifying the root cause of the runner pool overload.

The symptom complex centered around the CI runner pool's inability to manage its job queue effectively, highlighting a potential issue with resource allocation or configuration.

## Diagnosis steps and hypotheses
Initial hypotheses included misconfiguration of the runner pool, inadequate resource allocation, or a software bug. To investigate, the team used various tools, including the vault-style CLI and broker-admin. A key command used during diagnosis was:
```
vault-cli list --prefix /runners
```
This helped identify the scale of the issue and pointed towards the root cause being related to the runner pool's capacity to handle concurrent jobs.

## Resolution steps
1. Restart runner at /usr/local/bin/runner
2. Set max_jobs to 5 in /etc/runner/config
3. Increase log level to debug in /etc/runner/logging
4. Clear stale jobs with vault-cli delete --prefix /runners/stale

## Post-mortem note
The CI runner pool was overwhelmed by too many concurrent jobs. This incident highlights the importance of monitoring resource utilization and adjusting configurations accordingly to prevent similar disruptions in the future.
