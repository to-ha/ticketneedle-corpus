## Symptom
The incident began with reports of secret rotation breaks affecting one downstream consumer, which relied on the CI runner pool for continuous integration tasks. This breakage resulted in failed builds and deployments, causing a significant impact on development workflows. The symptom was first noticed when automated tests started failing due to expired secrets, indicating an issue with the secret rotation mechanism. Further investigation revealed that the CI runner pool was not functioning as expected, leading to a cascade of failures in dependent services.

As the issue persisted, it became clear that the problem was not isolated to a single service or component but was rather a symptom of a deeper issue within the CI runner pool itself. The automated build and deployment processes were unable to complete successfully, resulting in a backlog of unfinished tasks. This had a direct impact on the development team's ability to push updates and fixes, affecting both internal and external stakeholders.

The monitoring system alerted the team to an unusual pattern of errors related to the CI runner pool, prompting an immediate investigation into the cause of the failures. The error logs indicated issues with resource allocation and concurrency, suggesting that the CI runner pool was overwhelmed or misconfigured. This information helped narrow down the potential causes and guided the initial diagnostic efforts.

Given the critical nature of the CI runner pool to the development pipeline, resolving this issue quickly was paramount to minimize downtime and prevent further disruptions to service. The team's focus, therefore, was on identifying the root cause of the CI runner pool's malfunction and implementing a swift and effective resolution.

## Diagnosis steps and hypotheses
Initial hypotheses centered around configuration issues or recent changes to the CI runner pool. The team considered the possibility of a resource constraint, such as insufficient memory or CPU allocation, which could lead to the observed failures. To explore this hypothesis, they examined system logs and metrics for signs of resource exhaustion.

Another hypothesis was that there might be an issue with the secret rotation mechanism itself, independent of the CI runner pool's performance. This led to an investigation into the authentication and authorization processes used by the pool.

To gain more insight into the historical performance and potential issues with the CI runner pool, the team executed the following command:
```
flag-history --pool ci-runner-pool
```
This provided valuable information on past flags and configurations that might have impacted the pool's operation. However, after analyzing the output, it became clear that the root cause was more closely related to concurrency and capacity issues rather than historical configuration changes.

## Resolution steps
1. Restart ci-runner-pool with --max-retries 5
2. Set CI_CONCURRENCY to 10 in /etc/ci-config.yml
3. Run registry-gc with --dry-run to identify unused images
4. Remove unused images older than 30 days from the registry
5. Update broker-admin config with --ci-runner-pool-size 20

## Post-mortem note
The ci-runner-pool exhausted its capacity due to a concurrency issue. This incident highlighted the importance of regularly reviewing and adjusting the configuration of critical components like the CI runner pool to ensure they can handle the current workload demands. Future preventative measures will include more frequent monitoring of pool performance and automated alerts for potential concurrency issues, aiming to prevent similar incidents from occurring in the future.
