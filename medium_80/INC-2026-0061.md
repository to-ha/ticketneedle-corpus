## Symptom
The incident began with reports of an MTU black hole between two sites, causing intermittent packet loss and latency spikes for users relying on the recursive DNS resolver cluster. Initial observations suggested that the issue was not consistently reproducible, making it challenging to pinpoint the root cause. As the incident progressed, monitoring tools started to alert on increased error rates for DNS queries, further indicating a problem within the DNS infrastructure.

Further investigation into the MTU black hole revealed that it was specifically affecting traffic traversing a particular network path, hinting at a potential configuration or filtering issue along that route. The impact was significant, with users experiencing failures in resolving domain names, which in turn affected various applications and services dependent on DNS resolution.

The symptom complex was characterized by its unpredictability and the specific nature of the network paths affected, suggesting a nuanced issue that required careful diagnosis. Given the critical nature of DNS services to overall network functionality, resolving this incident was of the utmost priority.

As the on-call engineer delved deeper, tools like `iperf3` and `MTR` were utilized to better understand the network conditions and identify any bottlenecks or misconfigurations. These tools provided invaluable insights into the packet flow and helped in narrowing down the possible causes.

## Diagnosis steps and hypotheses
Initial hypotheses centered around potential network misconfigurations or recent changes to firewall rules that might be causing the MTU issues. The team explored the possibility of a rogue device or a software bug affecting packet transmission. 

One of the key diagnostic steps involved running `iperf3` tests to assess the connectivity and throughput between sites, which helped in isolating the problem to the DNS resolver cluster. Specifically, the command:
```
iperf3 -p 53 -c 10.0.1.5 -t 30
```
was used to test the performance of the DNS service, providing critical insights into its operation under load.

As the diagnosis progressed, it became clear that the issue was more closely related to the DNS resolver's operation rather than a pure network connectivity problem. The team considered hypotheses related to DNS cache sizes and the potential for the resolver to be overwhelmed by queries, leading to the observed symptoms.

## Resolution steps
1. Restart dns-resolver service on node 10.0.1.5
2. Set max_cache_size to 10000 in /etc/dns/conf.yaml
3. Run nft -f /etc/nftables.d/dns_rules.nft to update filter rules
4. Verify dns-resolver port 53 is open using iperf3 -p 53 -c 10.0.1.5

## Post-mortem note
The recursive DNS resolver cluster experienced cache overflow due to excessive queries. This root cause was identified after a thorough diagnosis that ruled out initial hypotheses related to network misconfigurations and routing issues. The resolution steps effectively addressed the cache overflow problem, ensuring the stability and performance of the DNS service. Future preventative measures will include monitoring DNS query loads more closely and adjusting cache sizes as needed to prevent similar incidents.
