## Symptom
The incident began with a noticeable spike in DNS resolution latency, peaking at 5-10 seconds. This symptom was first observed by users attempting to access internal web applications, who reported delays in page loading times. Initially, the issue seemed intermittent, but as time progressed, the latency became more consistent and widespread. Monitoring tools caught the anomaly, triggering alerts for the network operations team. The DNS resolution latency spike was the primary observable pattern, and it was this symptom that drove the initial troubleshooting efforts.

As the incident evolved, it became clear that the latency was not limited to a specific subset of users or applications but was instead a broader network issue. The network operations team started receiving reports from various departments, all experiencing similar delays in DNS resolution. This led to a surge in helpdesk tickets and internal communication about the ongoing issue. The impact on business operations was significant, with several critical services relying on timely DNS resolution to function correctly.

The monitoring system's ability to detect the latency spike was crucial in initiating the response to the incident. However, the initial alerts did not provide enough detail to pinpoint the root cause directly. Further investigation was necessary to understand why the DNS resolution was experiencing such high latency. Tools like `nslookup` and `Wireshark` were among the first reached for to start diagnosing the issue, given their utility in analyzing DNS traffic and network packet capture.

Given the nature of the symptom, the focus quickly turned to the network infrastructure that supports DNS resolution. This included examining the configuration of routers, particularly those involved in the OSPF backbone area, as they play a critical role in routing traffic within the network. The high latency in DNS resolution suggested there might be an issue with how packets were being routed or if there was congestion in the network paths used for DNS queries.

The initial hypothesis was that there could be a configuration error or a hardware issue affecting the routers responsible for the OSPF backbone area. This hypothesis was based on the understanding that any disruption in the OSPF area could lead to routing issues, which in turn could cause delays in DNS resolution. Thus, the first steps in diagnosis involved checking the status of these routers and analyzing network traffic to identify any anomalies.

## Diagnosis steps and hypotheses
The team suspected that the issue might be related to a routing loop or a misconfigured OSPF area, given the symptoms. To diagnose this, they used `mtr` to trace the path of packets and identify any potential loops or bottlenecks.
```
mtr -r 10.1.1.100
```
This command provided valuable insights into the path that packets were taking, helping to rule out certain hypotheses about the cause of the latency.

One of the initial hypotheses was that there might be a hardware failure in one of the routers. However, after running diagnostics on the suspected hardware, no issues were found, ruling out this hypothesis. Another hypothesis considered was that of a denial-of-service (DoS) attack targeting the DNS servers. Yet, upon analyzing traffic patterns and logs, there was no evidence to support this theory, leading to its dismissal.

The team also explored the possibility of a software bug in the router's OSPF implementation. Although this seemed plausible, after reviewing release notes and consulting with vendors, no known bugs matched the observed behavior, causing the team to look elsewhere for the root cause.

As the diagnosis progressed, the focus narrowed down to configuration issues within the OSPF backbone area. It was here that the team finally found evidence pointing towards the root cause of the problem. The OSPF backbone area id was found to be misconfigured, which explained the routing issues and subsequent DNS resolution latency.

## Resolution steps
1. Set ospf backbone area id to 0.0.0.1
2. Restart ospfd service on router 10.1.1.100
3. Update ospf config with metric-type 2 on interface eth0
4. Verify ospf neighbor state on router 10.1.1.200 with nslookup

## Post-mortem note
The ospf backbone area id was misconfigured as 0.0.0.2. This misconfiguration led to the significant latency in DNS resolution observed during the incident. The root cause was missed initially due to the complexity of the network and the fact that the symptoms could have been caused by a variety of issues. In hindsight, a more thorough review of the OSPF configuration at the outset might have led to a quicker resolution. Follow-up actions include a comprehensive audit of all network configurations to prevent similar misconfigurations in the future and additional training for the network operations team on troubleshooting OSPF-related issues.
