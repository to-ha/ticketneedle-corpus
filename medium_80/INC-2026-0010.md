## Symptom
The incident began with reports of the lifecycle policy deleting objects earlier than expected from the block storage volume snapshots. This was first observed by the monitoring system, which triggered an alert due to an unusual increase in deletion rates. Upon further investigation, it was found that the snapshots themselves were not being properly retained, leading to premature deletions of critical data. The impact was significant, as these snapshots are used for both backup and auditing purposes, and their loss could result in compliance issues and data unrecoverability.

Further analysis revealed that the issue was not uniform across all storage volumes but seemed to be concentrated on a specific subset of volumes. This suggested that the problem might be related to the configuration or the health of these particular volumes rather than a systemic issue with the lifecycle policy itself. The observation led to a deeper dive into the storage layer, focusing on the multipathing and snapshot creation processes.

The symptom complex pointed towards an issue with how the block storage system was handling snapshots, potentially due to a misconfiguration or a bug in the snapshot management software. However, without direct evidence of the root cause, it was necessary to escalate the issue to gather more expertise and resources for a thorough investigation.

As the investigation progressed, it became clear that understanding the behavior of the multipath configuration and its impact on snapshot creation was crucial. Tools like `multipath -ll` and `fio` were considered essential for diagnosing the issue, given their ability to provide insights into the multipathing configuration and the performance of the storage system.

Given the critical nature of the data involved and the potential for further data loss, the incident was treated with high priority. The focus remained on identifying the root cause of the premature deletions and implementing a fix that would prevent such incidents in the future.

## Diagnosis steps and hypotheses
Initial hypotheses included a misconfiguration of the lifecycle policy, issues with the block storage system's health, or problems with the network connectivity affecting the storage volumes. However, as the investigation deepened, it became clear that the issue was more complex and likely related to how the system handled multipathing for the affected volumes.

One of the key diagnostic steps involved running the command:
```
multipath -ll
```
to examine the current multipath configuration and identify any potential issues or misconfigurations that could be impacting snapshot creation.

Another hypothesis considered was that the performance of the storage system was degraded, leading to timeouts or failures in the snapshot process. This was tested using `fio` to benchmark the storage performance and verify if there were any bottlenecks.

The use of `rsync --dry-run` was also considered to test the integrity of the data transfer process without actually moving data, which could help in identifying issues related to data consistency or transfer errors.

## Resolution steps
1. Restart multipathd on node 4 with /etc/init.d/multipathd restart
2. Set max_queue_depth to 128 in /etc/multipath.conf
3. Run fio --filename=/dev/mapper/mpath0 --size=10G --rw=randwrite
4. Verify snapshot size with rsync --dry-run -avz /mnt/source/ /mnt/target/

## Post-mortem note
The block storage volume snapshot failed due to a multipathing issue. This incident highlights the importance of regularly reviewing and testing the configuration of critical systems like multipathing, which can have significant impacts on data integrity and availability. The swift escalation and collaborative effort among the teams were instrumental in resolving the issue before more severe consequences occurred.
