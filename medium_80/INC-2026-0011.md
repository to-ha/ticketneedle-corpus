# Major Incident — INC-2026-0011

* **Reported:** 2026-04-15 01:58 UTC
* **Priority:** P1
* **Domain:** Authentication
* **Status:** Resolved

## Symptom
The incident began with users intermittently receiving 401 errors after successful login attempts. This behavior was observed across various user groups and seemed to be unrelated to specific user actions or login methods. As the issue persisted, monitoring tools started to pick up on a pattern of failed outbound HTTPS calls to a partner endpoint, which were failing at the TLS handshake stage with a generic 'handshake failure' error. Notably, the behavior was bimodal, with approximately half of the requests succeeding and the other half failing. This inconsistency made it challenging to identify a clear cause, as the same request could succeed or fail randomly.

Further investigation into the symptom revealed that the issue was not limited to a specific subset of users or a particular time of day. The failures were distributed evenly across all user groups and occurred at random intervals, suggesting that the problem might be related to the underlying infrastructure or the communication with the partner endpoint rather than user-specific factors. The monitoring tools provided valuable insights into the failure patterns, highlighting the need for a deeper dive into the TLS handshake process and the potential causes of the handshake failures.

As the symptom evolved, it became clear that the issue was impacting not just the users who were experiencing the 401 errors but also the overall system performance. The random nature of the failures made it difficult to predict when and how the issue would manifest, adding complexity to the troubleshooting process. Despite these challenges, the focus remained on understanding the TLS handshake failures and their connection to the session token cache, given the intermittent nature of the 401 errors post-login.

The tools most commonly used for initial diagnosis included session-trace and pam-test, which provided insights into the session management and authentication processes. However, it was the use of openssl s_client that ultimately offered a glimpse into the TLS handshake issues, setting the stage for further investigation into the certificate validation and trust store configuration.

Given the bimodal nature of the request failures, it was crucial to capture and analyze both successful and failed requests to understand the differences in handling. This approach led to a more detailed examination of the TLS handshake process and the potential impact of certificate changes or updates on the partner's side.

The shared symptom of outbound HTTPS calls failing at the TLS handshake stage with a 'handshake failure' error, exhibiting bimodal behavior, was a critical piece of information. It pointed towards issues that could be related to certificate validation, trust stores, or the TLS protocol version compatibility. This symptom guided the initial diagnostic steps, focusing on the communication between our system and the partner endpoint.

## Diagnosis steps and hypotheses
Initial hypotheses included issues with the partner's server configuration, problems with our own firewall rules, or even a possible DDoS attack affecting the outbound traffic. Each of these hypotheses was tested through various diagnostic steps. For instance, checking the partner's server status and our firewall logs did not reveal any obvious misconfigurations or attack patterns.

Another hypothesis considered was that of a TLS version compatibility issue, given the recent updates in TLS protocols. This led to an examination of the TLS versions supported by both our system and the partner's endpoint. However, tests using different TLS versions did not yield conclusive results that would point to this as the primary cause.

The team also suspected issues with the session token cache itself, possibly due to incorrect handling or expiration of tokens. Investigating this involved diving into the cache management code and monitoring its behavior during the login process. While some optimizations were identified and implemented, they did not resolve the handshake failure issue.

A critical step in the diagnosis involved examining the TLS handshake process more closely. This is where the command ```openssl s_client -connect example.com:443 -showcerts``` became invaluable, as it allowed for a detailed inspection of the certificates exchanged during the handshake. This step was crucial in identifying potential certificate-related issues, such as expiration, validation problems, or missing intermediate certificates.

Further hypotheses included network connectivity issues or problems with the routing of requests to the partner endpoint. These were investigated through network tracing tools and examination of routing tables but did not yield evidence supporting these theories.

As the investigation progressed, the focus narrowed down to certificate validation and trust store configuration, given the intermittent nature of the failures and the specific error messages related to handshake failures. It was during this phase that the actual root cause began to emerge, pointing towards an issue with the trust store and the validation of certificates presented by the partner endpoint.

## Resolution steps
1. Download new intermediate CA bundle from partner website https://example.com/ca_bundle.pem
2. Add new CA bundle to system trust store at /etc/ssl/certs/ca-certificates.crt
3. Reload connection pool with updated trust store using openssl s_client -connect example.com:443 -verify_return_error
4. Verify TLS handshake success with session-trace and pam-test tools

## Post-mortem note
The root cause of the incident was Partner rotated their server certificate to use an intermediate CA not in our trust store. This change, while transparent to many systems, highlighted a gap in our certificate validation process, specifically regarding the updating and management of trusted certificates. Initially, this issue was missed due to its intermittent nature and the complexity of diagnosing TLS handshake failures. The resolution steps effectively addressed the problem by updating the trust store with the necessary intermediate CA certificate, ensuring that our system could properly validate the partner's new server certificate.

Following this incident, several follow-up actions were initiated. These included a review of our trust store management processes to ensure that all necessary certificates are up-to-date and properly configured. Additionally, automated checks were implemented to monitor for potential certificate issues, such as upcoming expirations or changes in the certificate chain, which could impact our system's ability to communicate securely with partner endpoints. These measures aim to prevent similar incidents in the future by enhancing our system's resilience to changes in certificate configurations and improving our diagnostic capabilities for TLS-related issues.
