* **Reported:** 2026-01-23 12:47 UTC
* **Priority:** P1
* **Domain:** Containers / Kubernetes
* **Status:** Resolved

## Symptom
The incident began with reports of nodes entering a NotReady state during a rolling kernel upgrade. This symptom was first observed by the monitoring team, who noticed that several nodes were failing to respond to health checks. As the upgrade progressed, more nodes began to exhibit this behavior, causing concern among the operations team. The NotReady state was characterized by an inability to schedule new pods on the affected nodes, as well as errors when attempting to access node metrics.

Further investigation revealed that the issue was not limited to a specific subset of nodes, but rather seemed to be affecting nodes randomly as they underwent the kernel upgrade. This randomness made it difficult to pinpoint a specific cause, and initial hypotheses focused on potential issues with the upgrade process itself. However, as the incident escalated, it became clear that the root cause was more complex and related to the cluster's autoscaling capabilities.

The impact of the incident was significant, as the reduced number of available nodes began to affect the overall capacity of the cluster. This, in turn, started to impact service performance, with some services experiencing increased latency and error rates. The operations team worked to mitigate these effects by manually scaling up other nodes, but it was clear that a more permanent solution was needed to resolve the incident.

As the incident continued to unfold, the team focused on understanding the relationship between the kernel upgrade, node state, and autoscaling. It became apparent that the cluster autoscaler was not functioning as expected, leading to a situation where nodes were being incorrectly marked as NotReady. This realization helped to guide the diagnosis and eventual resolution of the incident.

## Diagnosis steps and hypotheses
Initial hypotheses centered around issues with the kernel upgrade process, potentially causing nodes to become unresponsive. However, as diagnostic efforts progressed, attention shifted towards the cluster autoscaler and its potential role in marking nodes as NotReady. The team used various tools, including `crictl` and `etcdctl`, to gather insights into node and pod behavior.

One key diagnostic step involved running the following command to assess pod eviction rates and node performance:
```
crictl stats --timeout 10s
```
This command provided valuable information about the current state of pods on the affected nodes, helping to inform subsequent hypotheses. Despite this effort, initial hypotheses focusing on upgrade issues or node-level problems were eventually ruled out as the team gathered more evidence pointing towards an autoscaling-related root cause.

## Resolution steps
1. Restart cluster autoscaler with --v=4 flag
2. Set scale-down-delay to 5m in autoscaler config
3. Run crictl stats to verify pod eviction rates
4. Increase max-nodes-total to 20 in cluster config
5. Verify etcd cluster health with etcdctl member list

## Post-mortem note
The cluster autoscaler failed to scale up due to incorrect configuration. This root cause was identified through a combination of diagnostic efforts and hypothesis testing, ultimately leading to the resolution steps outlined above. The incident highlighted the importance of carefully configuring and monitoring autoscaling components to prevent similar issues in the future.
