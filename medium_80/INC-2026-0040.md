## Symptom
The incident began with a spike in deadlocks during a release window, which was observed by the monitoring system. The deadlocks were causing significant delays in query execution, leading to a backlog of pending requests. As the issue persisted, the database performance continued to degrade, resulting in increased error rates and slower response times. The users reported experiencing timeouts and errors when attempting to access the database, which had a direct impact on their ability to perform critical tasks.

The monitoring system alerted the team to the issue, and upon further investigation, it became clear that the deadlocks were related to a specific set of queries that were being executed concurrently. The team noticed that the queries were competing for resources, leading to contention and ultimately resulting in deadlocks. The symptom complex was characterized by a rapid increase in deadlock events, followed by a decline in database performance and an increase in error rates.

As the incident evolved, the team observed that the deadlocks were not limited to a specific set of queries, but rather were affecting a broader range of queries. This suggested that the issue was not isolated to a specific application or workflow, but rather was a more systemic problem. The team also noted that the deadlocks were occurring during peak usage hours, which further exacerbated the impact on users.

The database logs showed a significant increase in wait events, indicating that the queries were spending a large amount of time waiting for resources to become available. The logs also revealed that the deadlocks were causing the database to roll back transactions, resulting in additional overhead and further degrading performance. The team used tools such as `explain analyze` and `iostat` to gather more information about the queries and system resource utilization.

The symptom complex was characterized by a set of observable patterns, including increased deadlock events, declining database performance, and rising error rates. The team focused on understanding the root cause of these patterns, with the goal of identifying and addressing the underlying issue.

## Diagnosis steps and hypotheses
The team initially suspected that the issue was related to inadequate indexing, which could be causing the queries to scan large amounts of data and leading to contention. To investigate this hypothesis, they used `explain analyze` to examine the query plans and identify any potential indexing opportunities. However, the results showed that the existing indexes were adequate, and the queries were using them effectively.

Next, the team considered the possibility that the issue was related to inadequate connection pooling, which could be causing the database to become overwhelmed with concurrent connections. To investigate this hypothesis, they reviewed the connection pooling configuration and found that it was properly configured. They also checked the database logs for any signs of connection-related errors, but none were found.

The team then suspected that the issue might be related to disk I/O bottlenecks, which could be causing the database to slow down due to excessive disk activity. To investigate this hypothesis, they used `iostat` to monitor disk usage and identify any potential bottlenecks. However, the results showed that disk I/O was within normal limits, and there were no signs of bottlenecks.

Another hypothesis considered by the team was that the issue might be related to inadequate query optimization, which could be causing the queries to execute inefficiently and lead to contention. To investigate this hypothesis, they used `explain analyze` to examine the query plans and identify potential optimization opportunities. They found that some queries were using suboptimal plans, but optimizing them did not resolve the issue.

The team also suspected that the issue might be related to logical replication slot contention, which could be causing the database to slow down due to excessive replication activity. To investigate this hypothesis, they reviewed the replication configuration and found that it was properly configured. However, they did notice that the replication slot was filling up rapidly, which suggested that there might be an issue with the replication process.

After ruling out these hypotheses, the team finally discovered that the root cause of the issue was related to logical replication slot contention. They found that the replication slot was filling up rapidly due to a high volume of client connections, which was causing the database to slow down and resulting in deadlocks.

## Resolution steps
1. Restart pgbouncer with -v flag
2. Set max_client_conn to 100 in pgbouncer.ini
3. Run explain analyze on slow query with limit 10
4. Increase wal_sender_timeout to 3000ms in postgresql.conf
5. Check iostat for disk usage on /dev/sda1

## Post-mortem note
Logical replication slot was filled due to high client connection load. This issue was missed initially because the team was focused on other potential causes, such as indexing and query optimization. In retrospect, the team should have investigated the replication configuration more thoroughly, as it was a critical component of the database infrastructure. To prevent similar issues in the future, the team will implement additional monitoring and alerting for replication slot usage, and will also review the connection pooling configuration to ensure that it is properly sized for the expected workload.
