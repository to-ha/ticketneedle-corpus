## Symptom
The network team observed a peculiar MTU black hole issue between two sites, where packets larger than a certain size were being dropped. This resulted in intermittent connectivity issues and packet loss. The symptoms were first noticed by users attempting to access resources across the affected link, who reported slow or failed connections. Initial investigations suggested that the problem might be related to a misconfigured network device or a fault in the underlying infrastructure.

Further analysis revealed that the issue was not limited to a specific set of users or resources but was instead affecting all traffic traversing the link. The network team attempted to reproduce the issue using tools like `dig` and `traceroute` to better understand the path packets were taking and where they might be getting dropped. Wireshark captures were also taken to inspect packet sizes and potential fragmentation issues.

As the investigation continued, it became clear that the root cause of the problem was more complex than initially thought. The team considered various hypotheses, including misconfigured firewall rules, incorrect routing tables, and issues with the edge load balancer. Despite these efforts, the exact cause remained elusive, necessitating further escalation and analysis.

The impact of the incident was significant, with users experiencing frustration due to the unreliable network connectivity. This highlighted the need for swift resolution to restore full functionality and prevent further disruptions. The network team worked diligently to identify and address the root cause, recognizing the critical nature of the issue.

## Diagnosis steps and hypotheses
The diagnosis process involved several hypotheses, each of which was tested and eventually ruled out. One of the initial thoughts was that there might be a firewall rule blocking large packets, but after reviewing the firewall configurations, no such rules were found. Another hypothesis considered was that the routing tables might be incorrectly configured, leading to packets being sent via a path that did not support larger packet sizes. However, inspection of the routing tables did not reveal any issues.

To further investigate the MTU issue, the team executed the following command to verify DNS resolution:
```
dig +short loadbalancer.example.com
```
This step was crucial in understanding whether the load balancer's configuration or DNS resolution could be contributing factors to the observed symptoms. Despite these efforts, the team still needed to consider other potential causes, including issues with the edge load balancer itself.

## Resolution steps
1. Set timeout to 30 seconds for edge load balancer
2. Restart load balancer service on node 10.0.1.5
3. Update load balancer config at /etc/loadbalancer.conf
4. Verify DNS resolution using dig +short loadbalancer.example.com

## Post-mortem note
The edge load balancer timed out due to incorrect configuration. This incident highlights the importance of regularly reviewing and testing network configurations to prevent such issues. The swift escalation and collaborative effort between the network operations and engineering teams were key to resolving the incident quickly and minimizing user impact.
