## Symptom
The incident began with an observable pattern of object replication lag growing past the SLA threshold. This was initially detected by monitoring tools, which alerted the team to a potential issue with the cold-tier archive lifecycle policy. As the lag continued to increase, it became clear that this was not a transient issue, but rather a more persistent problem that required attention.

Further investigation revealed that the replication lag was affecting a specific subset of objects, all of which were stored in the archive tier. This suggested that the issue was related to the archive lifecycle policy, rather than a more general problem with the storage system.

The growing replication lag had significant implications for the system, as it meant that data was not being properly replicated and was therefore at risk of loss in the event of a failure. This necessitated prompt action to identify and resolve the root cause of the issue.

## Diagnosis steps and hypotheses
Initial diagnosis steps focused on investigating the archive lifecycle policy configuration and checking for any errors in the system logs. One hypothesis considered was that the issue was related to a misconfiguration of the policy, which was causing the replication lag. To investigate this, the team ran the command:
```
s3cmd ls -r s3://archive --recursive
```
This command helped to identify the objects that were affected by the replication lag and provided further insight into the issue. However, it did not reveal any obvious misconfigurations, and the team was forced to consider alternative hypotheses.

## Resolution steps
1. Set archive policy retention to 365 days
2. Run s3cmd sync on /data/archive with --delete-removed option
3. Verify multipath -ll shows all 4 paths to storage node 10.1.1.100
4. Restart iostat logging with interval 30 to monitor disk usage

## Post-mortem note
The archive lifecycle policy was not updated to reflect new retention requirements. This root cause was identified through a combination of log analysis and configuration reviews, and the resolution steps were effective in resolving the incident. The team will review the archive lifecycle policy configuration to ensure that it is properly updated to reflect changing retention requirements.
