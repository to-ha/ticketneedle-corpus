## Symptom
The incident began with reports of dashboard staleness, where metrics were not updating in real-time. This caused concern among the teams relying on these dashboards for operational insights. Upon initial investigation, it was observed that there was a significant metrics ingestion lag. This lag was causing the dashboards to show outdated information, which in turn was affecting the ability of the operations team to respond promptly to changes in the system's performance.

Further analysis revealed that the issue was not with the data sources themselves but rather with the tracing collector service responsible for ingesting these metrics. The service was not processing the metrics at the expected rate, leading to a backlog of unprocessed data. This backlog was the primary cause of the dashboard staleness observed by the users.

The impact of this incident was significant because it affected the visibility into system performance. Without real-time metrics, the operations team was blind to potential issues that could escalate into more severe problems. The lack of timely insights also meant that any corrective actions would be delayed, potentially leading to a wider impact on service availability and user experience.

As the incident progressed, the focus shifted towards identifying the root cause of the tracing collector service's inefficiency. It became clear that resolving this issue required a deep dive into the service's configuration, performance metrics, and logs to understand what was causing the slowdown.

## Diagnosis steps and hypotheses
Initial hypotheses included a potential misconfiguration of the tracing collector service, resource constraints such as CPU or memory limitations, or issues with the underlying database where metrics are stored. To investigate these hypotheses, various diagnostic tools were employed, including `trace-query` to check the metrics flow, `log-tail` to monitor the service logs in real-time, and `tsdb-cli` to inspect the metrics database.

One of the key commands used during diagnosis was:
```
log-tail /var/log/tracing/collector.log
```
This command provided valuable insights into the service's runtime behavior, helping to identify potential error patterns or performance issues.

Despite these efforts, the initial hypotheses did not yield a clear root cause, necessitating a deeper dive into the service's configuration and performance metrics. It was crucial to understand how the service was behaving under load and whether there were any external factors influencing its performance.

## Resolution steps
1. Restart tracing collector service with --debug flag
2. Set max_traces to 1000 in /etc/tracing/config.yaml
3. Increase disk space for /var/lib/trace-data to 10GB
4. Run tsdb-cli with --repair option on tracing database

## Post-mortem note
The tracing collector service crashed due to insufficient disk space. This root cause was identified after a thorough analysis of the service's logs and configuration. The resolution steps effectively addressed the issue by ensuring the service had sufficient resources to operate correctly and by repairing any potential damage to the metrics database. The incident highlighted the importance of monitoring resource utilization for critical services like the tracing collector to prevent similar issues in the future.
