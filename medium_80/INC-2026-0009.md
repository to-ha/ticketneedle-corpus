## Symptom
The incident began with reports of the log forwarder experiencing back-pressure, resulting in events being dropped silently. This issue was first noticed by the monitoring team, who observed a significant decrease in the number of logs being forwarded to the SIEM. The symptom was not immediately apparent to users, as the affected logs were primarily used for auditing and debugging purposes. However, as the incident progressed, the impact on the system's visibility and troubleshooting capabilities became more pronounced. The monitoring dashboard showed a steady increase in dropped events over a period of 30 minutes, with the rate of drops accelerating rapidly towards the end of this timeframe.

Further investigation revealed that the issue was not limited to a specific log source or type, but rather appeared to be a systemic problem affecting all logs being forwarded through the log forwarder. The team attempted to use `log-tail` to inspect the logs in real-time, but the high volume of dropped events made it challenging to identify any patterns or correlations. The lack of visibility into the system's behavior made it difficult for the team to determine the root cause of the issue, and the situation continued to deteriorate.

As the incident continued, the team began to notice that the tracing collector was not reporting any new spans, which further exacerbated the issue. The `trace-query` service was still operational, but it was not receiving any new data, making it impossible to troubleshoot the problem using tracing. The team realized that the issue was likely related to the tracing collector, but they were unsure of the exact cause.

The user impact of the incident was significant, as the lack of logging and tracing data made it challenging for the team to identify and resolve issues in a timely manner. The business impact was also substantial, as the incident affected the team's ability to monitor and troubleshoot the system, leading to potential delays and outages.

The monitoring system caught the initial decrease in log volume, but it did not detect the root cause of the issue. The team had to rely on manual inspection and troubleshooting to identify the problem. In hindsight, additional monitoring and alerting could have been implemented to detect similar issues earlier and prevent them from escalating.

## Diagnosis steps and hypotheses
The team initially suspected that the issue was related to the log forwarder's configuration, and they attempted to verify this by checking the configuration files and metrics. However, after reviewing the logs and metrics, they found no evidence to support this hypothesis. They then suspected that the issue might be related to the tracing collector's performance, and they attempted to use `trace-query` to inspect the tracing data. Again, they found no evidence to support this hypothesis.

The team then considered the possibility that the issue was related to the SIEM's configuration or performance. They checked the SIEM's metrics and logs but found no issues. They also suspected that the problem might be related to network connectivity or firewall rules, but after checking the network configuration and firewall rules, they found no evidence to support this hypothesis.

Another hypothesis considered was that the issue was related to the tracing collector's database. The team used the following command to check the database's consistency:
```
tsdb-cli --check-consistency /var/lib/tsdb
```
However, the output of this command did not reveal any issues with the database.

The team also suspected that the issue might be related to the log forwarder's resource utilization. They checked the log forwarder's CPU and memory usage but found no evidence of resource exhaustion.

Finally, after exhausting all other possibilities, the team discovered that the tracing collector had exceeded its storage capacity, which was causing it to stop processing new spans. This realization led to the identification of the root cause and the implementation of the resolution steps.

## Resolution steps
1. Restart tracing collector on node 3
2. Set max_trace_size to 10MB in /etc/tracing/config.yml
3. Increase tsdb storage size to 50GB
4. Run tsdb-cli with --repair flag
5. Verify trace-query service is listening on port 4317

## Post-mortem note
The tracing collector exceeded its storage capacity and stopped processing new spans. This issue was missed initially due to the lack of monitoring and alerting on the tracing collector's storage capacity. In hindsight, additional monitoring and alerting could have been implemented to detect similar issues earlier and prevent them from escalating. The team has since implemented additional monitoring and alerting to prevent similar incidents in the future. The resolution steps were effective in resolving the incident, and the system has been stable since the implementation of these steps. The team will continue to monitor the system closely to ensure that similar issues do not occur in the future.
