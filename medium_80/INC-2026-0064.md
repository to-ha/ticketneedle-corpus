## Symptom
The incident presented as an MTU black hole between two sites, causing intermittent delays in outbound API calls. Users reported that these calls would occasionally take 5-10 seconds to complete because DNS resolution would stall. Notably, flushing the resolver cache would temporarily resolve the issue for a few minutes, indicating a potential problem with DNS resolution.

Further investigation revealed that the symptom was not consistent across all sites, suggesting a localized issue rather than a global DNS problem. The affected sites were those communicating through a specific WAN aggregation router, hinting at a possible configuration or connectivity issue related to this device.

Tools like MTR, tcpdump, and traceroute were utilized to diagnose the network path and identify any bottlenecks or misconfigurations that could be causing the MTU black hole and subsequent DNS resolution stalls.

## Diagnosis steps and hypotheses
Initial hypotheses included a possible MTU mismatch or a routing loop causing the black hole. However, upon further investigation using `dig +dnssec example.com`, it became clear that the issue was more closely related to DNS resolution and potentially DNSSEC validation. 

## Resolution steps
1. Disable DNSSEC validation for zone example.com
2. Set dnssec-validation no for zone example.com in /etc/named.conf
3. Restart named service with systemctl restart named
4. Verify zone example.com resolves without DNSSEC

## Post-mortem note
DNSSEC validation failed on delegated zone example.com due to expired keys. This failure led to a cascading effect, causing the observed MTU black hole and delays in DNS resolution. The swift identification and resolution of this issue underscore the importance of regular key rotation and validation checks for DNSSEC.
