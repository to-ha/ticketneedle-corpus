# Major Incident — INC-2026-0018
* **Ticket ID:** INC-2026-0018
* **Reported:** 2026-02-04 09:45 UTC
* **Priority:** P1
* **Domain:** Database
* **Status:** Resolved

## Symptom
The incident began with a notable increase in query latency, specifically a jump from 20ms to 4s in the p99 latency metric. This significant degradation in performance was first observed by monitoring tools and promptly reported by the on-call team. The elevated latency affected read operations primarily, suggesting an issue with the database's ability to efficiently handle incoming queries.

Further investigation revealed that this latency increase was correlated with a rise in pending writes on the read-replica pair. The read-replica, designed to offload read traffic from the primary database, was experiencing a backlog. This situation not only impacted the performance of read queries but also had the potential to affect the overall health and synchronization of the database cluster.

As the incident unfolded, the impact on users became more pronounced. Applications relying on the database for read operations began to time out or return errors, leading to a deterioration in user experience. The increase in latency and the subsequent application errors underscored the urgency of resolving the issue promptly to prevent further disruption.

The symptom complex centered around the query latency and the pending writes backlog pointed towards an issue related to the database's read-replica configuration or its ability to manage connections efficiently. Understanding the root cause of this issue required a deeper dive into the database's performance metrics and configuration settings.

## Diagnosis steps and hypotheses
Initial hypotheses included the possibility of a network issue affecting communication between the primary database and its read-replicas, a misconfiguration in the read-replica setup, or an overload of connections to the database. To explore these hypotheses, the team employed various diagnostic tools, including `pidstat` to monitor process statistics and `psql` to inspect database replication status.

One key command used in the diagnosis was:
```
pidstat -p $(pgrep postgres) 10 10
```
This command helped in understanding the process-level activity of the PostgreSQL server, providing insights into CPU usage, memory consumption, and other vital statistics that could indicate resource contention or other performance bottlenecks.

However, as the team delved deeper, they found that the network and basic replication setup seemed intact, with no obvious signs of misconfiguration. The focus then shifted towards understanding the connection load and how it might be impacting the read-replica's ability to keep up with incoming queries.

## Resolution steps
1. Restart read-replica on host db02 with --port 5433
2. Set max_connections to 100 in postgresql.conf on db01
3. Increase pidstat sampling interval to 10 seconds
4. Adjust pgbouncer max_client_conn to match new max_connections
5. Verify replication lag with psql -h db02 -p 5433 -c 'SELECT pg_last_xlog_receive_location()'

## Post-mortem note
Read-replica connection overload caused a backlog of pending writes. This incident highlighted the importance of closely monitoring database connection loads and adjusting configuration settings as necessary to prevent such overloads. Regular review of database performance metrics and proactive adjustments to settings like max_connections can help mitigate the risk of similar incidents in the future.
