## Diagnosis steps and hypotheses
The team considered several hypotheses to explain the object replication lag. One initial suspicion was that the issue might be related to a misconfigured storage device, which could be causing the replication delays. To investigate this hypothesis, the team used multipath -ll to examine the storage device configuration. However, the output did not reveal any obvious issues, ruling out this hypothesis.

Another hypothesis was that the cold-tier archive lifecycle policy might be causing the replication lag. The team suspected that the policy might be outdated or incorrectly configured, leading to delays in object replication. To diagnose this issue, the team used fio to test the storage performance and identify any potential bottlenecks. The command used was:
```
fio --filename=/dev/sdb --direct=1 --rw=randwrite --bs=4k
```
The results showed that the storage performance was within expected limits, but the team still needed to investigate the cold-tier archive lifecycle policy further.

The team also considered the possibility that the issue might be related to a faulty storage device. To diagnose this hypothesis, the team used smartctl to monitor the storage devices and check for any errors. However, the output did not reveal any obvious issues, ruling out this hypothesis.

As the team continued to investigate, they discovered that the cold-tier archive lifecycle policy had not been updated after a recent storage capacity expansion. This realization led to a new hypothesis: that the outdated policy might be causing the object replication lag. The team decided to investigate this hypothesis further and adjust the policy as needed.

After ruling out several hypotheses, the team finally identified the root cause of the issue: the cold-tier archive lifecycle policy was not updated after the storage capacity expansion. This outdated policy was causing the object replication lag, which had a significant impact on business operations.

## Resolution steps
1. Set archive.lifecycle.policy.cold_tier_retention_days to 90
2. Run fio --filename=/dev/sdb --direct=1 --rw=randwrite --bs=4k --size=10G
3. Verify multipath -ll shows path /dev/mapper/mpath0p1s1 as active
4. Adjust smartctl -a /dev/sda --smart=on to enable SMART monitoring

## Post-mortem note
