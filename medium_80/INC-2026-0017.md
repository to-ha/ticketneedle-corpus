# Major Incident — INC-2026-0017
* **Ticket ID:** INC-2026-0017
* **Reported:** 2026-04-26 12:22 UTC
* **Priority:** P1
* **Domain:** Network
* **Status:** Resolved

## Symptom
The network operations team observed BGP session flapping every few minutes on the edge load balancer. This flapping caused brief losses of connectivity for users accessing external services. The symptom was first noticed by monitoring tools, which alerted on repeated session establishments and terminations.

Further investigation revealed that the BGP session flapping was not limited to a single peer or prefix, suggesting a broader issue with the load balancer's ability to maintain stable connections. The operations team attempted to verify the issue using MTR, but the results were inconclusive due to the transient nature of the problem.

As the incident progressed, it became clear that the BGP session flapping was impacting user experience, with reports of intermittent connectivity issues and slow service response times. The team recognized the need for swift action to resolve the issue and prevent further disruptions.

## Escalation timeline
* 12:34 — L1 Network operations: Rahul began reviewing network logs to identify potential causes of the BGP session flapping.
* 13:02 — L2 Network engineer: Camille joined the investigation, focusing on configuration settings that might be contributing to the instability.
* 13:35 — L3 Network architect: Rafael was engaged to provide expertise on load balancer design and potential optimizations to mitigate the issue.

## Diagnosis steps and hypotheses
Initial hypotheses centered around misconfigurations or bugs in the BGP implementation. However, after reviewing configuration files and consulting documentation, these possibilities were ruled out. The team then considered the possibility of excessive traffic overwhelming the load balancer, leading to session flapping. To investigate this hypothesis, the team used `tcpdump` to capture traffic:
```
tcpdump -i eth0 port 80 -n -vv -s 0 -c 100
```
This command provided insight into the traffic patterns and helped identify the root cause of the issue.

## Resolution steps
1. Restart load balancer service on node 10.1.1.5
2. Set connection timeout to 30 seconds in /etc/loadbalancer.conf
3. Increase max connections from 1000 to 2000 in /etc/loadbalancer.conf

## Post-mortem note
The edge load balancer was overwhelmed by excessive incoming traffic. This incident highlights the importance of monitoring traffic patterns and adjusting load balancer settings accordingly to prevent similar issues in the future.
