## Symptom
The incident began with alerts firing and resolving within seconds in a flapping pattern, causing concern among the monitoring team. This pattern was first observed by the L1 SRE on-call, Bashir, who noticed that the alerts were related to the tracing collector service. As the issue persisted, it became clear that the problem was not just a simple glitch, but a more complex issue that required attention from the SRE team. The tracing collector service is a critical component of the monitoring system, responsible for collecting and processing trace data from various sources. When this service is not functioning correctly, it can lead to gaps in monitoring coverage and make it difficult to diagnose issues.

The flapping pattern of alerts was observed across multiple nodes, indicating that the issue was not isolated to a single host. The alerts were triggering due to the tracing collector service failing to start or crashing shortly after startup. This led to a loss of trace data, making it challenging for the SRE team to investigate the root cause of the issue. The monitoring team relied on tools like tsdb-cli, alert-history, and trace-query to try and understand the nature of the problem.

As the incident evolved, the SRE team noticed that the tracing collector service was experiencing issues with its database indexes. The service was unable to start due to corrupted indexes, which prevented it from collecting and processing trace data. This realization helped the team to focus their investigation on the database and indexing aspects of the tracing collector service.

The user and business impact of the incident was significant, as the lack of trace data made it difficult for developers and operations teams to diagnose and resolve issues. The monitoring team worked closely with the SRE team to try and restore the tracing collector service to its normal functioning state. The collaboration between the teams helped to accelerate the investigation and resolution of the incident.

The monitoring system caught the initial alerts and notified the L1 SRE on-call, Bashir, who immediately began investigating the issue. However, the monitoring system did not catch the underlying root cause of the problem, which was related to the corrupted database indexes. This highlighted the need for more detailed monitoring and alerting around the tracing collector service, particularly around its database and indexing components.

## Diagnosis steps and hypotheses
The SRE team considered several hypotheses during the investigation, including issues with the tracing collector service configuration, problems with the underlying database, and networking issues. One of the initial hypotheses was that the issue was related to the tracing collector service configuration, specifically the settings related to database connections. The team tried adjusting these settings, but it did not resolve the issue.

Another hypothesis was that the problem was related to the database itself, possibly due to corruption or performance issues. The team used tools like tsdb-cli to investigate the database and its indexes. They ran the following command to check the indexes:
```
tsdb-cli --check-indexes /var/lib/tsdb
```
This command helped the team to identify that the indexes were indeed corrupted, which was preventing the tracing collector service from starting.

The team also considered the possibility of networking issues, such as connectivity problems between the tracing collector service and the database. However, after investigating the network configuration and connectivity, they ruled out this hypothesis.

As the investigation continued, the team discovered that the corrupted indexes were the root cause of the issue. They realized that the tracing collector service was failing to start due to these corrupted indexes, which prevented it from collecting and processing trace data.

The team tried several diagnostic steps to resolve the issue, including restarting the tracing collector service, adjusting the database settings, and running repair commands on the database. However, it was not until they focused on repairing the corrupted indexes that they were able to resolve the issue.

## Resolution steps
1. Restart tracing collector service on node 10.0.2.15
2. Set tsdb.max_idle_connections to 50 in /etc/tsdb.conf
3. Increase trace-buffer-size to 1048576 in /etc/trace-config.yaml
4. Run tsdb-cli with --repair option to fix corrupted indexes

## Post-mortem note
