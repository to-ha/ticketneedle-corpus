# Major Incident — INC-2026-0042
* **Ticket ID:** INC-2026-0042
* **Reported:** 2026-01-09 01:41 UTC
* **Priority:** P1
* **Domain:** Database
* **Status:** Resolved

## Symptom
During the early hours of the incident, monitoring tools began to report a spike in deadlocks within the database. This increase in deadlocks coincided with a scheduled release window, suggesting that the deployment might have triggered or exacerbated the issue. Further investigation into the database logs revealed that these deadlocks were predominantly related to the WAL (Write-Ahead Logging) archive process. The spike in deadlocks started approximately 10 minutes after the release window opened and continued to rise until the issue was addressed.

As the deadlocks increased, database performance began to degrade noticeably. Queries that normally executed quickly began to time out, and users reported delays in data updates. The monitoring dashboard showed a sharp increase in the number of blocked queries, further indicating that the deadlock situation was worsening over time. Given the critical nature of the database for ongoing operations, this symptom complex posed a significant risk to service availability and data integrity.

The observable pattern of deadlocks spiking during the release window, coupled with the performance degradation and increase in blocked queries, suggested that the issue was closely related to how the database handled the increased load or changes introduced during the deployment. It was crucial to quickly identify the root cause to prevent further disruption and potential data loss.

Initial observations pointed towards a potential misconfiguration or overload of the WAL archive process, given its role in managing database consistency and recovery. However, without deeper investigation, it was unclear if this was indeed the root cause or merely a symptom of a larger issue.

## Diagnosis steps and hypotheses
Initial hypotheses included the possibility of inadequate database resources (such as insufficient connections or improper configuration of the WAL archiver), issues with the recent deployment (like incorrect schema changes or poorly optimized queries), or even network connectivity problems affecting the database cluster. 

To investigate these hypotheses, several diagnostic steps were taken. One key command used was:
```
psql -c 'SHOW max_wal_senders'
```
This command helped in understanding the current configuration of the database regarding WAL sender processes, which could be crucial in identifying if the setup was adequate for the current load.

However, as each hypothesis was explored and evidence collected, it became clear that the root cause was more specific to the WAL archive process than initially thought. The increase in deadlocks during the release window suggested that the database was struggling to keep up with the changes being applied, possibly due to an insufficient number of WAL senders or an issue with the archiving process itself.

## Resolution steps
1. Restart WAL archiver with -v flag
2. Set archive_timeout to 60 seconds
3. Increase max_wal_senders to 10
4. Clear stale archives from /var/lib/pgsql/archive
5. Verify archive_mode is on in postgresql.conf

## Post-mortem note
The WAL archive process failed due to insufficient max_wal_senders. This incident highlights the importance of closely monitoring database configuration settings, especially during and after significant changes or deployments, to ensure that the database can handle increased loads without performance degradation. Reviewing and adjusting these settings proactively can prevent similar incidents in the future.
