## Symptom

The incident began with reports of a trace sampler rate misconfiguration, resulting in missing critical spans. This issue was first observed by the monitoring team at 21:16 UTC on February 12, 2026. Initially, the impact seemed minimal, affecting only a small subset of users. However, as the minutes passed, the scope of the problem expanded, and more users began to experience issues with their tracing data. The monitoring dashboard, which is designed to catch such discrepancies, alerted the on-call team to the anomaly. Upon further investigation, it became clear that the log aggregation forwarder was not operating correctly, leading to a significant gap in the trace data.

As the symptom evolved, the team noticed that the missing spans were not random but rather followed a specific pattern related to the volume of logs being processed. The log aggregation forwarder, responsible for collecting and forwarding logs to the tracing system, seemed overwhelmed. This realization prompted a deeper dive into the forwarder's performance metrics, where an unusually high log volume was observed. The team speculated that this high volume might be the root cause of the issue, but they needed more evidence to confirm their hypothesis.

The user impact was significant, as the missing trace data hindered the ability to diagnose and resolve issues efficiently. Developers rely heavily on tracing data to understand the flow of requests through the system and identify bottlenecks or errors. Without this data, troubleshooting became a challenging and time-consuming process. The business impact was also notable, as prolonged resolution times for issues can lead to decreased customer satisfaction and potential revenue loss.

The monitoring system played a crucial role in detecting the anomaly early on. Alerts were triggered based on predefined thresholds for trace data completeness, ensuring that the team was notified promptly. However, the initial alerts did not pinpoint the exact cause of the problem, necessitating further investigation. The team used tools like `tsdb-cli` and `trace-query` to analyze the tracing data and metrics, aiming to understand the nature of the discrepancy.

In the hours following the initial report, the symptom persisted, with intermittent periods of improved trace data availability. These fluctuations suggested that the issue might be related to the load on the log aggregation forwarder or its configuration. The team prepared to escalate the incident, recognizing the need for additional expertise to resolve the complex problem.

## Diagnosis steps and hypotheses

The diagnosis process involved considering several hypotheses based on the observed symptoms and the team's understanding of the system. Initially, the team suspected that the issue might be related to a misconfigured trace sampler rate, as this would directly affect the availability of trace data. However, after reviewing the configuration files and comparing them against known good configurations, this hypothesis was ruled out.

Another hypothesis considered was that the tracing system itself was experiencing issues, possibly due to a software bug or a compatibility problem with recent updates. The team ran diagnostic tests on the tracing system, including queries to verify data integrity and consistency, but found no evidence to support this hypothesis.

The team also explored the possibility of network connectivity issues between the log aggregation forwarder and the tracing system. This could potentially cause data loss or corruption during transmission. However, network diagnostics, including `ping` and `traceroute`, did not reveal any connectivity problems.

To better understand the load on the log aggregation forwarder, the team executed the following command to query the rate of log volume:
```
tsdb-cli --query 'rate(log_volume[1m])'
```
This query provided insight into the recent log volume trends, helping the team to assess whether the forwarder was indeed overwhelmed.

Further investigation led to the hypothesis that the log aggregation forwarder's buffer capacity might be insufficient for the current log volume, causing it to drop or misroute trace data. This hypothesis seemed plausible given the observed symptoms and the high log volume indicated by the metrics. The team decided to investigate the forwarder's configuration and performance metrics more closely.

After ruling out several hypotheses and gathering more evidence, the team finally uncovered the root cause of the incident. The log aggregation forwarder was indeed struggling to keep up with the high volume of logs, but the critical issue was its buffer capacity and the way it handled retries when forwarding logs to the tracing system.

## Resolution steps

1. Restart log-forwarder service with --max-retries 5
2. Set /etc/log-forwarder.conf buffer_size to 1048576
3. Increase tsdb storage size to 50G using tsdb-cli
4. Adjust trace-query timeout to 30000ms
5. Run promtool-equivalent with --log-level debug

## Post-mortem note

The log aggregation forwarder exceeded its buffer capacity due to high log volume. This root cause was initially missed because the team focused on the symptoms related to trace data availability rather than the underlying log processing issue. In retrospect, a closer examination of the log aggregation forwarder's performance and configuration earlier in the investigation might have led to a quicker resolution. Follow-up actions include reviewing the monitoring setup to ensure that similar issues are caught earlier and implementing adjustments to the log aggregation forwarder's buffer size and retry mechanism to prevent such incidents in the future. Additionally, the team plans to conduct a thorough review of the tracing system's configuration and performance metrics to identify any potential bottlenecks or areas for improvement.
