## Symptom
The network team observed a significant increase in packet loss between two sites, indicating an MTU black hole. This issue was identified through monitoring tools that track network performance and latency. Further investigation revealed that the packet loss was specifically occurring when traffic was routed through the anycast VIP.

## Escalation timeline
- 21:50 — L1 Network operations: Yuki immediately began reviewing network logs to identify the source of the issue.
- 22:00 — L2 Network engineer: Mei took over the investigation, focusing on the anycast VIP configuration and its potential impact on traffic routing.
- 22:39 — L3 Network architect: Anya was consulted to provide expertise on the network architecture and potential design flaws that could be contributing to the packet loss.
- 23:37 — Service owner: Amelia worked closely with the network team to understand the business impact of the issue and to prioritize a swift resolution.

## Diagnosis steps and hypotheses
Initial hypotheses suggested a possible misconfiguration in the routing tables or an issue with the anycast VIP setup. To investigate further, the team used various tools such as `dig` and `tcpdump`. A key command used in the diagnosis was:
```
tcpdump -i any port 53 -w capture.pcap
```
This helped in capturing DNS traffic and analyzing potential issues with DNS resolution that could be affecting the anycast VIP routing.

## Resolution steps
1. Set ttl to 300 on anycast VIP
2. Run dig +trace against VIP
3. Verify routing table with tcpdump -i any port 53
4. Adjust BGP local pref to 100 on affected route

## Post-mortem note
The anycast VIP routing was misconfigured, causing packet loss. This incident highlighted the importance of regular audits of network configurations to prevent similar issues in the future.
