## Symptom
The incident began with reports of secret rotation breaking one downstream consumer, which relied on the feature-flag service for configuration updates. This symptom was first observed by monitoring tools at 23:25 UTC, triggering an alert that was escalated to the on-call team. As the issue persisted, it became clear that the problem was not isolated to a single consumer but was instead a widespread issue affecting all services that depended on the feature-flag service for dynamic configuration. The impact was significant, with several critical services experiencing failures due to expired or invalid secrets.

Further investigation revealed that the feature-flag service was failing to properly rotate secrets, leading to a cascade of errors in dependent services. This failure was observed across multiple environments, suggesting a systemic issue rather than an isolated problem. The monitoring dashboard showed a spike in error rates for services relying on the feature-flag service, with logs indicating repeated attempts to fetch invalid or expired secrets.

The symptom complex was characterized by repeated errors in secret rotation, leading to service failures and disruptions to downstream consumers. This pattern suggested a problem with the feature-flag service's ability to manage and rotate secrets effectively. Given the critical nature of secret rotation for service security and integrity, resolving this issue became a high priority.

As the incident unfolded, the focus remained on understanding and addressing the root cause of the secret rotation failures, with an emphasis on restoring the feature-flag service's functionality to prevent further disruptions. The observed pattern of errors and service failures provided a clear direction for the diagnostic efforts, guiding the team towards investigating the feature-flag service's internal mechanisms and dependencies.

## Diagnosis steps and hypotheses
Initial hypotheses centered around issues with secret storage, network connectivity, or configuration mismatches. However, as diagnostics progressed, it became clear that the problem was more closely related to the feature-flag service's internal state and its ability to garbage collect expired flags. One of the key diagnostic steps involved running the command:
```
registry-gc --debug
```
This helped in understanding the garbage collection process and identifying potential bottlenecks or failures in flag expiration handling. Despite this, initial hypotheses about storage or connectivity issues were gradually ruled out as evidence pointed towards a problem with flag management within the service.

Further investigation considered the possibility of a misconfiguration or a bug in the feature-flag service's flag rotation logic. However, detailed analysis of the service's code and configuration did not reveal any obvious issues, suggesting that the problem might be related to the service's runtime state or its interactions with other components.

## Resolution steps
1. Restart feature-flag service on node 3
2. Set flag_history_retention to 30 days
3. Run registry-gc with --dry-run flag
4. Update broker-admin config at /etc/broker/admin.conf
5. Flush stale cache entries older than 1 hour

## Post-mortem note
The feature-flag service failed to garbage collect expired flags. This root cause explanation aligns with the observed symptoms and the eventual resolution steps taken. The failure to properly manage flag expiration led to a cascade of errors, affecting secret rotation and, by extension, downstream services. Understanding this root cause will inform future improvements to the feature-flag service, aiming to prevent similar incidents through enhanced flag management and garbage collection mechanisms.
